Traditional Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) predominantly rely on signature-based and supervised
learning approaches that require large volumes of labeled attack data. However, generating such labeled data
is costly, time-consuming, and often impractical, especially in the presence of class imbalance and zero-day attacks. To address these limitations, this paper proposes MVNIDS, a Multiview-based Network Intrusion Detection
System employing a self-supervised learning paradigm. The proposed method constructs three complementary
views from raw packet capture data, namely, Network View, Flow View, and Image View, each capturing distinct
protocol, temporal, and structural characteristics of network traﬃc. Independent autoencoder models are trained
on benign samples for each view, and their reconstruction errors are fused through a majority-voting mechanism
to automatically generate “Attack” and “Benign” pseudo-labels. These labels are subsequently used to train a
binary classiﬁer for ﬁnal intrusion detection. Experimental evaluation on the CICIDS2018 dataset, focusing on
FTP BruteForce and UDP DoS attacks, demonstrates that MVNIDS outperforms most view-speciﬁc and supervised
baselines, achieving up to 98.3 % F1-score and 98.5 % accuracy. The multiview representation enhances detection robustness and enables eﬀective identiﬁcation of zero-day and variant attacks, highlighting MVNIDS as a
scalable, computationally eﬃcient, and generalizable framework for modern network security applications.

1. Introduction
Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) play a critical role in
safeguarding modern communication infrastructures against a growing
range of cyber threats. These threats are easily generated by attackers using open-source utilities like LOIC [1], Slowloris [2], Nmap [3], etc. and
can hamper overall communication between entities and aﬀect system
performance. However, most existing NIDS frameworks remain heavily
dependent on signature-based and supervised learning techniques for
attack detection. Signature-based systems rely on manually engineered
rules or known attack patterns, while supervised learning models depend on large volumes of labeled data for training. In both cases, the
availability and quality of labeled data directly determine system performance.
The reliance on labeled datasets presents several challenges. First,
curating labeled attack data is costly and time-consuming, as it requires
extensive domain expertise and continuous updates to accommodate
new or evolving attack variants. Second, even in widely used benchmark
datasets, the number of attack samples is signiﬁcantly smaller than that
of benign samples, resulting in high class imbalance. This imbalance
leads to biased models that tend to favor normal traﬃc, often missing
subtle or rare malicious patterns. Moreover, in real-world network environments, new or previously unseen attacks, commonly referred to as

zero-day attacks are frequent and cannot be detected reliably by systems
trained on static labeled data.
Moreover, most Internet traﬃc today is encrypted and thus the majority of the IDS solutions have issues with Deep Packet Inspection. In
order to eﬀectively develop an IDS solution for modern encrypted network traﬃc, only payload information is not suﬃcient for detecting attacks. It is also required to utilize information from other sources like
header and ﬂow statistics [4] for accurate detection. Several IDS solutions like Guo et. al. [5], Bhale et. al. [6], Koc et. al. [7], etc. utilizing
the ﬂow level information have been developed earlier to resolve this
problem.
Earlier approaches like rule-based IDS solutions (i.e. Snort [8], and
Bro [9]) consider header ﬁelds and payload distribution details only for
attack detection, due to which payload-related attacks are not captured.
On the other hand, solutions like Perdisci et. al. [10], Ariu et. al. [11],
and Thorat et. al. [12] consider only the payload information and do not
utilize header or ﬂow level information due to which the ﬂow sequence
information is missing and such solutions are unable to detect certain
attacks. In other solutions, the packet header and payload are utilized
together but they are unable to detect low rate attacks.
To address these limitations, this paper proposes MVNIDS, a
Multiview-based Network Intrusion Detection System that employs a
self-supervised learning paradigm. The proposed method eliminates the

3. Proposed methodology
The proposed method considers the raw packets from network communication. Various packet sniﬃng tools like Wireshark, tcpdump, [44]
etc. can be used to capture these raw packets from the port in dedicated
machine or network switch that is running in promiscuous mode. These
raw packets contain communication information among diﬀerent hosts.
For the experiment purpose, we used the raw packets already extracted
for speciﬁc attacks, but in the real network, a sniﬃng module is required
to capture the packets consecutively with a pre-deﬁned time window
length. As shown in the ﬂow diagram (Fig. 1), the extracted network
packets are ﬁrst converted to multiple diﬀerent views using the subset
of packets captured at ﬁxed time window length and later the viewspeciﬁc autoencoder models are trained and view-speciﬁc reconstruction error thresholds are estimated by training the autoencoder models
with reconstruction error as a loss function. These trained encoders (part
of autoencoder) are further utilized for generating representation from
the extracted views and used for producing labeled data with the majority voting on view-speciﬁc encoder classiﬁcation results. This label
generation process also handle the scenario for the slight change on the
threshold values to build the more robust model that can handle the
minimal change in learned threshold as the value of threshold may vary
over time due to change in network characteristics. In the ﬁnal stage
of processing, the prepared labeled data is utilized to build the binary
classiﬁcation model that is used to predict the outcome as Benign or
Attack.

long-term patterns. Too small windows might capture too much noise,

6. Conclusion
We proposed MVNIDS, a Multiview-based Network Intrusion Detection System that leverages a self-supervised learning approach to overcome the limitations of traditional signature-based and supervised IDS
methods. By independently training autoencoders on Network, Flow,
and Image views derived from raw packet data and combining their outputs through a majority-voting mechanism, MVNIDS eﬀectively generates its own labels and learns robust behavioral representations without
manual annotation. Experimental evaluation on the CICIDS2018 dataset
for FTP BruteForce and UDP DoS attacks demonstrates that the proposed
framework achieves higher accuracy, precision, and F1-scores than most
view-speciﬁc or supervised baselines, while maintaining reduced computational complexity to be used as an online IDS. The system’s multiview design enhances both accuracy and adaptability, enabling detection of zero-day and evolving attacks through protocol-agnostic and
temporally aware representations
