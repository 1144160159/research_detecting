Hybrid Approach for Machine Anomaly Detection
Across Diverse Operating Conditions: Combining
Self-Supervised Learning With Vibration Statistics
Seongjae Lee

and Taehyoun Kim , Member, IEEE

Abstract—Failure detection in rotating machines is critical for
reducing maintenance costs, economic losses, and safety-related
accidents. Deep learning has become a robust and powerful
solution for machine fault diagnosis, but it faces two major
challenges: scarcity of labeled faulty data and variability of
machine operating conditions. To address these challenges, we
propose a novel hybrid machine anomaly detection methodology
that integrates an innovative pretext task-based self-supervised
learning framework with vibration statistics. First, we designed
a pretext task to classify the rotating speeds and trained an
auxiliary classifier on it. This auxiliary classifier effectively
extracts fault-discriminative features related to rotating speed,
an operating condition closely associated with various machine
faults. Moreover, the proposed pretext task applies to the fault
diagnosis of general rotating machines because the rotating speed
can be easily obtained using a Hall sensor. Second, a hybrid
feature was generated by combining the auxiliary classifier
outputs with six statistical features derived from raw vibration
signals. These statistical features augment the fault-discriminative
ability of the auxiliary classifier outputs by incorporating the
magnitude and asymmetry of the signal, which the auxiliary classifier alone cannot capture. The evaluation results demonstrated
that our approach significantly outperformed 11 state-of-the-art
anomaly detection methods on three different vibration datasets
encompassing multiple operating conditions and a real-world
run-to-failure scenario. In addition, our method is capable of
performing real-time inference on an edge device platform,
demonstrating its applicability to practical industrial setups.
Furthermore, feature visualization analysis highlighted how the
proposed hybrid feature enables a more distinct separation
between faulty and healthy data samples than that achieved with
statistical features or auxiliary classifier outputs alone.
Index Terms—Anomaly detection, diverse operating conditions, feature integration, machine fault diagnosis, self-supervised
learning, vibration statistics.

II. R ELATED W ORK
A. Machine Learning-Based Methods
Machine learning-based methods have been widely used
for anomaly detection even before the advent of deep learning. Recent studies have focused on improving the feature
extraction process. de Novaes Pires Leite et al. [22] proposed
a method for diagnosing wind turbine faults by extracting
features from the time, frequency, and mechanical domains of
vibration signals. Chen et al. [23] investigated a Transformer
fault diagnosis method based on impulse frequency response
analysis of current signals using a pulse generator. Hu et al.
[24] conducted order analysis on the frequency domain of
vibration signals to extract vibration vectors for diagnosing
faults in rotating machinery.
Machine learning algorithms used in [22], [23], and [24]
achieved strong performance in handling manually extracted
tabular features. However, they relied on domain-specific
analysis for feature extraction. To overcome this limitation,
this study employed a machine learning model for anomaly
detection but used automatically extracted features without
depending on domain-specific decision-making or analysis.
B. Reconstruction Methods
The reconstruction method identifies machine faults by
using the reconstruction error of encoder–decoder architectures, such as AEs, as the anomaly score. An AE model
consists of an encoder that compresses the input data and a
decoder that reconstructs the compressed data to its original
form. The model is trained to minimize reconstruction errors,
such as the mean squared error, solely with healthy data.
Hence, the model accurately reconstructs healthy data but
struggles to reconstruct faulty data that are not included in the

training data. Consequently, the reconstruction errors of the
faulty data usually exceed those of the healthy data, resulting
in data with reconstruction errors exceeding a predefined
threshold being classified as faulty.
Many studies have utilized the reconstruction method for
machine anomaly detection. Ahmed et al. [25] proposed a
gearbox fault diagnosis method using a denoising AE (DAE),
and Choi et al. [26] developed a lightweight bearing fault
diagnosis model by integrating principal component analysis
with AE to reduce the computational demands. Research using
AE architectures incorporating CNN or RNN layers has also
been conducted. Kim and Ro [27] diagnosed dry vacuum pump
faults using features extracted by discrete wavelet transform as
the inputs for long short-term memory-based AE. Zhao et al.
[28] proposed CAE-CWT, a model that detects bearing faults
using a 2-D convolutional AE (CAE) with 2-D spectrograms
generated using continuous wavelet transform (CWT). Some
studies have employed the emerging Transformer architecture
as the encoder–decoder model. For example, Xu et al. [29]
proposed an anomaly Transformer (ATF) architecture, which
is trained to minimize both the reconstruction error and
association discrepancy.
Recently, several studies have incorporated reconstruction
methods into adversarial training strategies or combined them
with machine learning techniques. In [30], [31], and [32],
researchers used generative adversarial networks (GANs) to
diagnose faults in bearings, structures, and industrial products,
respectively. Hojjati and Armanfard [33] introduced deep
autoencoding support vector data descriptor (DASVDD), an
anomaly detection method that combines the reconstruction
error of AE with the distance metric of SVDD. Yoon and
Yu [34] and Du et al. [35] developed methods for machine
anomaly detection by combining the AE model with SVDD
and isolation forest (iForest), respectively.
Unlike machine learning-based methods, reconstruction
methods are well-suited for raw input data, such as vibration
and sound signals from industrial environments. However, in
reconstruction methods, the model is trained to reconstruct
the input data accurately after compression, rather than discriminate faulty data from healthy data. Consequently, its
performance can degrade when the distribution of the healthy
data used for training is complex [12]. Since this study aimed
to address the challenges in fault diagnosis under various
operating conditions, reconstruction methods were not utilized.
Instead, a self-supervised learning approach was adopted to
train a deep learning model as a feature extractor. During
the performance evaluation, the proposed approach was compared with representative reconstruction methods across three
datasets representing diverse operating conditions.
C. Self-Supervised Learning-Based Methods
Self-supervised learning can be categorized into contrastive
learning and auxiliary pretext task learning. Contrastive learning trains a model by using the similarity between data pairs.
Specifically, the model is trained to embed similar data pairs,
referred to as positive pairs, closer together and dissimilar data
pairs, referred to as negative pairs, farther apart in the latent
embedding space. Several studies have applied contrastive

3540215

learning to machine anomaly detection. Yang et al. [36],
Cui et al. [37], and Wang et al. [38] proposed machine
anomaly detection methods based on contrastive learning.
Yang et al. [36] trained a model using negative sample pairs
generated by masking specific regions in the frequency domain
of vibration signals. Cui et al. [37] augmented vibration
signal data to generate positive samples and trained the
model to minimize their distance from the original samples.
Wang et al. [38] proposed a self-supervised diagnosis method
for faults in industrial processes by utilizing the internal contrastive learning (ICL) strategy [39]. This approach generates
positive and negative pairs using only a subset of the training
samples, eliminating the need for external data augmentation.
Although contrastive learning has shown effectiveness in
machine anomaly detection, it does not fully capture the
fault-discriminative characteristics associated with machine
operating conditions. Consequently, its performance may
degrade under diverse operating conditions. For example, the
method in [37] can handle datasets with various motor loads
and rotating speeds, but it requires additional labeled faulty
data to improve the model performance.
In contrast, auxiliary pretext task learning trains a model
through a pretext task based on pseudo-labels generated from
metadata. For example, in [40] and [41], models were trained
for anomaly detection through a pretext task to classify the
machine ID, which is provided as metadata in the DCASE
2020 anomalous sound detection dataset. Since machine ID
reflects the operating conditions of machines in the DCASE
2020 dataset, this approach allows the model to effectively
handle diverse operating conditions. However, this metadata
is specific to the dataset and is not universally applicable to
rotating machinery fault diagnosis. To address these limitations
in existing pretext task learning-based approaches, this paper
presents a new pretext task that can be widely applied to
vibration data from rotating machinery operating under various
conditions. In summary, the advantage of our approach lies
in overcoming the limitations of prior anomaly detection
methods based on machine learning and auxiliary pretext task
learning.
III. M ETHODOLOGY
This section describes the proposed method. As illustrated
in Fig. 1, the proposed method consists of three components: an auxiliary classifier, a statistical feature extractor,
and an anomaly detector. Initially, the auxiliary classifier is
trained through a pretext task designed to classify rotating
speeds based on vibration signals measured from a healthy
machine. During the testing phase, the outputs of the classifier
serve as feature vectors for anomaly detection. Next, the
statistical feature extractor calculates the statistical features,
including the mean (µ), peak (pk), rms, crest factor (CF),
skewness (skew), and kurtosis (kurt), from the vibration
signals. These statistical features are then combined with
the auxiliary classifier outputs to form a hybrid feature.
Finally, the anomaly detector identifies machine faults based
on the hybrid feature using the local outlier factor (LOF)
algorithm.

VI. C ONCLUSION AND F UTURE W ORK
This study proposes a novel machine anomaly detection
method to overcome two key challenges in deploying deep
learning-based fault diagnosis for rotating machinery in realworld industrial settings: the scarcity of labeled faulty data and
the variability of machine operating conditions. To overcome
these challenges, we first designed a pretext task to classify
rotating speeds measured by Hall sensors and trained the auxiliary classifier through this task. Then, based on the auxiliary
classifier outputs, we proposed a hybrid feature integration
approach that combines them with vibration statistics.
To evaluate our method under diverse machine operating
conditions and fault types, experiments were conducted on
the following three datasets: UOS, HUST, and XJTU-SY. The
proposed method consistently outperformed 11 comparison
models across all datasets and enabled real-time anomaly
detection on a Raspberry Pi 4B. Moreover, extensive ablation
studies and feature visualizations confirmed the effectiveness
of the hybrid feature. The analysis revealed that the hybrid
feature effectively compensates for the limitations of individual
statistical features or auxiliary classifier outputs, enabling a
clear separation between healthy and faulty data.
In future work, we aim to develop a more robust anomaly
detection algorithm capable of handling extended machine
operating conditions, limited data availability, and constrained
hardware resources for efficient unsupervised on-device fault
diagnosis.
