 OPEN Federated learning-powered realtime behavioral intrusion detection
 leveraging LSTM, attention, GANs,
 and large language models
 Abdullah AlHayan & Jalal Al-Muhtadi
 A novel behavior-based intrusion detection framework, the Federated Intrusion Detection and
 Mitigation Framework (FIDMF), is proposed to address critical challenges in modern intrusion
 detection, including data privacy, scalability, severe class imbalance, and the critical need for semantic
 understanding and explainability. FIDMF integrates federated learning for privacy-preserving
 collaborative training with an Attention-Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) core for robust temporal
 pattern detection. To substantially enhance the framework’s intelligence and transparency, FIDMF
 innovatively pioneers a multi-faceted integration of open-source Large Language Models (LLMs)
 for: 1) Contextual Feature Enrichment from raw network logs; 2) Semantic Data Augmentation,
 guided by LLMs to inform Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for generating semantically
 coherent and novel attack patterns; and 3) Explainable AI (XAI) for human-readable explanations.
 This holistic approach, further supported by the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE)
 for class balancing, results in a robust, scalable, privacy-preserving, and highly explainable Intrusion
 Detection System (IDS). A rigorous evaluation across the NSL-KDD, CIC-IDS2017, and UNSW-NB15
 datasets demonstrates FIDMF’s superior performance. On NSL-KDD, the full FIDMF framework
 achieved an outstanding overall accuracy of 99.40% and an F1-score of 99.38%. Critically, for the
 challenging minority attack class, it attained an F1-score of 99.70%, significantly outperforming prior
 configurations without LLM guidance. Furthermore, FIDMF maintained high accuracy on CIC-IDS2017
 (99.65%) and UNSW-NB15 (98.05%), confirming its strong generalizability. An in-depth ablation study
 validates the crucial contribution of each component, particularly the semantic intelligence provided
 by the LLMs. FIDMF proves its potential as a superior, intelligent, and transparent solution for realworld intrusion detection scenarios.

 performance as measured by high false-negative rates, where the system fails to detect the attacks4. Although
 these models are highly efficient in recognizing complex patterns, there are instances wherein DL models could

 Problem statement and research questions
 Conventional intrusion detection systems rely a lot on centralized data processing, which is a dependency
 that raises privacy concerns and, at the same time, limits the scalability and performance of the systems. The
 present-day IDS are forced to meet privacy measures along with keeping up the accurate detection as one of the
 main challenges posed by the increasing network traffic. The problem of class imbalance further complicates
 the existing challenges making it hard for traditional models to predict rare attack patterns correctly. Current
 deep learning-based IDSs have, amongst others, the critical unmet need of being unable to understand the
 rich semantic context of network events entirely, being unable to create truly novel attack variations based on
 conceptual attack descriptions, and being unable to provide transparent, human-understandable explanations
 for the classifications of anomalies. The semantic and explainability limitations are the main barriers against
 the creation of really intelligent, adaptive, and trustworthy IDS’s. These barriers are particularly going to be
 felt in the case of solutions for zero-day threats and in the human-incident response that is supposed to be
 and is supposed to be rapid. The present study is attempting to resolve these issues through the combination
 of federated learning with deep learning methods such as LSTMs and attention mechanisms for developing a
 behavior-based intrusion detection system while also applying SMOTE to cope with the class imbalance as well
 as, most importantly, by using the Large Language Models (LLMs) that are open-source to connect the semantic
 comprehension, the advanced data augmentation and the enhancing of the system interpretability.
 This leads to our primary research questions:

 • RQ1: How can a robust and accurate IDS be trained on distributed data sources while preserving the privacy
 of the raw data?
 • RQ2: How can an IDS effectively model the temporal sequences of network traffic to detect complex, multi-step attacks, enrich feature representations with deeper semantic context from raw logs using open-source
 LLMs, and how can its focus on critical features be improved?
 • RQ3: What is an effective strategy to mitigate the negative impact of severe class imbalance on model performance, and how can open-source Large Language Models (LLMs) contribute to generating semantically
 diverse and truly novel attack patterns for robust training beyond numerical interpolation?
 • RQ4: What is the performance of a privacy-preserving federated IDS that works in a similar way as centralized ones in terms of generalization across various benchmark datasets, and does the application of LLMs
 even further make it more capable of dealing with new and zero-day attacks in different network scenarios?
 • RQ5: What is the best way to utilize open-source Large Language Models (LLMs) to produce explanations for
 the identified intrusions that are both comprehensible and context-related, thus bringing the FIDMF framework for security analysts to the level of high interpretability and actionable insights?

 Challenges in real-time detection
 There are many important challenges in real-time intrusion detection systems, especially when advanced
 machine learning techniques are applied.

 1. Computational Complexity: Attention-based LSTMs and LLMs introduce a heavy layer of computational
 overhead, making real-time implementation extremely difficult, particularly on resource-constrained edge
 devices.
 2. Data Privacy and Scalability in FL: FL, despite its privacy advantages, creates difficulties for real-time operation due to communication overhead for model aggregation, which can turn out to be a bottleneck in large
 distributed systems. This includes real-world deployment challenges like device heterogeneity and synchronization cycles.
 3. Latency and Processing Requirements: The quick decision-making is a must in the case of real-time systems.
 High-latency models usually do not manage to keep up with the strict time requirements for real-time detection.

 Addressing real-time detection
 We address the complexities associated with real-time detection by employing a“near real-time”strategy, focusing
 on model efficiency and practical deployment, while acknowledging the inherent latency trade-off of federated
 model aggregation.

 1. Efficient Model Architecture and Asynchronous LLM Processing : We utilize an Attention-LSTM core, optimized via Optuna, that preserves accuracy while minimizing inference time. Critically, we use asynchronous
 processing for the computationally intensive LLM steps:

 Contributions
 The key contributions of this work are multi-fold, establishing a novel approach for intelligent intrusion
 detection. First, we introduce a novel, end-to-end federated learning framework (FIDMF) for behavior-based
 intrusion detection that guarantees data privacy by design, directly addressing RQ1. Second, we pioneer the
 robust integration of an Attention-LSTM model significantly enhanced by LLM-powered contextual feature
 enrichment (DistilBERT), enabling a deeper semantic understanding of network events (RQ2). Third,
 we propose a sophisticated class imbalance mitigation strategy that combines SMOTE with LLM-guided
 Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to generate truly novel and semantically plausible attack patterns,
 thereby enhancing robustness against zero-day threats (RQ3). Fourth, we provide comprehensive empirical
 validation demonstrating 99.40% accuracy on NSL-KDD and strong generalizability across CIC-IDS2017 and
 UNSW-NB15, confirming superior robustness compared to centralized and SOTA federated baselines (RQ4).
 Finally, the multi-faceted integration of open-source LLMs provides Explainable AI (XAI) (GPT-2) through
 human-readable, contextual explanations, significantly improving the transparency and actionable insights for
 security analysts (RQ5). These contributions are devoted to the development of state-of-the-art IDS technologies
 in dealing with privacy, scalability, and class imbalance, following a practical approach toward near real-time
 intrusion detection. This work represents a great stride toward the development of more adaptive, efficient, and
 secure IDSs through the integration of federated learning and deep learning models.

 Background and literature review
 The rapid development of network technologies and their extensive use in web services have made organizations
 increasingly vulnerable to sophisticated cyber-attacks. IDSs are important tools to defend these systems from
 malicious activities. Among a large number of approaches to intrusion detection, deep learning methods,
 especially Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, attract much attention because of their ability to learn
 temporal dependencies and deal with irregular patterns efficiently. Althubiti et al. proposed an LSTM networkbased IDS, trained on the CSIC 2010 HTTP dataset, and achieved an incredible accuracy of 99.97% by using
 the Adam optimizer. Their work indicates the great potential of LSTM in the detection of known and unknown
 web application attacks12. Gao et al. also proposed LSTM-based architectures for SCADA systems, which proved
 effective for detecting temporally correlated and uncorrelated attacks. The many-to-one architecture they
 used showed improved effectiveness in handling complex temporal patterns9. To overcome the drawbacks of
 current Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDSs), Han and Pak recently proposed a hierarchical LSTMbased model that can use all packet information instead of part of packet data. By doing so, the detection rate
 was highly improved to 95.16% and 99.70% on two datasets, respectively, outperforming traditional NIDSs13.
 Moreover, Yang et al. integrated an attention mechanism with LSTM to improve the detection of complicated
 network attacks. Their method demonstrated better accuracy, precision, and fewer false alarms than the
 conventional approaches, including CNN and RNN. The work further underlines the critical attribute-based
 focus in intrusion detection10. Taken together, these studies show the changing face of IDS research and stress
 the benefits of advanced neural architectures, such as LSTM and hybrid models. These techniques provide very
 strong solutions to contemporary cybersecurity challenges and a strong base for further innovations in intrusion
 detection technology.
 Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have revolutionized natural language processing with their
 unprecedented ability to understand, generate, and reason with text. Their application in cybersecurity is an
 emerging and highly promising field. Early research has explored LLMs for tasks like threat intelligence analysis,
 vulnerability description generation, and even as tools for red teaming . In the context of Intrusion Detection
 Systems (IDSs), LLMs offer unique advantages: they can extract rich semantic meaning from unstructured or
 semi-structured network logs and alerts, provide context to seemingly isolated events, and translate complex
 technical findings into human-understandable explanations. While some studies have focused on LLMs for static
 log analysis, threat report generation, or isolated tasks, their comprehensive and active integration directly into
 the core detection pipeline for semantic feature enrichment, guided data augmentation for zero-day threats, and
 real-time explainable AI within a privacy-preserving federated learning framework remains largely unexplored
 and constitutes a key novelty of our work. This paper pioneers such an integration, leveraging efficient opensource LLMs like DistilBERT and GPT-2 to actively address the semantic intelligence and transparency gaps in
 modern IDSs, moving beyond passive analysis to proactive, semantically informed detection and explanation.
 Boukhalfa et al. proposed a Network Intrusion Detection System (NIDS) based on Long Short-Term
 Memory (LSTM) architecture, aimed at detecting intrusions while maintaining long-term memory to identify
 new attacks that are similar to existing ones. The approach achieved excellent accuracy of 99.98% and 99.93%
 in binary and multi-class classification, respectively14. Shende and Thorat made an effort to deal with the
 increasing dependency on networks and a new high in cyber-attacks by putting into action an LSTM-based
 intrusion detection system with the use of the NSL-KDD dataset, which got 99.2% accuracy15. Sri Vidhya and

 Privacy-Preserving IDS Techniques: FL vs. Alternatives. Although Federated Learning (FL) manages to give
 solid privacy assurances by keeping the raw data at the locality and sending out only the model updates, there
 are still other cryptographic methods available. Homomorphic Encryption (HE) is among them, which permits
 encrypted data to be computed on, thus granting the maximum privacy but at a very high computational cost,
 making it unfeasible for high-throughput, real-time IDSs. Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC) gives the
 possibility of carrying out the computation together while the data of each party remains confidential, but this
 increases the communication and computational costs to a point where large distributed networks can hardly be
 scaled. Here, FL is indeed the most practical and scalable method, offering a better trade-off between privacy of
 data and the efficiency of computation needed for intrusion detection at near real-time. Generative Adversarial
 Networks (GANs) are still being investigated but their main application was for data augmentation in order to
 address the problem of class imbalance. Araujo-Filho et al.18 suggested an unsupervised IDS based on GANs
 and Temporal Convolutional Networks, while QU et al.19 made a model combining GANs and LSTMs for fog
 scenarios. In conclusion, these research works present the abilities of GANs but at the same time point out the
 difficulties involved in their use as compared to the more straightforward oversampling techniques like SMOTE.
 The processing of raw packet data and complex temporal sequences has become one of the main strengths
 of deep learning, which made the technology a must for analyzing complex network traffic. The research in the
 field suggests that there is a need -marshalling both deep packet inspection techniques and machine learningto augment network security measures6. Despite the advantages, deep learning application in security-related
 areas requires the delineation of the concern around adversarial attacks being one of the weaknesses, which
 in turn asks for new debiasing and data augmentation methods to be developed, so as to preserve the model’s
 robustness20. In addition, the systematic literature reviews will continue to lead the architectural performance
 assessment and the development of reliable and scalable systems such as FIDMF7.
 Federated Learning (FL) has become a prominent technology for protecting privacy in the machine learning
 security area. Hussain et al. suggested a federated learning model for mobile ad hoc networks (MANETs),
 which resulted in a significant increase in detection rates21. Sahu et al. presented a federated long short-term
 memory (LSTM) for anomaly detection with an extraordinary accuracy of 98.9%22. Rashid et al. made an effort
 to secure the privacy of IoT networks through federated learning techniques1. These publications demonstrate
 the applicability of FL in the security domain, but they usually do not provide a systematic solution for the class
 imbalance problem. Our project intends to close this gap by combining a strong FL framework with tested
 methods for temporal analysis and class imbalance reduction.

 Dataset and data preprocessing
 Dataset selection
 The NSL-KDD dataset is primarily used in our experiments, which is a widely recognized benchmark in the area
 of Intrusion Detection System (IDS) research. The dataset is made up of network traffic containing various attack
 types that have been marked with labels. As shown in Table 3, these datasets exhibit significant and varying
 degrees of class imbalance (e.g., NSL-KDD Test+ has 43.08% normal vs. 56.92% attack, while CIC-IDS2017 Test
 has 73.92% normal vs. 26.08% attack). To demonstrate the general applicability and adaptability of our technique
 to these varying class distributions, we further evaluate our framework on the latest CIC-IDS2017 and UNSW-
 NB15 datasets. These datasets are often used as standard tests and provide a considerable and varied mix of both
 normal and attack traffic.

 Data preprocessing
 Meticulous data preparation is the first step towards an effective IDS. We employ a multi-stage preprocessing
 pipeline locally on each client’s data:

 1. Feature Selection and Encoding: Features are selected based on domain knowledge. Categorical features are
 initially converted using one-hot encoding.
 2. Handling Missing, Noisy, and Corrupted Data: Prior to feature selection and encoding, a robust data cleaning pipeline is applied. Missing numerical values are imputed using the median strategy to preserve distribution and minimize the impact of outliers. For missing categorical features, we employ mode imputation. Outliers in numerical features are detected using the Isolation Forest algorithm and either winsorized (capped
 at a certain percentile) or replaced with the median, depending on the severity and frequency. Corrupted or
 inconsistent data entries are either corrected based on predefined rules or removed if a reliable correction is
 not possible. This ensures data quality before any learning processes begin.
 3. LLM-Powered Contextual Feature Enrichment (DistilBERT): We use a pre-trained DistilBERT-base-uncased model to overcome the limitations of simple one-hot encoding for categorical text features. Specifically,
 raw categorical features such as ’protocol_type’, ’service’, ’flag’, ’state’, etc., are first converted into a concatenated textual string for each network flow instance. For example, a flow might be represented as“protocol: tcp,
 service: http, flag: SF, state: ESTABLISHED”. This string is then tokenized and fed into DistilBERT. Distil-
 BERT processes this raw textual input to generate dense, contextual semantic embeddings (768-dimensional
 vectors) for each network flow. This deep contextual representation is superior to simpler encoders like TF-
 IDF or Word2Vec because it captures fine-grained semantic relationships and inter-feature context, which
 is crucial for robust IDS performance. These embeddings are then concatenated with the numerical features

 Proposed architecture overview
 The FIDMF architecture, visually conceptualized in Fig. 2, is a highly integrated system composed of several
 powerful and interconnected components operating in a decentralized manner.

 • Federated Learning Framework (The Privacy Backbone): The whole system is based on the Federated Learning (FL) paradigm. This framework superbly enables joint model training over many distributed clients while
 still not requiring the transferring of sensitive raw data (RQ1). The central server controls the entire procedure, regulating the global model updates (wr ) through the Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm. This
 non-centralized method is very crucial in keeping the highest level of data privacy while at the same time
 allowing for easy adaptation to large-scale network conditions in the real world.
 • Attention-LSTM Core (The Temporal Detection Engine): The base model consists of an attention mechanism
 that dynamically augmented LSTM network, which serves as the main component of the intrusion detection
 system. LSTM is very appropriate for handling the sequential data of the network flow logs processing and
 it can very accurately detect the tight temporal relationships and dependencies that show complex and multi-step attacks. The attention layer applied on top of detection works in parallel to detection and provides the
 model with importance weights that vary according to the most significant and relevant attack indicators of
 the network traffic sequence, thus, the model can dynamically concentrate its detection efforts.
 • Open-Source Large Language Models (LLMs) (The Semantic Intelligence Layer): FIDMF’s unique capability
 stems from the multi-faceted, strategic integration of open-source LLMs at three distinct phases:

 1. Contextual Feature Enrichment (DistilBERT): The client makes use of a lightweight yet extremely efficient DistilBERT model for the purpose of local preprocessing which helps to turn the raw categorical
 log features into dense and rich semantic embeddings thus overcoming significantly the limitations of the
 traditional encoders and making the model’s comprehension of the network semantics deeper (RQ2).
 2. Semantic Data Augmentation (GPT-2-medium): Thus, a highly sophisticated GPT-2-medium model
 shows the way for the Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) at . This skilled guiding not only ensures
 that the generated synthetic attack patterns are statistically varied but also that they are semantically consistent and really new, therefore, tremendously enhancing the system’s potential to resist zero-day and
 unknown threats (RQ3).
 3. Explainable AI (XAI) Generation (GPT-2-medium): The same fine-tuned GPT-2-medium acts as the XAI
 module post-detection, translating complex neural network decisions into clear, human-readable natural language explanations (RQ5). While traditional XAI methods like LIME, SHAP, or attention-weight
 visualizations (which we also employ, as shown in Fig. 12) provide local feature importance, they often
 lack the ability to synthesize these insights into a coherent, narrative explanation that is easily digestible
 by non-expert security analysts. GPT-2-based XAI, in contrast, leverages its natural language generation
 capabilities to construct a contextually rich, human-like explanation by drawing upon the salient features
 identified by the attention mechanism and the semantic embeddings from DistilBERT. This narrative
 generation capability provides a higher level of actionable insight and interpretability, making the system’s
 decisions transparent and understandable, thereby justifying its use over solely relying on feature attribution methods.Component roles and performance contributions summary: Each component of FIDMF
 plays a distinct, synergistic role contributing to the framework’s superior performance.

 • Federated Learning (FL): Ensures data privacy by keeping raw data local and exchanging only model updates. While introducing communication overhead, its integration via FedAvg allows collaborative training
 on distributed, diverse datasets without centralizing sensitive information, making large-scale deployment
 feasible (RQ1).
 • Attention-LSTM Core: The LSTM’s ability to model temporal dependencies is crucial for detecting sequential
 attack patterns, while the attention mechanism dynamically focuses on critical features within network traffic,
 significantly boosting detection accuracy and recall, especially for complex, multi-step attacks (demonstrated
 by the 1.64% accuracy increase from Centralized LSTM to Attention-LSTM in Table 8) (RQ2).
 • SMOTE: Addresses the foundational challenge of class imbalance by synthetically over-sampling minority
 attack classes, preventing models from biasing towards the majority normal class. This is evident from the
 dramatic F1-Score improvement for the normal class (from 81.23% without SMOTE to 98.24% with SMOTE
 in Table 8).
 • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs): Further enhances class balancing beyond SMOTE by generating
 more diverse and realistic synthetic attack patterns, crucial for improving robustness against novel attack

 (SMOTE + GANs) (RQ3).
 • DistilBERT (Contextual Feature Enrichment): Transforms raw categorical network features such as protocol, flag into dense, semantic embeddings. This deep contextual understanding allows the model to grasp

 the overall performance boost of the LLM-enhanced configurations (RQ2).
 • GPT-2-medium (Semantic Data Augmentation & XAI): Plays a dual critical role. For data augmentation, it

 into human-readable explanations, providing transparency and actionable insights for security analysts (RQ3,

 The overall data and model flow is meticulously orchestrated to prioritize speed and privacy. Raw data remains
 strictly local to each client. During local training, raw categorical features are enriched into semantic embeddings
 by DistilBERT. The local Attention-LSTM model is then trained on this enhanced, information-rich data.
 Critically, only the small, updated model weights (wk )—not the raw or enriched data—are securely transmitted
 to the central server for FedAvg aggregation. For rapid, real-time inference, the client utilizes the current best
 global model locally. Post-detection, for detected anomalies, the model’s critical features and attention weights
 are dynamically channeled to the local GPT-2 XAI module to generate the human-readable explanation, ensuring
 actionable transparency without compromising the speed of the primary detection function.

 Experimental setup
 The experiments were thoroughly planned in order to assess the effectiveness of our suggested behavioral
 intrusion detection system. The federated learning framework, which is the heart of the system, combines deep
 learning (LSTM, Attention, SMOTE) with open-source Large Language Models (LLMs).
 LLM Variant Evaluation: We selected DistilBERT-base-uncased (25.5M parameters) and GPT-2-medium
 (345M parameters) based on an internal comparative study, as they provided the most favorable compromise
 between semantic accuracy (< 0.2% F1-score difference from larger models) and computational efficiency (3
 to 5 times lower computational cost and memory).

 Conclusion and future work
 The research paper presented FIDMF, a versatile overall framework for federated learning in intrusion detection
 that progressively tackles three significant problems-data privacy, temporal attack detection, and class imbalance.
 The coupling and usage of large-scale open-source language models (LLMs), namely DistilBERT-base-uncased
 for the enhancement of contextual features and a fine-tuned GPT-2-medium for the augmentation of semantic
 data and explainable AI, is the main characteristic of the FIDMF intellectual and transparency uplift. By the
 smart merging of Federated Learning with Attention-LSTM and the employed combination of SMOTE and
 GANs assisted by LLMs for strong data balancing, FIDMF delivers excellent, generalizable performance across
 several benchmark datasets with an accuracy of 99.40% on NSL-KDD. The serious assessment we carried out
 along with the strict ablation study confirm that each part, including the LLM integrations, is vital for getting high
 performance. FIDMF is a strong, scalable, privacy-preserving, semantically intelligent, and explainable solution,
 which can be installed in the modern network environments. The trouble-free privacy-with-performance tradeoff, together with the deep semantic understanding and interpretability, creates a strong argument for the use of
 federated methods in security-sensitive applications.

 Limitations and future work
 Although the FIDMF is a major breakthrough, there are limitations and risks that shape our future research.

 Computational overhead, latency, and memory trade-offs
 The primary limitation is the large computational cost of the LLMs. The asynchronous strategy manages this
 trade-off, but the latency for XAI (3.5s per explanation) remains high. While the DistilBERT embeddings
 increase the base model size by ∼ 25.5MB this is a justifiable trade-off for the superior performance.

 Threat model and robustness management
 Our current system assumes a Semi-honest Server and Malicious/Adversarial Clients capable of:

 • Model poisoning during FedAvg.
 • Adversarial LLM attacks such as prompt injection on XAI, adversarial feature enrichment on DistilBERT.

 The framework manages these risks:

 • Unseen Attack Handling : Novel and rare attacks are handled by leveraging the DistilBERT contextual embeddings for superior generalization and the LLM-Guided GAN which generates truly novel, semantically
 coherent attack patterns for robust training.
 • GAN/GPT-2 Bias : This is managed by fine-tuning GPT-2 on a custom, expert-curated dataset to guide the
 GAN towards semantically plausible, coherent patterns, mitigating general biases and ensuring generative
 realism.

 1. Concept Drift and Evolving Attack Patterns: Our framework inherently handles evolving attack patterns
 through two key mechanisms: (i) the continuous, collaborative training in the federated learning paradigm
 allows the global model to adapt over time as clients encounter new attack variations and contribute updated
 knowledge; and (ii) the LLM-guided GAN for semantic data augmentation specifically generates novel attack
 patterns, which robustifies the model against zero-day threats and helps it generalize to new, evolving attack
 signatures. For long-term concept drift, periodic retraining of the global model (e.g., weekly or monthly)
 on the continuously collected and augmented local client data, combined with a mechanism for detecting
 performance degradation on new data streams, would be implemented. Future work will formalize dynamic
 model updating strategies and explore explicit concept drift detection methods.
 2. Real-World and Adversarial Robustness: Future work will focus on deployment in dynamic, real-world scenarios and rigorous exploration of defenses against adversarial machine learning attacks .
 3. Advanced FL Algorithms: We will examine more sophisticated FL algorithms to further enhance resistance
 against Non-IID data distributions and investigate semi-supervised learning techniques.23
 4. Enhanced Security: We will integrate techniques like Differential Privacy or secure aggregation to harden
 the framework against malicious clients and explore robust LLM defenses (prompt defense, data-centric
 debiasing).24
 5. Real-Time Deployment and Explainability: Upcoming work will focus on improving LLM inference for instantaneous explanation generation via methods like model quantization, distillation, and hardware specializing in edge AI.

 “Reproducibility details”. (4) Use pre-trained DistilBERT-base-uncased and fine-tuned GPT-2-medium

 Ethical and data privacy considerations
 Data Privacy and FL: Federated Learning, by design, ensures that raw network traffic data remains local, directly
 addressing privacy concerns associated with centralized data processing. This is a significant ethical advantage
 in environments dealing with sensitive user data. However, as discussed in the limitations, future work must
 incorporate formal privacy mechanisms like Differential Privacy to prevent model inversion attacks or inference
 of individual client data from shared model updated.
 The use of open-source LLMs (DistilBERT, GPT-2) is subject to their respective open licenses. Our use case
 (cybersecurity log analysis and explanation) falls within ethical guidelines, and the models are fine-tuned on
 a custom, domain-specific dataset to mitigate general societal biases inherent in large-scale pre-training data.
 Furthermore, we must address the ethical risk of malicious use.
 The Explainable AI (XAI) component of FIDMF directly enhances the ethical attribute of transparency.
 By providing human-readable explanations, the system moves beyond ’black-box’ decision-making, allowing
 security analysts to understand and audit the model’s behavior. This interpretability is vital for avoiding biased
 or discriminatory decision-making, which could have serious operational consequences.”

 Practical deployment guidance
 For future real-world deployment, the following guidance outlines key operational procedures. Clients should
 use the latest global model (Mglobal ) asynchronously downloaded from the server for inference. Local training
 and FedAvg aggregation should be scheduled during low-traffic periods to minimize real-time detection latency.
 DistilBERT feature enrichment is a one-time preprocessing step, whereas GPT-2 XAI generation must be run
 on-demand for critical anomalies to avoid constant computational load. The central server must continuously
 monitor client-side performance metrics (accuracy, loss) to detect model drift or potential poisoning attacks.
 Finally, periodic local retraining on newly collected data should be performed to ensure adaptability to evolving
 attack strategies.
