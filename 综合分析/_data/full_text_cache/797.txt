 The threat model in this paper considers a range of adversarial attacks targeting the space segment. Satellite systems
are capable of communicating not only with ground stations
but also with various other devices, such as user terminals
and routers, enabling direct data exchange. We assume that
the adversary possesses advanced penetration capabilities,
allowing them to control and exploit a large number of groundbased network routers and user terminal devices. Leveraging
these compromised devices, adversaries can directly target the
space segment—such as in-orbit satellites—through various
attack vectors, including malicious command injection, signal
spoofing, and denial-of-service (DoS) attacks. Additionally, by
compromising critical ground infrastructure (e.g., ground control stations), they can perform more covert and sophisticated
indirect attacks. Such attacks can result in precise disruption of
satellite nodes, theft of sensitive data, or even functional failure
and permanent paralysis, posing a severe threat to the stability
and security of the entire space system. Therefore, to effectively counter the growing cyber threats in satellite networks,
