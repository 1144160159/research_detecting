 IV. E VALUATION OF THE E XPERIMENT AND
 A NALYSIS OF THE R ESULTS
 The experimental data were obtained from an in-orbit satellite. Over six months of continuous operation, voltage and
current data from multiple channels were recorded by sensors
monitoring the atomic clocks operational status. The data were
sampled at 0.1 Hz. During in-orbit operation, several boundary
samples that fell below the alarm threshold were identified by
experts from the Chinese Space Agency. These samples serve Fig. 3. Optimal global features.
as critical empirical evidence supporting this study.
 The dataset contains 1894 time-series segments, among
which 92 are labeled as anomalous samples. These annotations are based on engineering ground event logs and expert
manual inspections, ensuring a high level of reliability. In the
experimental setup, the first 300 normal segments are selected
as the training set, and another 300 normal segments are used
as the validation set. In addition, two rare boundary samples,
identified by engineering experts, are included. The remaining
data constitute the test set, which includes 1200 normal
segments and the aforementioned 92 anomalous segments.
 The anomalous samples cover three major types: point
anomalies, contextual anomalies, and gradually evolving
anomalies. These types collectively represent a wide range
of abnormal behaviors that may occur during in-orbit satellite operations. Before being fed into the model, the raw
spaceborne sensor data undergo several preprocessing steps,
including data decoding, removal of transient outliers caused Fig. 4. Optimal local features.
by single-event upsets, and interpolation for estimating missing values, in order to improve data continuity and usability.
