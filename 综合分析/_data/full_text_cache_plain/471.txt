Intelligent Anomaly Detection Method for
Consumer Electronics Based on Feature-Driven
Learning and Diffusion Model
Guodong Wang , Qianqian Li , Qun Wang, Hadeel Alsolai , and Xuejia Jiang

Abstract—The increasing complexity of consumer electronics
and their dependence on continuous multivariate sensor data
make intelligent anomaly detection a critical task. This paper
proposes a novel anomaly detection framework, termed FDDiffusion (Feature-Driven Diffusion Framework), tailored to
time-series data generated by consumer electronic devices. The
framework is composed of three key components. First, the
Status-Aware Multimodal Encoder (SAME) extracts temporally
aligned and semantically enriched representations from heterogeneous sensor streams by incorporating device status, behavioral
context, and operational states. Second, the Condition-Aware
Diffusion Model with Residual Refinement (CADM) leverages
these embeddings to guide the reverse denoising trajectory of a
diffusion process, ensuring that reconstructed sequences conform
to normal behavior patterns. To enhance sensitivity to subtle
deviations, CADM includes a residual refinement decoder with
localized attention. Third, the Multi-Scale Adaptive Anomaly
Scoring Mechanism (MAASM) fuses reconstruction loss, latent
divergence, and semantic inconsistency into an interpretable and
adaptive anomaly score, adjusted dynamically based on recent
operational statistics. Experimental results on real-world device
datasets demonstrate that FD-Diffusion outperforms existing
methods in both detection accuracy and interpretability, offering a scalable solution for intelligent monitoring of consumer
electronics under dynamic and diverse usage conditions.
Index Terms—Feature-driven learning, diffusion model, timeseries anomaly detection, consumer electronics.

II. R ELATED W ORK
Machine learning-based anomaly detection algorithms primarily rely on distance- and density-based methods to identify
anomalies. Rathore and Harsha [22] proposed a K-Nearest
Neighbors (KNN) anomaly detection approach by calculating the distance between each data point and its k-nearest
neighbors, and labeling the top m data points with the
largest distances as anomalies. Xu et al. [23] used densitybased methods, specifically the Local Outlier Factor (LOF),
to detect outliers by analyzing the local density of data
regions—where a larger LOF value indicates that a point
lies in a lower-density neighborhood, and is thus considered
anomalous. Liu et al. [24] introduced a novel approach
combining Principal Component Analysis with noise handling
and Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with
Noise (PCA-DBSCAN). This method applies dimensionality reduction and spectral data clustering to identify and
remove anomalies, and incorporates tunable parameters to
improve clustering performance. Fenza et al. [25] applied
the K-means clustering algorithm to multivariate time-series
electricity consumption data from 370 users, assigning preliminary category labels to each user’s consumption profile,
which helps in detecting anomalous consumption patterns
in later tasks. Djenouri et al. [26] utilized the K-means
method to detect anomalies in urban traffic flow data by constructing a probabilistic database of traffic flow distributions
based on spatiotemporal data, and applying anomaly detection
to future traffic forecasts—effectively addressing detection
challenges under skewed data distributions. While these methods offer more flexible and sophisticated alternatives to
purely statistical approaches and have shown effectiveness in
many applications, they largely depend on manually defined
anomaly thresholds, are highly sensitive to assumptions about
data distributions and parameter settings, and require practitioners to possess strong domain expertise to properly
configure and tune the models. Moreover, they are computationally expensive, require substantial memory resources,
and generally lack robust generalization capabilities. Most
importantly, these algorithms focus primarily on modeling and
predicting features at individual time points, without accounting for the temporal dependencies inherent in time-series
data.

Deep learning-based anomaly detection models detect

Kozat [27] studied unsupervised anomaly detection and introduced an LSTM-based neural network algorithm to identify
product quality anomalies. Lindemann et al. [28] proposed a
data-driven quality optimization method for system anomaly
detection, where LSTM units were used to extract sequence
features and an anomaly detection model was built using OneClass SVM (OCSVM). Zhang et al. [29] and Lu et al. [30]
respectively employed Autoencoders (AE) and Denoising
Autoencoders (DAE) to extract abstract features from vibration
signals, overcoming the limitations of traditional handcrafted
features. These models were trained on normal data using
reconstruction error as the loss function. During testing, the
reconstruction error served as the anomaly score, and values
exceeding a set threshold were classified as anomalies. In
addition, Chen et al. [31] improved performance by randomly
varying the connection architectures of autoencoders, using
different structural configurations and connection densities to
reduce computational complexity. Combined with adaptive
sampling, this approach aimed to enhance model diversity
and reduce training time. Lin et al. [32] proposed a VAELSTM hybrid model for unsupervised time-series anomaly
detection, integrating the representation learning power of the
Variational Autoencoder (VAE) with the temporal modeling
capability of LSTM. The VAE module captured structural
patterns in local time windows, while the LSTM estimated
long-term dependencies across sequences, forming a classical
case of combining generative and predictive neural networks
in anomaly detection. Compared to autoencoders, Generative
Adversarial Networks (GANs) based on adversarial training
can generate more detailed sample reconstructions. Inspired
by GANs, Audibert et al. [33] proposed the USAD method
for multivariate time-series anomaly detection. USAD features
an autoencoder architecture with a two-phase adversarial
training framework that improves model stability and reduces
risks of collapse or non-convergence typical of GANs. Abul
Bashar and Nayak [34] introduced TanoGAN for unsupervised anomaly detection in time-series data, modeling
normal temporal behavior through adversarial training. Realtime time-series inputs are mapped to a latent space, then

reconstructed, and anomalies are detected based on the loss
between real and reconstructed data.
III. M ETHOD
A. Overview of FD-Diffusion Architecture
The proposed Feature-Driven Diffusion Framework (FDDiffusion) is an end-to-end architecture designed for anomaly
detection in multivariate time-series data from consumer
electronics. It integrates feature representation, generative
reconstruction, and adaptive scoring to capture both temporal dependencies and semantic patterns in sensor data. The
architecture is shown in Fig. 1. FD-Diffusion is composed
of three core modules: the Status-Aware Multimodal Encoder
(SAME), the Condition-Aware Diffusion Model with Residual
Refinement (CADM), and the Multi-Scale Adaptive Anomaly
Scoring Mechanism (MAASM). SAME extracts unified latent
features from heterogeneous sensor streams while embedding
operational context. CADM uses these features to guide a
reverse diffusion process, generating behaviorally consistent
reconstructions. A residual refinement decoder is incorporated
to enhance sensitivity to subtle variations. MAASM fuses
reconstruction error, latent divergence, and semantic deviation
into a final anomaly score, which is evaluated through an
adaptive thresholding strategy. Together, these three modules
form a unified framework capable of accurate and interpretable
anomaly detection in dynamic consumer device environments.
B. Status-Aware Multimodal Encoder
In real-world consumer electronics, devices are equipped
with various sensors to monitor temperature, current, CPU
usage, network signal strength, vibration, and more. These
multivariate sensor streams form a high-dimensional and
heterogeneous time-series signal that reflects the dynamic
behavior of the device. However, these signals are not only
multimodal in nature but are also inherently asynchronous,
noisy, and behavior-dependent. An effective anomaly detection
system must not only model intra-modality temporal dynamics
but also integrate cross-modal semantics under the influence
of time-varying operational status. To meet this requirement,
we design the Status-Aware Multimodal Encoder (SAME).

C. Condition-Aware Diffusion Model
To effectively detect anomalies in multivariate time-series
data from consumer electronics, it is not sufficient to merely
reconstruct inputs with low distortion. It is essential that
the reconstruction process be guided by contextual behavior
semantics, capable of reproducing the fine-grained, modalityspecific dynamics that characterize normal device operation.
The proposed Condition-Aware Diffusion Model with Residual
Refinement (CADM) addresses this challenge by extending
the standard denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM)
framework into a conditional and temporally refined generation process. CADM not only reconstructs input signals in a
stepwise denoising fashion, but also injects behavioral context
into the generative pathway and enhances local detail recovery
through residual refinement.

The design of CADM brings several benefits for the
task of time-series anomaly detection in consumer electronics. The progressive denoising mechanism ensures that the
model captures both coarse-grained and fine-grained structures
throughout the reconstruction pathway. The condition-aware
architecture allows the model to adaptively align reconstruction behavior with contextual semantics, which is essential for

local continuity, which is especially valuable when detecting
short, sharp anomalies that would otherwise be averaged out

By combining structured probabilistic modeling with
semantic guidance and localized reconstruction enhancement,

anomaly-oriented signal generation. Its outputs not only
support direct anomaly scoring but also offer interpretable
reconstruction pathways that reveal the dynamics of normalversus-abnormal transitions over time.

The design of MAASM reflects three important principles.
First, it acknowledges the multi-faceted nature of anomalies in
time-series signals by evaluating reconstruction quality, latent
distribution alignment, and semantic conformity simultaneously. Second, it adapts to data drift and behavioral change

FD-Diffusion pipeline.
IV. E XPERIMENT
A. Dataset
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed FD-Diffusion
framework, we conduct experiments on a real-world dataset
collected from consumer electronic devices in practical usage
scenarios. The dataset comprises multivariate time-series signals recorded from various internal sensors embedded in
devices such as smart home terminals, wearable electronics,
and portable communication modules. These sensors monitor
parameters such as temperature, voltage, power consumption,
CPU load, vibration, and wireless connectivity in real time. In
total, the dataset consists of 903028 samples, each representing
a short fixed-length segment of multivariate sensor readings.
These samples reflect a wide range of device operation
modes, including standby, active processing, charging, and
communication phases. Manual inspection and logging records
were used to identify both normal and anomalous behaviors,
though anomaly labels are only used for evaluation purposes
and not in model training. We divide the dataset into two nonoverlapping subsets. Two-thirds of the samples are used as
the training set, which contains only normal operation data
and is used to learn the underlying behavioral distribution.
The remaining one-third is used as the testing set, which
includes both normal and anomalous sequences for evaluation.
This split ensures a realistic setting where the model must
detect unseen and unlabeled anomalies based solely on learned
normal behavior during training.

encoder consists of three 1D convolutional layers followed

D. Comparison With Existing Methods
To validate the effectiveness of the FD-Diffusion framework, we compare it against existing anomaly detection
methods. All models are trained and tested under the same
dataset and evaluation protocol described in previous sections.
The implementations follow publicly available repositories or

reproduced settings from their original papers. The comparison is based on four standard metrics. Table I summarizes
the experimental results. FD-Diffusion achieves the best
performance across all evaluation metrics, significantly outperforming both classical and state-of-the-art baseline models.
Specifically, FD-Diffusion obtains an accuracy of 0.941, a
precision of 0.881, a recall of 0.863, and an F1 score of
0.872. Compared with the best-performing baseline method
MP-MixerNet, which achieves an F1 score of 0.831, our
method improves by 4.1 percentage points. When compared
to earlier transformer-based models such as TS2Vec (F1 score

complex inter-sensor correlations. Transformer-based methods including TranAD (F1 score 0.732) and AnomTran
(0.690) show improved learning over recurrent architectures
but still struggle to capture subtle, context-aware behavioral
anomalies. Recent multivariate anomaly detection models
like MP-MixerNet and MulGad demonstrate relatively strong
performance, both achieving F1 scores above 0.81. However,
their effectiveness tends to degrade under highly asynchronous
conditions or when sensor modalities exhibit distributional
imbalance. In contrast, FD-Diffusion leverages a behavioraware encoder, condition-guided diffusion reconstruction, and
a multi-perspective anomaly scoring mechanism. These components jointly contribute to maintaining high detection
precision (0.881) while significantly improving recall (0.863),
thereby achieving a strong balance between false positives
and false negatives. The observed numerical improvements
validate the advantage of integrating status-aware feature
learning with conditional generative modeling. Furthermore,
the use of adaptive scoring in MAASM enhances robustness
to operational variability, ensuring that the framework adapts
well to real-world fluctuations in device states. These results
confirm the generalization capability and high sensitivity of
FD-Diffusion in detecting diverse anomalies across complex
consumer electronics scenarios.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we proposed FD-Diffusion, a novel featuredriven diffusion-based framework for intelligent anomaly
detection in consumer electronics. The framework consists of
three key components: the Status-Aware Multimodal Encoder
(SAME), which captures behavioral context and heterogeneous sensor dependencies; the Condition-Aware Diffusion
Model (CADM), which reconstructs normal patterns through
guided generation and residual refinement; and the Multi-Scale
Adaptive Anomaly Scoring Mechanism (MAASM), which
integrates multi-perspective evaluation metrics to quantify
abnormality. Extensive experiments on a real-world consumer

operational conditions.
