Reference Model-Based Cyber-Attack Detection for Stochastically
Uncertain Wind Turbine Systems
Álvaro Martı́n Gómez , Sina Hassani , Szymon Greś , Rafał Wiśniewski , and Steven X. Ding

Abstract—As wind turbines become integral to critical infrastructure, ensuring their resilience against faults and cyberattacks is essential. This work implements a reference modelbased anomaly detection scheme for wind turbines with stochastic
parametric uncertainty. An Hi /H∞ reference filter balances fault
sensitivity and disturbance rejection, while a robust filter is
designed via an H∞ model matching problem to track reference
residuals while bounding the effect of uncertainty. This bound is
made less conservative by weighting residual components based
on their sensitivity to uncertainty. Residual covariance due to
parametric uncertainty is derived from a statistical analysis of
the residual generator dynamics and applied in a Generalized
Likelihood Ratio test. This provides a rigorous alternative to
heuristic covariance inflation by capturing the directional properties of uncertainty in the residual subspace. Experimental
results compare the residuals and uncertainty bounds of the
reference and robust filters. The proposed method reduces
residual sensitivity to changes in operating conditions as well
as decision function variability due to parametric uncertainty.
Index Terms—wind energy, anomaly detection, robust filters,
stochastic uncertainties.

I. I NTRODUCTION
YBER threats to the energy sector, particularly wind
generation, are escalating due to sophisticated attacks
by nation-state actors and financially motivated criminals.
Common attack vectors include ransomware (e.g., Nordex,
Deutsche Windtechnik), supply chain compromises, and exploitation of third-party access, often leading to operational
disruptions, loss of remote monitoring, or reputational damage
[1]. For instance, the 2022 Viasat satellite modem attack
disabled communication with 5,800 wind turbines [2], while
ransomware incidents forced temporary disconnections from
control systems. Unlike overt attacks like ransomware, where
detection is trivial but prevention plays a crucial role, false
data injection attacks (FDIA) may evade traditional safeguards,
and pose a subtler risk by manipulating operational data to
destabilize grid decisions without immediate detection. Hence,
they are the focus of this work.

provide effective approaches to anomaly detection without

modelling errors. This challenge is amplified when proprietary

have explored zonotopic observer-based approaches combined
with neuro-fuzzy or TS/MANFIS modelling, enabling setmembership state estimation and robust fault detection under
bounded uncertainties [9], [10]. These methods provide guaranteed consistency bounds and have shown strong performance
for fault detection under set-bounded uncertainty descriptions.
In this work, a different perspective is adopted by focusing on
stochastic parametric uncertainty, with the aim of enabling a
probabilistic characterization of residuals that can be directly
used in statistical change detection.

performance [14]. Other approaches have employed H∞ /H∞
optimization frameworks to simultaneously minimize disturbance effects while maximizing the maximum influence of
faults [15], as well as H− /H∞ formulations that specifically
maximize the minimum singular value of the transfer matrix

However, while these methods effectively handle unknown
inputs and stochastic noise, they remain fundamentally limited in addressing parametric uncertainties that affect system
dynamics and sensor/actuator parameters. Such parameter
variations corrupt residual signals, leading to performance
degradation through increased false alarms and missed detections. The reference model-based approach [14], [22]–[24]
addresses these challenges by formulating fault detection as
an H∞ model-matching problem (MMP). The method uses a
reference model, which serves as an optimal residual generator
under the assumption of no model uncertainties aside from
disturbances. Simultaneously, it aims to minimize the H∞
norm of the difference between the designed residual generator
and this reference model. With model parameters estimated via
Bayesian inference, modelling errors are treated as stochastic
uncertainties, leading to an LMI solution originally derived in
Chapter 8 in [21].
This anomaly detection scheme has been given very little
attention, mainly due to its complexity and its tendency to
become overly conservative. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, [25] is the only related application, which specifically
focuses on an open-loop wind turbine pitch actuator and drivetrain system for sensor and actuator fault detection. However,
the paper omits critical details regarding the uncertainties
chosen in the system parameters and lacks an evaluation of
the residual’s robustness improvements relative to the standard
Hi /H∞ unified solution when these uncertainties are present.
These gaps highlight the need for a more thorough and welldocumented application of this technique to key wind turbine
controllers, with a clearer demonstration of its advantages
and limitations and adjustments to some of the metrics to
reduce conservativeness. This paper is an extended version
of the work presented at the 2025 IEEE Industry Applications
Society Annual Meeting [26].
II. S TRATEGY OVERVIEW AND ATTACK S CENARIOS
The anomaly detection scheme of this work adopts a
reference model-based strategy, illustrated in Fig. 1, to address parametric uncertainty while preserving fault sensitivity.
The approach separates performance specification from robust
implementation through a two-step design. First, a reference
residual generator is constructed using the nominal model.
In this work, the Hi /H∞ ”unified solution” is selected as
reference residual generator, as it provides an optimal tradeoff between disturbance attenuation and fault sensitivity across
the residual space [7], [21]. This reference defines the desired
residual behaviour. Second, a robust residual generator is
obtained by solving an H∞ model-matching problem (MMP)
[21], where the objective is to minimize the deviation between
the implemented residual and the reference residual in the
presence of uncertainties. This formulation ensures that the
residual closely reproduces the nominal optimal response

while explicitly accounting for disturbances and parametric
variations. As shown in Fig. 1, the design is performed offline,
and the resulting robust filter is used online for residual
generation and subsequent anomaly detection.
Following the structure proposed in [27], the filter receives
potentially corrupted sensor measurements together with control signals that remain unaltered at the point of filtering, since
any manipulation of these signals would occur downstream
in the communication network. However, these uncorrupted
control signals may still reflect the effects of attacks due to
the feedback loop.
In this work, we focus on false data injection attacks
(FDIA), i.e., integrity attacks that directly manipulate sensor
and actuator signals in the control loop. In contrast to availability attacks such as denial-of-service, which are typically
straightforward to detect due to missing or blocked data,
FDI attacks alter critical variables while preserving normal
communication patterns, making them significantly more challenging to detect and potentially more damaging for closedloop operation
The chosen attack scenario corresponds to a bias injection
attack, a subclass of FDIA introduced in [27]. This attack
consists of injecting a slowly varying bias into selected signals,
with the objective of gradually driving the system away from
its nominal operating condition and potentially into an unsafe
region. This class of attacks requires only limited model
knowledge and no access to the full system state, making
it representative of realistic adversaries with constrained resources.
The attack is generated through a first-order low-pass dynamics, allowing the adversary to regulate the rate of change of
the injected signal via the gain parameter a∞ and time constant

The model (31)-(36) is linearized via a Taylor expansion

• Test 2 (torque reference attack): bias injection in Tg,ref

a distinguishable residual response. The time constant is fixed
at βa = 0.999 in (1), with a sampling interval of 0.01 s, resulting in a sufficiently abrupt injection profile for visualization

Fig. 4 shows the residual behaviour for Test 1, where the
bias injection attack is applied at t = 200 s, inducing a steadystate bias of 1·ωrop into the measurement of ωr . The controller
compensates the false rotor speed increase by adjusting the
pitch angle, which in turn causes the reduction of the actual
rotor speed. This triggers the first reference residual, while the
robust residuals show a reduced sensitivity, alongside a general
decrease in residual variability.
Fig. 5 shows the residual behaviour for Test 2, where the
attack is applied to the reference torque Tg,ref , inducing a
steady-state bias of −0.1 · Tgop . To compensate and maintain
the rotor speed operating point, the control system responds
with a slight increase in pitch actuation, since pitch control
has a significantly greater influence on rotor speed than torque
control. As a result, the actual rotor speed slightly decreases,
while the actual generator torque increases. The affected
residuals are therefore rωr and rTg .
Fig. 6 shows the residual behaviour for Test 3, where a bias
injection attack is applied to the pitch reference signal βref
with magnitude 0.1 · β op . The system rapidly compensates for
the induced bias to prevent a drop in rotor speed, resulting in a
decrease in the actual pitch angle and a corresponding increase
in the actual rotor speed. These deviations trigger responses
in the residuals rωr and rβ .

To more clearly illustrate the response of the MMP residuals
to a considerable change in operating conditions, the operating
point of the wind was changed from 14 m/s to 10 m/s at
t = 100 s, as shown in Fig. 7. In the figure, the dark and
light red curves represent the first Hi /H∞ reference residual
and the H∞ MMP robust residual for the perturbed system,
respectively. For comparison, the dark and light blue curves
show the same rωr residuals from Fig. 6. It is clear from the
results that the robust residual is significantly less sensitive to
the sudden change in operating conditions than the reference
residual.
As explained in Section III, selecting thresholds based solely
on the theoretical χ2 distribution under H0 lead to overly
optimistic false-alarm rates. Therefore, the threshold is chosen
as the (1 − α)-quantile of the empirical distribution of J(k)
under attack-free operation to achieve a desired false-alarm
probability α. For α = 0.5%, this yields Jth = 152.96 for the
Hi /H∞ filter and Jth = 85.54 for the robust filter.
Robustness and sensitivity are evaluated under identical calibration conditions, with results summarized in Table IV. Under
H0 , the 99th percentile of J(k) is lower for the robust filter
(q0.99 = 79.88) than for the Hi /H∞ filter (q0.99 = 126.55),
indicating reduced sensitivity to uncertainty. Under H1 , both
methods achieve a detection probability of 1 within a 10 s

window after the attack onset for all tested scenarios, while
the robust filter yields equal or smaller detection delays (e.g.,
3.6 s vs. 7 s in Test 1).
Regarding computational cost, the online implementation
reduces to a linear observer update and the evaluation of the
GLR test statistic. In the MATLAB implementation, the average execution time per iteration for the robust filter and GLR
test statistic is 1.6 × 10−5 s and 4.16 × 10−4 s, respectively,
which is well below the sampling period of 0.01 s.
Fig. 8 complements Fig. 7 by comparing the GLR test
statistics of the reference and robust approaches under the
change in operating conditions from 14 m/s to 10 m/s at
t = 100 s. Although the robust residual generator is designed
to handle variations of up to 1 m/s, a larger change of
4 m/s highlights more clearly its performance, resulting in a
noticeable reduction in variability compared to the reference
filter.
VI. C ONCLUSION
This paper presents a reference model-based anomaly detection scheme for wind turbine systems subject to stochastic
parameter uncertainties, with a particular focus on detecting
false data injection cyber-attacks. The approach employs an
Hi /H∞ reference filter, which offers an optimal balance
between fault sensitivity and disturbance rejection, to design
a robust filter through an H∞ model matching framework.
This filter tracks the reference residuals while accounting for
parametric uncertainty. A closed-form expression for the residual covariance is derived from the statistical properties of the
residual generator dynamics, providing a rigorous alternative
to the common practice of inflating process noise covariances.
Furthermore, the robust filter design is modified by weighting
the influence of each residual component in the derivation
of the residual variance bound to regulate conservativeness.
Experimental results show a substantial reduction in residual
sensitivity to uncertainty. The method’s effectiveness strongly
depends on accurate modelling of system uncertainties; other-
