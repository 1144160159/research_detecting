GraphTAN: Temporal Attention Network for
Learning Graph-Level Embedding
Yan-Jiang Cai , Hai-Chun Cai , Chun-Yang Zhang , C. L. Philip Chen , Life Fellow, IEEE,
and Qian-Xi Tang

Abstract—Numerous significant temporal graph tasks, such
as graph similarity ranking, trend analysis and anomaly detection, necessitate low-dimensional and high-order graph-level
embedding in terms of the evolving nodes and topologies over
time. However, most existing graph embedding methods focus on
extracting node-level embeddings, while ignoring these evolutions.
Therefore, these methods inadequately consider the impact of
trends on the overall graph. Moreover, there are a large number
of nodes in temporal graphs, which make it difficult to generate
effective graph embeddings directly by aggregating dynamic
node embeddings. In this study, we propose a novel temporal
attention network for learning graph-level embedding learning
called GraphTAN. Specifically, the proposed model employs
pooling attention to select crucial nodes and filter out noisy ones
for each snapshot, thereby enhancing the quality of aggregated
graph embeddings. Furthermore, we design a graph-level temporal attention mechanism to effectively extract temporal graph
embeddings, capturing trends and patterns across snapshots.
The proposed model is evaluated on three downstream tasks.
Experimental results demonstrate that GraphTAN captures both
the topology structure and fine-grained trend effectively, outperforming the state-of-the-art methods with big margins on multiple
tasks over several real networks.
Index Terms—Anomaly detection, graph-level representation
learning, graph neural networks (GNNs), temporal attention,
trend analysis.

II. RELATED WORKS
Graph representation learning methods are primarily categorized into two types: node-level and graph-level methods.
In static graph scenarios, they represent each node or graph
with a single vector. Alternatively, temporal graphs involve the
analysis of multiple snapshots, which entails generating a set
of vectors for each node or snapshot, with a different number
of nodes at each timestamp.
A. Static Node-Level Embedding
Matrix factorization-based methods involve decomposing
graph-associated matrices, such as adjacency or Laplacian matrices, into lower-dimensional representations, while preserving key structural relationships of the graph. Techniques for
static node embedding encompass matrix factorization-based
methods, such as singular value decomposition (SVD) [21] and
locally linear embedding (LLE) [22], which decompose the
Laplacian matrix or higher-order adjacency matrices to generate node embeddings that preserve distances between nodes.
Random walk-based methods specify different walking strategies to generate a sequence of nodes starting from every node
on the graph, further maximizing the occurrence probabilities
of all the nodes in the sequence when searching for their representations. Random walk-based methods such as DeepWalk
[23] and node2vec [1] create random paths over the graph and
produce node embeddings through a word2vec-like architecture
[24]. Deep learning-based methods employ neural networks to
learn graph representations by jointly encoding the topological
structure and node attributes. In recent years, deep learningbased methods, particularly GNNs [16], have gained popularity,
which includes graph convolutional networks (GCN) [2], graph
attention networks (GAT) [25], and GraphSage [26]. These
methods employ effective message-passing mechanisms to aggregate information between nodes, producing expressive node
representations. However, traditional static graph methods are
limited in their ability to handle temporal graphs, as they only
capture static structural information and struggle to account for
temporal variations.
B. Temporal Node-Level Embedding
A significant portion of existing research endeavors focuses
on the depiction of node-level embeddings within the temporal
domain. Part of these methods use static node embedding techniques directly, which are independently applied to temporal
snapshots individually, followed by aligning them to produce a
unified representation [27]. However, the alignment technique
struggles to handle scenarios where nodes are added or removed
over time. As a result, many methods use sequence models to
deal with the temporal nodes.

Dynamic self-attention network (DySAT) [28] combines
information from the structural neighborhood and temporal
dynamics patterns through two attention modules to
generate dynamic node representations. However, it
has high model complexity, which is not applicable
to large-scale graphs. Motif-preserving temporal shift
network (MTSN) [29] introduces a dynamic GNN
architecture that integrates network structure features and
temporal patterns through a time-series migration mechanism.
This design aims to capture the evolutionary patterns inherent
in dynamic networks proficiently. DyG2Vec [30] introduces a
window-based encoder–decoder model for temporal graphs.
TREND [31] operates on temporal events and node dynamics,
utilizing a GNN framework grounded in the Hawkes process.
However, this process formulation does not scale for large
graphs with millions of links.
While the aforementioned methods exhibit diversity, they all
focus on temporal node-level representations. Consequently,
aggregating node embeddings into graph-level embeddings is
challenging, as these methods predominantly capture local node
dynamics, making it difficult to effectively model the global
temporal patterns of the entire graph.
C. Static Graph-Level Embedding
In graph-level methodologies, the emphasis is placed on analyzing and modeling the overall structure of the graph, rather
than concentrating solely on individual nodes or local node
relationships. UGraphEmb [10] involves embedding the entire
graph into a vector space by utilizing the Graph Edit Distance
(GED) metric [32] to preserve the proximity between graphs
as the guidance for the training process. Hierarchical graph
contrastive learning (HGCL) [33] proposes a hierarchical graph
contrastive learning framework, which explores the hierarchical
structural semantics of graphs at both node and graph levels.
Meanwhile, Hi-PART [34] develops an effective pooling mechanism with a hierarchical partition tree, offering a significant
improvement over the Weisfeiler–Lehman test. Graph2vec [19]
conducts a static embedding of the entire graph without taking
temporal factors into account, analogizing the graph to a document. In this analogy, rooted subgraphs surrounding each node
are construed as contextual elements constituting the document.
While static graph-level embedding methods effectively learn
graph representations, they are challenging to apply directly to
temporal graphs, as they fail to capture trends and overlook temporal patterns. Specifically, these methods treat each timestamp
independently, without integrating the dynamics necessary for
evolving graph embeddings.
D. Temporal Graph-Level Embedding
Temporal graph-level embedding methods differ significantly from node-level embedding methods. The latter focuses
on capturing individual node changes, which can be managed with classical sequence models. In contrast, temporal
graph-level tasks demand an extensive understanding of global
changes across the entire graph. Temporal graph-level embedding involves assessing both the influence of nodes on the

overall graph structure and their relevance over time, presenting
a more complex and challenging task. The tdGraphEmbed [8]
leverages the Doc2Vec [35] architecture to produce temporal
graph-level embeddings. Unlike traditional node embedding
methods that use random walks, this approach globally embeds
the entire graph structure and its nodes at each timestamp.
Notably, it incorporates node attributes shared across different timestamps to capture the temporal patterns. In contrast,
GraphERT [9] employs Transformer architecture [36] to embed the entire graph. It encodes each snapshot of the graph
by conducting numerous random walks, which are fed into
Transformer. By incorporating a temporal model to forecast
the temporal indices of the random walks, the model captures
both the contextual information of the paths and their temporal
relationships.
Although existing methods have shown effectiveness, they
rely on a two-stage process that separately learns snapshot
features and temporal relationships, which struggle to adapt to
rapidly changing snapshots and temporal node importance [37].
In addition, these methods apply a uniform strategy across all
snapshots [30]. This strategy fails to capture the dynamic influence of nodes on the graph, potentially undermining structural
integrity and incorporating unimportant nodes into graph-level
embeddings [38]. Furthermore, these methods fail to account
for variations in node importance over time, leading to a suboptimal exploration of evolutionary patterns in temporal graphs
from both structural and feature perspectives. This highlights
the need for explicit modeling of evolution to more effectively
capture both structural and temporal patterns.
III. PROPOSED MODEL
In this section, the main components of GraphTAN are
introduced. As shown in Fig. 2, GraphTAN has three modules:
structural attention module, pooling attention module, and temporal attention module. With these three different modules, efficient learning of temporal graph-level embeddings is achieved.
The three modules collaborate to capture temporal patterns and
can be stacked to construct GNNs with different complexity.

of T graph snapshots: G = {G1 , G2 · · · , GT }. Each snapshot
is denoted as Gt = (Vt , Et ), where Vt represents nodes and Et
represents edges. Consequently, each snapshot encapsulates the
graph’s structure over a discrete timestamp.
Let V represent the set of all nodes present across all snapshots of G. Therefore, a subset of the vertex set Vt within Gt can
be specified as Vt ⊆ V . Notably, the count of nodes at each time
instance may vary from those in preceding instances, meaning
that |Vt | = |Vt+1 |.
Our goal is to learn the embedding htG of the graph at each
timestamp t ∈ {1, 2, . . . , T }, where htG ∈ Rd . The graph embeddings are expected to capture the temporal patterns of the
graph, encompassing both its topological structure and node
features, which can be effectively utilized in various downstream tasks.

E. GraphTAN Architecture
In this section, the overall framework of GraphTAN is
introduced, which is a new temporal GNN architecture
developed for learning graph-level embeddings. GraphTAN incorporates structural attention, pooling attention, and temporal
self-attention layers as fundamental modules. The model is
designed to process a collection of snapshots with the objective
of learning graph-level embeddings for each snapshot.
The structural attention layer computes node embeddings for
each snapshot by extracting features from the local neighborhood of each node. These node embeddings are fed into the
pooling layer, which computes the importance of each node
through attention scores and selects the nodes that are more important for each snapshot. Following the selection of nodes, the
readout function is used for generating graph-level embeddings,
serving as the input for the temporal attention layer. This layer
is designed to concentrate on historical snapshots to capture
temporal patterns in the graph structure. The temporal attention layer has the capability to provide temporal graph-level
embeddings and is designed to enhance adaptability to dynamic
changes within the graph-level embeddings. The integration of
structural and temporal patterns allows models to better capture
the temporal evolution of interactions and their impact on graph
attributes, resulting in more accurate predictions and deeper
insights into temporal graphs.

IV. EXPERIMENT
In this section, the effectiveness of the proposed GraphTAN is evaluated on four large real-world social networks.
By comparing the results of similarity ranking and anomaly
detection tasks with state-of-the-art models, it is confirmed that
the embeddings generated by GraphTAN demonstrate superior
performance across multiple tasks.
In the experiments, all graph snapshots as well as nodes at
the current timestamp are available, excluding newly emerging
nodes that have not been previously observed. This setup ensures a controlled environment for evaluating the effectiveness
of our approach.
A. Datasets
Four large real-world social networks are used in the following experiments, including Facebook wall posts, the Enron
email dataset, the Reddit Game of Thrones community, and
the Reddit Formula1 community. A comprehensive statistical
overview of these networks is delineated in Table I, which
describes the number of nodes and edges, the time step and the
temporal resolution of each temporal graph.
1) Facebook wall posts is a social network, nodes represent
users, and directed edges represent that user A posts on
the wall of user B at a given timestamp, spanning August
2006 to January 2009 with monthly resolution.
2) Enron is the company’s email network that captures all
internal communications, with temporal edges denoting
emails from user A to B at time t. The timestamps are
weekly from January 1999 to June 2002.

3) Reddit “Game of Thrones” includes daily interaction
graphs for 62 days, from July 1 to August 31, 2017, based
on user comments and replies.
4) Reddit “Formula1” with graphs constructed at a daily
level of detail for 61 successive days, from March 1 to
April 30, 2019.
B. Baselines
We conducted comprehensive experiments with a variety of
state-of-the-art benchmarks, encompassing both node-level and
graph-level embedding methods.
For the static node embedding approach, node changes over
time are captured by aligning each node’s embedding to the
same feature space on each snapshot. The graph embeddings are
derived through node embeddings, by utilizing the conventional
average pooling function. The following node-level embedding
techniques are taken into account.
1) Node2vec (2016) [1] employs the node2vec algorithm on
each snapshot and subsequently adjusts the embeddings
through rotation to achieve alignment [27].
2) SDNE (2016) [3] employs the SDNE algorithm on
each snapshot and subsequently adjusts the embeddings
through rotation to achieve alignment [27].
3) GAE (2016) [41] implements GNN with mask autoencoder framework on graph data, using GCNs as encoder
and decoder. GAE is applied to each snapshot and subsequently adjusts the embeddings through rotation to
achieve alignment [27].
4) DynamicTriad (2018) [42] utilizes the Dynamic Triad
to extract dynamic graph node embedding, ensuring
the preservation of both structural and evolutionary
graphs.
5) Dynamic SDNE (2018) [11] employs deep autoencoders
in a manner akin to SDNE, progressively creating node
embeddings for a graph at time t, initiating with the node
embeddings at time t − 1.
6) DynAERNN (2020) [6] initiates the process with a fully
connected encoder to extract a low-dimensional, hidden representation, which is subsequently fed through an
LSTM network and a fully connected decoder.
7) EvolveGCN (2020) [43] uses GCN to generate node
embeddings on each snapshot. These embeddings are
subsequently input to a recurrent architecture to capture
temporal dynamics. The model is trained for a link prediction task, and node embeddings are extracted at each
timestamp.
8) MTSN (2021) [29] incorporates network motif into a
temporal transition framework, which adeptly captures
the developmental trends within evolving networks. The
model is trained on link prediction task, and node embeddings are extracted at each timestamp.
9) TREND (2022) [31] is driven by temporal events
and node dynamics, utilizing a Hawkes process-based
GNN. The model is also trained on link prediction
task, and the node embeddings are derived from each
timestamp.

H. Complexity Analysis
The main computational cost of GraphTAN is composed of
three attention modules. We assume that the output dimension
of each layer is d. For structural attention layer, the complexity
of calculating Ht is O(ETd), where E represents the number of
edges, and T denotes the number of snapshots. The complexity
of the pooling attention layer and temporal self-attention layer
is O(NTd + T 2 d), where N represents the number of nodes in
each snapshot. Therefore, the overall complexity of GraphTAN
is O(ETd + pNTd + T 2 d).
For tdGraphEmbed and GraphERT, γ is the number of samples per node, and L indicates the length of random walk
sequences. w is the window size for the context nodes. In addition, we record the training time of each baseline for a single
epoch in Table VII, where UGraphEmb is the static graph-level
method, while tdGraphEmbed and GraphERT are the temporal
graph-level methods. As shown in Table VII, GraphTAN is
more efficient than other temporal graph-level methods in terms
of both training time and computational complexity. Although
UGraphEmb, as a static graph-level method, demonstrates the
highest efficiency by disregarding temporal correlations, its
performance on other tasks is significantly inferior to that of
temporal graph-level methods. Overall, the proposed GraphTAN achieves a satisfactory balance between performance and
computational complexity.

V. CONCLUSION
Learning low-dimensional graph embeddings is crucial for
multiple temporal tasks, yet most existing research focuses on
node-level embeddings. GraphTAN is a self-supervised, endto-end graph-level embedding method for temporal graphs.
Specifically, we propose a novel architecture based on three
attention mechanisms, a practical new approach for the modeling of temporal graphs. This approach allows for the adaptive
incorporation of a variety of information into the embedded
learning process.
Extensive experimental results have shown that GraphTAN
outperforms both static and temporal node/graph embedding
methods in graph similarity and temporal anomaly detection
tasks. The results of the ablation study underscore the critical role that temporal features play in learning high-quality
graph embeddings. While the random walk-based methods have
proven effective, they struggle with nodes with the importance
that varies over time. Therefore, an end-to-end model is required to capture structural features, identify temporal patterns,
and filter out temporal noise nodes. Although GraphTAN excels
at modeling temporal graphs, its multiple attention modules
increase training difficulty and computational complexity. Additionally, the model’s generalization may be limited, requiring
further adjustments when applied to different datasets and tasks.
Moreover, works on training strategies for temporal graph-level
embedding models remain limited. Future work could explore
unsupervised approaches, such as contrastive learning and generative modeling, to train complex models more efficiently and
enhance generalization.
