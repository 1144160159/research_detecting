MSFE-AD: Multiscale Feature-Enhanced Early
Satellite Sensor Data Anomaly Detection
Based on Rare Boundary Samples
Lin Yang , Xiyuan Peng , Kai Guo , Student Member, IEEE, and Datong Liu , Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract—With the increasing number of satellites being
launched, in-orbit health monitoring has garnered significant
attention. Analyzing operational data collected by spaceborne
sensors enables the early detection of anomalies, thereby helping
to prevent critical failures. However, due to the high morphological similarity between early fault data and normal data, achieving
high detection rates while maintaining low false alarm rates
remains a challenge. To address this issue, we propose a novel
anomaly detection method that leverages rare boundary samples
to guide key feature selection for feature augmentation, thereby
constructing a highly discriminative feature space. The deep
model incorporates a multifeature anomaly detection strategy
to accurately identify abnormal data. By jointly extracting features from multiple domains and employing an adaptive weight
allocation mechanism, the model enhances generalization across
diverse data distributions. Experiments on real satellite sensor
datasets demonstrate excellent detection performance, achieving
a recall of 0.826, a precision of 0.938, and an F1-score of 0.878,
indicating strong detection capability and robustness. Ablation
studies confirm the effectiveness of integrating multiple feature
augmentation strategies in balancing detection sensitivity and
precision. These results highlight the proposed method’s superior
ability to detect early anomalies in satellite systems.
Index Terms—Anomaly detection, early anomalies, feature
enhancement, satellite telemetry, sensor data.

Early anomaly detection is challenging due to the high similarity between anomalous sensor data and normal data. Some
anomalous data overlap with normal data and are difficult
to distinguish (see Fig. 1); when there are no representative
boundary samples, the model cannot learn to distinguish
between normal and anomalous classes, and the samples near
the decision boundary can be misclassified. In spacecraft
anomaly detection systems, boundary information can lead to
overly conservative thresholds or overly sensitive thresholds.
It is worth noting that during satellite operation and maintenance, engineers typically perform regular manual inspections
of sensor data. Boundary samples are often manually identified
by experts and are indicative of weak faults. However, these
samples are often discarded due to the difficulty of incorporating them into anomaly detection models. Due to the diversity
of anomaly patterns, anomaly detection is often approached
as a semisupervised learning task. This approach enables the
model to detect a wide range of anomalies, but it neglects
boundary samples that contain valuable prior knowledge. In
our ongoing observations of in-orbit satellite sensor data, we
have found that some anomalous data missed by the detector
exhibit similarities to boundary samples previously labeled by
experts. Therefore, we propose to leverage these previously
discarded boundary samples to enhance the performance of
the anomaly detector. These samples are distributed near the
decision boundary between the normal and anomalous classes
in the feature space, but they occur infrequently and are
commonly referred to as rare boundary samples.
Boundary samples play an important role in classification
tasks, as they may influence the positioning of decision boundaries [14]. In particular, boundary oversampling techniques can
enhance overall performance in imbalanced classification tasks
[15]. These methods employ data augmentation techniques to

mitigate category imbalance and enhance the classifiers ability
to recognize boundary samples [16]. However, several challenges remain with existing methods. The generated samples
may be overly concentrated in certain regions, causing the
model to rely excessively on localized features and resulting
in overfitting. In addition, these methods are ineffective in
handling the classification of multidimensional, multiclass,
imbalanced data. Moreover, data augmentation techniques
designed for time-series classification may not be directly
applicable to time-series anomaly detection tasks [17].
To address the aforementioned challenges, we propose the
MSFE-AD model, which fully leverages the prior knowledge
contained in expert-labeled boundary samples during training.
The proposed method effectively captures subtle anomalies
induced by incipient faults.
The main contributions of this article are summarized as
follows.
1) This article presents the first attempt to improve anomaly
detection performance using rare boundary samples.
A feature engineering method is introduced to significantly enhance data characterization by mining potential
features from original data and selecting the most discriminative subset.
2) A multiscale anomaly detection strategy is proposed,
employing parallel processing of heterogeneous detection models to improve detection precision and generalization ability. A learnable influence factor is introduced
to adaptively adjust the contribution weights of different
detection strategies, maintaining model stability in complex environments. This approach effectively balances
the tradeoff between detection sensitivity and robustness,
providing an interpretable and adaptable solution for
early satellite anomaly detection.

II. IR ELEVANT M ETHODOLOGIES
Sensor data are a form of time-series data. Timeseries anomaly detection methods are generally categorized
into prediction- and reconstruction-based approaches [18].
Prediction-based methods are sensitive to short-term fluctuations and are well-suited for real-time anomaly detection. In
contrast, reconstruction-based methods are more effective in
capturing trend deviations in time-series data. In this work,
we adopt a reconstruction-based anomaly detection approach.
Among various reconstruction models, autoencoders (AEs)
and their variants have been widely adopted for this task.
A. Anomaly Detection Methods Based on Reconstruction
AEs are a class of neural networks designed for data
compression and reconstruction tasks. They are designed to

replicate input data through learned internal representations.
An AE typically consists of an encoder and a decoder. The
encoder maps the input into a latent representation, commonly
referred to as the encoding, while the decoder reconstructs the
input data from this latent space, referred to as the decoding.
When trained on normal data, AEs typically exhibit large
reconstruction errors when presented with anomalous inputs,
making them well-suited for time-series anomaly detection.
For example, Kim et al. [19] proposed a Bayesian
auto-optimization approach for AE hyperparameters, which
enhances anomaly detection performance for boiler tube
leakages by optimizing latent dimensionality. Rani et al.
[20] introduced a probabilistic wavelet neural operator AE
(PWNOAE) for anomaly detection in industrial process data.
In this model, wavelets were employed for time–frequency
localization of time-series data, while deep models were used
to learn complex features from the data.
Variational AEs (VAEs) are generative models that combine
the strengths of AEs and variational inference. Compared with
traditional AEs, VAEs offer enhanced generative capabilities,
more flexible latent representations, and improved probabilistic
modeling. Li et al. [21] incorporated recurrent neural network
(RNN) and gated recurrent unit (GRU) structures to model
temporal dependencies and uncertainty in time series and
employed a VAE to compute reconstruction loss, followed by
a density-based detector for anomaly identification. To address
the nonlinearity, dynamics, and non-Gaussian characteristics of
industrial process data, Zhang et al. [22] proposed a recurrent
Kalman VAE (RK-VAE), which integrates Kalman filtering for
noise suppression.
Long short-term memory (LSTM) networks possess powerful nonlinear modeling capabilities and effectively capture
temporal dependencies in sequential data. When combined
with AEs or VAEs, the resulting models leverage the strengths
of both structures, thereby improving their suitability for
time-series anomaly detection. Han et al. [23] introduced an
LSTM-based VAE (LSTM-VAE) with reconstruction probability for fault detection in ship components. Guha et al. [24]
proposed an anomaly detection mechanism that first applies
wavelet decomposition to extract high- and mid-frequency
signals, thereby mitigating the impact of rare anomalies in
the training data, and then uses a VAE-LSTM model for
reconstruction-based detection in power grid data.
B. Feature Enhancement Methods
In time-series analysis, feature enhancement can significantly improve model performance by emphasizing
salient characteristics of the original data. Frequency-domain
enhancement techniques improve the models capacity to capture periodicity and trends by extracting spectral features from
time-series data. Frequency-enhanced channel attention mechanisms [25], which apply frequency-domain transformations to
improve feature extraction in time-series prediction, emphasize
the importance of multiscale frequency information. Dualfrequency augmented attention networks [26], which combine
high- and low-frequency components to predict the remaining
useful life of aircraft engines, demonstrate superior performance by capturing distinct frequency decay modes.

3564212

Self-supervised learning leverages the data itself to automatically generate labels, thereby improving the models ability to
discriminate time-series features. TimesURL [27] introduces
a self-supervised contrastive learning framework to improve
both the quantity and quality of negative samples. Hu et al.
[28] proposed a generalized representation model based on
contrastive learning, employing recurrent generative adversarial networks (R-GANs) to generate positive and negative
samples.
The multiscale fusion technique enhances model learning
for complex time series by integrating features across different
scales. Zhang et al. [29] introduced a hierarchical transformer
structure to capture frequency-domain features at multiple time
scales, enhancing multivariate time-series prediction through
multiscale feature fusion. Zhong et al. [30] employed the
discrete wavelet transform (DWT) to obtain multiscale timeseries features, combined with an energy-based multiscale
weighting strategy to improve the models transferability. These
methods emphasize the importance of multiscale modeling in
addressing complex time-series analysis tasks.
These research efforts provide a solid theoretical foundation
for this work. The following section elaborates on the novel
feature enhancement framework proposed herein.
III. P ROPOSED M ETHOD
In this section, the framework of MSFE-AD is introduced,
including three components: feature engineering, reconstruction modeling, and detection strategy, as illustrated in Fig. 2. In
the feature engineering module, both global and local features
are extracted, and the most representative features are selected
and reinforced in the data. The reconstruction model is implemented using a VAE, with LSTM networks employed for both
the encoder and the decoder to reconstruct the input time
series. The detection strategy employs two complementary
methods at different time scales to enhance anomaly detection
performance. An influence factor is introduced to adaptively
weight the contribution of each detection strategy, maintaining
a balance between them.

B. Reconstruction Model
Reconstruction-based anomaly detection methods require
training a model that effectively captures the distribution
of normal data. In this work, a VAE-LSTM architecture is
adopted as the baseline model. A VAE is a generative model
in which the encoder maps input data to a latent distribution, and the decoder reconstructs the original data from this
representation. A well-known limitation of traditional AE is
its tendency to overfit during training. To address this, the
VAE encodes inputs as probability distributions—typically
Gaussian—rather than deterministic points in the latent space.
This stochastic formulation introduces beneficial randomness
and serves as an implicit regularization mechanism, thereby
enhancing model generalization. Given that satellite sensor
data are time series in nature, employing LSTM as the
underlying network enables the model to capture temporal
dependencies and latent features more effectively. A twolayer LSTM architecture offers stronger nonlinear modeling
capacity compared to a single-layer network. However, deeper
architectures often yield diminishing performance gains while
increasing computational overhead. Thus, a two-layer structure
is adopted as a practical tradeoff.
VAE-LSTM is a fusion of VAE and LSTM. The VAE-LSTM
model integrates the temporal modeling capability of LSTM
with the probabilistic representation learning of VAE. This
combination enables effective capture of complex temporal
dependencies and latent distributions, making it particularly
suitable for detecting subtle, evolving, or early-stage anomalies
in time-series data. The encoder and the decoder of the VAE
are constructed using LSTM networks. The deep model utilizes both the divergence loss and reconstruction loss to jointly

D. Model Update Mechanism
This study designs three model update strategies to enhance
the adaptability and robustness of the model during long-term
spacecraft operation. The first is a periodic update strategy,
where the model is retrained at fixed time intervals using
all newly collected data, allowing it to continuously adapt to
changes in system status. The second is an event-triggered
update strategy. When the system receives newly labeled
boundary samples from domain experts, the model update is
immediately triggered to enhance its capability in recognizing
early anomalies. The third is an incremental update strategy.
When the system detects concept drift in the data, only the
global feature modeling component and the original data
modeling component are incrementally updated, while the
local feature detection component remains unchanged. This
ensures local stability while improving the overall adaptability
of the model.
In addition, this study proposes a method for the detection
and early warning of concept drift. In practical deployment, telemetry parameters with limited variation ranges are
first filtered out by engineers, allowing the system to focus
on dynamic parameters that are more susceptible to drift.
For these parameters, we continuously monitor the trend of
anomaly scores. If a sustained deviation in the anomaly score
is observed, concept drift is considered likely to have occurred,
and the model update mechanism is subsequently triggered.
All the above update strategies enable timely correction
of model parameters and dynamic adjustment of decision
thresholds, thereby allowing adaptive tuning of the decision
boundary and improving the models responsiveness to changes
in system behavior.
E. Deployment
The proposed anomaly detection algorithm is primarily
deployed at the ground control center to monitor and identify
early anomalies during satellite operations. At fixed time
intervals, the algorithm automatically retrieves specified key
spaceborne sensor data from the ground-based data reception
and processing system. Before being input into the model,
the retrieved data undergo a series of preprocessing steps to
improve data quality and enhance model robustness.
During the training phase, the anomaly detection framework supports the manual injection of boundary samples to
strengthen the models ability to perceive early faults. Once
trained, the model can automatically perform anomaly detection tasks, producing an anomaly label and a corresponding
anomaly score for each data segment. When a potential
anomaly is detected, the system immediately alerts operation
and control personnel to assist in further analysis and intervention.
In addition, the framework also supports deployment on
the satellite side to meet the real-time detection needs of
certain critical sensor parameters. In such cases, the model
can be trained on the ground and the updated parameters

IV. E VALUATION OF THE E XPERIMENT AND
A NALYSIS OF THE R ESULTS
The experimental data were obtained from an in-orbit satellite. Over six months of continuous operation, voltage and
current data from multiple channels were recorded by sensors
monitoring the atomic clocks operational status. The data were
sampled at 0.1 Hz. During in-orbit operation, several boundary
samples that fell below the alarm threshold were identified by
experts from the Chinese Space Agency. These samples serve
as critical empirical evidence supporting this study.
The dataset contains 1894 time-series segments, among
which 92 are labeled as anomalous samples. These annotations are based on engineering ground event logs and expert
manual inspections, ensuring a high level of reliability. In the
experimental setup, the first 300 normal segments are selected
as the training set, and another 300 normal segments are used
as the validation set. In addition, two rare boundary samples,
identified by engineering experts, are included. The remaining
data constitute the test set, which includes 1200 normal
segments and the aforementioned 92 anomalous segments.
The anomalous samples cover three major types: point
anomalies, contextual anomalies, and gradually evolving
anomalies. These types collectively represent a wide range
of abnormal behaviors that may occur during in-orbit satellite operations. Before being fed into the model, the raw
spaceborne sensor data undergo several preprocessing steps,
including data decoding, removal of transient outliers caused
by single-event upsets, and interpolation for estimating missing values, in order to improve data continuity and usability.

the impact of false positives on overall detection performance

B. Stability Analysis of the Multiscale Detection Score
Fusion Method
To ensure the reliability of the multiscale detection score
fusion results, it is necessary to analyze the stability of the
fusion (14). On the one hand, such analysis prevents the
output from diverging or exhibiting abnormal fluctuations
when the inputs remain within a reasonable range, thereby
avoiding misjudgments in anomaly detection. On the other
hand, demonstrating that the output variations remain bounded
under small input perturbations improves the overall robustness of the system, ensuring that the proposed fusion method
possesses practical applicability in real-world scenarios.
First, the input to the fusion formula is a bounded set
composed of detection scores from different time scales.
Accordingly, the input parameters are bounded. Since the
denominator D(θ) remains strictly positive within the interval θ ∈ [0, 1], inequality-based derivations confirm that the
output L(θ) is also bounded. This demonstrates that the
fusion function satisfies the bounded-input bounded-output
(BIBO) stability condition, implying that the fusion output
does not diverge when the input scores are confined to a finite
range, thereby preventing abnormal fluctuations during the
decision-making process.
Furthermore, in terms of system convergence, if the detection scores of the subdetectors converge, the limit of the fusion
output L necessarily exists due to the commutativity of limits
for continuous functions. This establishes that the overall
system exhibits asymptotic stability. From the perspective of
local robustness, algebraic simplification and upper bound
estimation of the partial derivatives of the fusion function
indicate that the function satisfies Lipschitz continuity on
a specific compact set. Consequently, small perturbations in
the input (e.g., those caused by significant variance across
time scales) lead to bounded variations in the output—this

The proposed anomaly detection framework adopts a
modular design, in which key components such as feature
augmentation, time-series modeling, and anomaly scoring are
decoupled. This allows flexible replacement and optimization
of submodules while maintaining the overall structural stability. Currently, time-series modeling is implemented using a
VAE-LSTM model. In extensive comparative experiments, this
model demonstrated outstanding performance in several key
metrics, including precision, recall, and F1-score, and was,
therefore, selected as the default modeling component in the
framework.
However, we also acknowledge that model selection based
solely on performance metrics typically relies on known
anomalous samples, which introduces certain limitations.
Specifically, a “best-performing model” selected based on
existing anomaly types may perform poorly when encountering previously unseen anomalies. This evaluation strategy
is essentially conducted under the closed-world assumption,
which may fail to fully reflect the model’s robustness and
generalization ability in real-world open environments.
To address this issue, we plan to explore more generalpurpose model evaluation mechanisms in the future, with the
aim of enhancing the reliability and practical guidance value
of the model in real-world applications.
D. Interpretability Analysis
The interpretability of deep learning methods has long been
a major challenge in practical applications. In the proposed
framework, feature engineering is applied before training the
deep learning model to extract a series of features with
clear physical meanings from the raw telemetry data. These
features capture the variation patterns and statistical properties of telemetry signals across different dimensions. This
domain knowledge-driven feature construction enhances the
interpretability of detection results, making the model outputs
easier for experts to understand and verify.
E. Computational Efficiency and Deployment Feasibility
Although the proposed model achieves promising performance in anomaly detection tasks, its computational efficiency
is also an important consideration. On a computer configured with an Intel i5-12400 CPU, 16 GB of RAM, and
an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Ti GPU, the model takes
approximately 85 s to complete inference on the entire dataset.
Compared with LSTM-based models, the inference time is
slightly longer; however, it demonstrates a significant advantage in computational efficiency over models based on GAN
or transformer architectures. This advantage becomes more
pronounced as the input sequence length increases.
Similar to other deep learning-based anomaly detection
methods, the computational cost of this model during training

F. Applicability and Limitations
The proposed method focuses on detecting early faults in
satellites, which is of great significance for enhancing mission
safety and extending satellite service life. The framework
adopts a multiscale parallel heterogeneous model along with
a corresponding anomaly scoring scheme, aiming to ensure
applicability beyond a single scenario. Our experimental
dataset includes point anomalies, contextual anomalies, and
gradually evolving anomalies. The results demonstrate that the
proposed method exhibits strong competitiveness under the
current experimental setting.
However, for more complex anomaly patterns (e.g.,
semantic anomalies), the discriminative ability of the existing framework may be limited in the absence of sufficient domain knowledge. For certain satellites with high
maneuverability—such as military reconnaissance satellites or
technology demonstration satellites—their sensor data may
exhibit significant nonstationarity and complex variations due
to frequent trajectory changes and dynamic task adjustments. In such cases, the framework may face performance
constraints.
In addition, mislabeled anomalies in the training data,
insufficient training samples, or a lack of awareness of abrupt
changes in mission conditions may result in biased modeling
and degraded detection performance.
VI. C ONCLUSION
During spacecraft operation and maintenance, a very small
number of boundary samples labeled manually by domain
experts exist. These samples cannot be detected by existing
anomaly detection methods. Such boundary samples lie near
the classification decision boundary and are highly similar
to both normal and anomalous data. This article proposes
an anomaly detection framework that constructs a highly
discriminative feature space using boundary samples. The
method significantly enhances the separability of normal and
anomalous samples in the feature distribution, thereby effectively reducing the learning difficulty of neural networks in
early anomaly detection tasks and improving classification
precision.
By introducing feature weighting factors to dynamically
fuse the outputs of multiscale parallel heterogeneous submodels, the framework achieves a dynamic balance across
different feature perspectives. This effectively mitigates the

overfitting risk caused by clustering of boundary samples. On
a real satellite sensor dataset, the proposed method achieves
excellent detection performance: a recall rate of 0.826, reflecting strong recognition of anomalous events; a precision of
0.938, indicating overall reliable judgment; and an F1-score
of 0.878, demonstrating a balanced tradeoff between precision
and recall. Compared with other state-of-the-art methods, the
proposed anomaly detection framework attains the best results.
The proposed anomaly detection model addresses specific engineering application scenarios. The validation data
were collected from spacecraft payloads experiencing gradual
degradation. Such data collection is challenging and requires
manual annotation by domain experts. More data are needed in
the future to further validate the model. The proposed method
has a clear underlying principle. As a general framework,
its deep learning model component can be replaced. Future
work will focus on improving evaluation mechanisms, exploring more robust and generalizable deep models, and further
strengthening the collaborative design between deep models
and anomaly detection strategies to enhance overall detection
performance and application reliability.
