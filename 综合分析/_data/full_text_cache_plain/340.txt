A Meta-Unified Global Cyber Threat Intelligence
Model for Industrial Cross-Cloud Networks
Deepika Saxena , Member, IEEE, and Ashutosh Kumar Singh, Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract—Industrial cross-cloud networks (ICCNs) combine
services from multiple providers to support critical operations, but this diversity also creates major security challenges.
Because each provider follows different protocols and safeguards,
responses to cyber threats are often inconsistent and delayed,
reducing the effectiveness of existing Cyber Threat Intelligence
(CTI) systems. To address this gap, we propose a three-layered
CTI framework that unifies learning and adaptation across
heterogeneous clouds. The first layer implements a two-tier
federated learning (FL) strategy, developing two global models:
MLP-SimiFed and MLP-Non-SimiFed, within each industry cloud
to manage scalability and heterogeneity. The second layer aggregates these models across distributed clouds into a Unified Global
Model, strengthening collaborative defense. The third layer
leverages transfer learning to produce the Meta Unified Global
Cyber Threat Intelligence (MUG-CTI) model, which enables swift
adaptation to emerging threats. Through test-bed simulation,
MUG-CTI demonstrates superior threat management, achieving
up to 20.7% and 43.06% higher accuracy, and reducing hamming
loss by up to 73.8% and 98.3% compared to Federated Learning
and SVM-driven methods, respectively.
Index Terms—Cross-clouds, cyber threat intelligence, cybersecurity, industry clouds, multi-dimensional federated learning.

A. Related Work
Recent advancements in CTI have introduced several models to secure industrial cloud environments. Yazdinejad et al.
[10] employ federated learning for privacy-preserving, clusterbased anomaly detection in Industrial Internet of Things
(IIoT), achieving high accuracy with minimal bandwidth.
Li et al. [11] combine federated learning with fog/edge computing to detect and mitigate zombie nodes in IIoT networks,
improving accuracy, reducing mitigation time, and increasing
the attack cost for adversaries. Liu et al. [12] propose a secure
and lightweight feature selection system (SeiFS) for machine
learning (ML) tasks, enabling efficient interactions between
multiple entities and a single server. Homayoun et al. [13]
introduce deep ransomware threat hunting and intelligence
system (DRTHIS) using long short-term memory (LSTM) and
convolutional neural network (CNN), achieving high accuracy
in identifying known and unseen ransomware families in fog
layer nodes. Mm et al. [14] propose a support vector machine
(SVM)-based distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) detection
algorithm with four phases: training, pre-processing, feature
selection, and classification. Gao et al. [15] present heterogeneous information network-based cyber threat intelligence
(HinCTI), a system using heterogeneous networks and graph
convolutional networks (GCN) to model CTI and identify
threat types based on infrastructure node similarities. Hu et al.
[16] propose Graph2vec+RF, which uses graph embedding and
Random Forest to enable early and accurate network intrusion
detection from initial network packets. Sundaramoorthy et al.
[17] propose a hybrid intrusion detection system (IDS) for
cloud computing and wireless sensor networks (WSN)s using
multi-layer perceptron (MLP), optimizing detection accuracy
and reducing false positives. A data-driven decision support
framework is designed in [18] to optimize cybersecurity forensic investigations by using a repository of known adversarial
tactics and threat intelligence to guide investigators in selecting
the most efficient next steps, thereby, reducing the duration and
cost of the investigation.
B. Research Gaps and Our Contributions
Despite significant advances in CTI research, several
challenges persist in securing Industrial Cross-Cloud Networks (ICCNs). Existing CTI and Federated Learning (FL)
approaches often address only a single dimension, such as
non-independent and non-identically distributed (non-IID) data
handling or privacy preservation, but lack holistic mechanisms
to simultaneously manage dynamic topologies, model stability,
and evolving threats. Consequently, threat responses are often

fragmented and delayed, limiting the capability of current
systems to provide unified, real-time protection in ICCNs.
The key limitations of existing research can be summarized
as follows:
1) Model-level adaptation: FL in ICCNs must handle
highly non-IID data and heterogeneous local infrastructures, which can lead to biased or unstable global models
[10], [11].
2) Threat-evolution adaptation: ICCNs encounter continuously emerging and polymorphic attacks, including
supply-chain compromises and AI-driven malware,
requiring rapid incorporation of new threat intelligence
into existing CTI models [15], [16].
3) Coverage of diverse threats: Many existing systems,
such as DRTHIS [13], DDoS detection methods [14],
and hybrid IDSs [17], focus on specific attack types and
fail to address the full spectrum of multi-vector threats
prevalent in ICCNs.
4) Handling heterogeneity and dynamism: CTI and FL
methods often struggle to adapt to the heterogeneous
and dynamically changing infrastructure of ICCNs [10],
[11], [15], [18].
5) Real-time adaptability: Current approaches generally
lack the capability to continuously update and respond to
rapidly evolving threats across distributed cloud infrastructures [10], [11].
To address the identified gaps, we introduce a multi-diverse
CTI model, conceptualized as a three-layered Meta-Unified
Global CTI (MUG-CTI) framework, as depicted in Fig. 2.
The first and second layers employ Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)-based heterogeneous Federated Learning (FL)
strategies, both similarity-driven and non-similarity-driven for
scalable, privacy-preserving collaboration within and across
industry clouds. The third layer integrates Transfer Learning to ensure real-time adaptability and knowledge transfer.
Collectively, this layered design provides a synchronized,
proactive, and globally unified defense mechanism configured
for industrial cross-cloud networks.The primary contributions
of this work are as follows:
1) Novel Meta-Unified CTI Architecture: We propose a
tri-layered Meta Unified Global Cyber Threat Intelligence (MUG-CTI) model, representing a first-of-its-kind
architecture that unifies threat detection, analysis, and
mitigation across heterogeneous industrial cross-cloud
networks. This design addresses the limitations of fragmented and delayed threat responses in existing systems.
2) Layer-wise Heterogeneous Federated Learning Strategy: The first layer introduces a novel two-tier FL
optimization that develops two distinct global models: MLP-SimiFed and MLP-Non-SimiFed, within each
industry cloud. This approach is unique in explicitly
handling non-IID data distributions and heterogeneous
infrastructures, providing stable and scalable localglobal model learning.
3) Unified Global CTI Aggregation Mechanism: The second layer presents a novel aggregation strategy that
combines multiple global models from distributed clouds
into a single Unified Global Model. This ensures a

II. MUG-CTI M ODEL
Consider a cross-cloud environment with Z geographically
distributed industry clouds {IC1 , IC2 , . . ., ICZ }, each cloud
maintains K cyber threat repositories {CT B1 ICi , CT B2 ICi ,
. . ., CT BK ICi }, where K ≥ 1 and i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , Z}.
These repositories are used to develop a comprehensive and
diverse CTI model for global cybersecurity in the Industrial Cross-Cloud Network (ICCN). As illustrated in Fig. 3,
the proposed multi-diverse CTI model is a three-layered
meta-unified global framework that leverages Multi-Layer

Perceptron (MLP)-based, two-tier heterogeneous Federated
Learning (FL) optimization techniques for real-time learning
and model adaptation.
In the first layer, two global models are developed at each
industry cloud platform using first-level heterogeneous FL
optimization: the MLP-SimiFed Global Model (SGM) and the
MLP-Non-SimiFed Global Model (N-SGM). The second layer
employs second-level heterogeneous FL optimization to build
a Unified Global CTI (UGM-CTI) model by aggregating
multiple MLP-SimiFed and MLP-Non-SimiFed global models
(developed in the first layer) in geographically distributed
industry clouds {IC1 , IC2 , . . ., ICZ }. In the third layer, the
diversified cyber threat intelligence from the Unified Global
CTI model is further enhanced using Transfer Learning to
learn new and advanced cyber threats, culminating in the
development of the Meta Unified Global Cyber Threat Intelligence (MUG-CTI) model. The operational workflow for each
of the three layers is detailed in the following subsections.

F. Interpretability of CTI Insights for Operational Forensics

CTI-driven defenses across distributed cloud environments.

vulnerable to adversarial manipulations such as data poisoning,
backdoor attacks, and model update manipulations. In the
proposed MUG-CTI model, robustness is formally analyzed
by considering the effect of adversarial updates A on the
aggregated global model parameters θ. Let θbenign denote the
aggregated model in the absence of adversaries and θadv the
parameters under adversarial influence. The degradation in
