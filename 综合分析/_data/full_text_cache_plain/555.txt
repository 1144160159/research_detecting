Temporal Network Embedding Enhanced
With Long-Range Dynamics and
Self-Supervised Learning
Zhizheng Wang , Yuanyuan Sun , Member, IEEE, Zhihao Yang , Senior Member, IEEE,
Liang Yang , Member, IEEE, and Hongfei Lin , Member, IEEE
Abstract— Temporal network embedding (TNE) has promoted
the research of knowledge discovery and reasoning on networks. It aims to embed vertices of temporal networks into a
low-dimensional vector space while preserving network structures and temporal properties. However, most existing methods
have limitations in capturing dynamics over long distances, which
makes it difficult to explore multihop topological associations
among vertices. To tackle this challenge, we propose LongTNE,
which learns the long-range dynamics of vertices to endow TNE
with the ability to capture high-order proximity (HP) of networks.
In LongTNE, we employ graph self-supervised learning (Graph
SSL) to optimize the establishment probability of deep links in
each network snapshot. We also present an accumulated forward
update (AFU) module to fathom global temporal evolution
among multiple network snapshots. The empirical results on six
temporal networks demonstrate that, in addition to achieving
state-of-the-art performance on network mining tasks, LongTNE
can be handily extended to existing TNE methods.
Index Terms— Graph self-supervised learning (Graph SSL),
long-range dynamics, temporal network embedding (TNE).

I. I NTRODUCTION
ITH the development of network representation learning (NRL), knowledge discovery and reasoning on networks have been active research for decades [1], [2], [3], [4],
[5], [6], [7], which is involved into social networks [8], [9],
road networks [10], spatial networks [11], and visual networks
[12], [13]. NRL aims to learn a mapping function L to project
vertex v into a d-dimensional vector. They typically discover
implicit knowledge through link prediction [14], [15]. Despite
the advanced NRL algorithms having achieved promising
performance in many network-mining scenarios, they are
confronted with the challenges of discovering long-range and
evolutionary knowledge from temporal networks.

Practically, early NRL methods focused on static networks [16], [17], [18], [19], [20] and encoded networks by
aggregating stable topology of vertices. However, networks
exhibit complex temporal and spatial properties in the real
world [21], so their topological structure will be constantly
evolving. As a result, temporal network embedding (TNE) is
proposed to capture dynamic features of networks [22], [23],
[24], [25], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30] in recent years. They
either simplify the evolution process as a series of network
snapshots or explore the evolution pattern of edges via the
temporal point process [31]. However, most TNE methods
only model the local topology through stochastic processes,
which indicates they emphasize learning short-range dynamics
of vertices. However, in temporal networks, vertices will gradually show similarities over long distances as time goes on.
Therefore, although TNE provides an effective way of learning the dynamic representation of networks, they still have
limitations in discovering deep knowledge from networks.
Naturally, the above challenges can be tackled by a scalable
TNE method that can simultaneously explore networks’ deep
and evolutionary patterns by modeling long-distance proximity. Among the existing TNEs, one of the illuminating studies
in preserving distance approximation is DynamicTriad [23].
As Fig. 1(a) shows, it modeled the triad closure process that
how a closed triad develops from an open triad. Essentially,
DynamicTriad aims to generate an edge for two unconnected vertices but with common neighborhoods, which is
an application of local structural evolution [32], [33], [34]

in encoding path information. Inspired by DynamicTriad,
we extend the triad closure process to long-path reachable
vertices, defined as long-range dynamic proximity in our study.
As shown in Fig. 1(b), two four-step (k = 4) reachable
vertices (e.g., vertices 3 and 9) and a group of internal vertices
(e.g., vertices 2, 6, 7, and 8) construct a pseudo-closed triad,
from which we can capture the four-hop dynamic features
between vertices 3 and 9.
However, applying the triad closure process to preserve
long-range dynamics of vertices may have the following
problems.
1) How do we discriminate topological roles of unconnected vertices? Methods based on local structure in
developing a triad closure considered all unobserved
links as negative samples and maximized the distance
between positive and negative samples. So, they failed
to distinguish the topological roles between long-path
dependent vertices and irrelevant vertices.
2) How do we simulate the triad closure process in temporal networks with the adaptive strategy? Generally,
the probability of generating links for two unconnected
vertices was estimated by static parameters derived from
the network topology. However, this probability should
be adjusted based on the distance among vertices in
path-dependent scenarios.
3) Which training manner should be selected to enable the
model scalability? Recent attention-based graph neural
networks [35], [36], [37] also attempted to improve the
triad closure process for exploring high-order proximity (HP). They were typically trained in a supervised
fashion and required a large number of annotated
data. However, due to potentially connected vertices
being uncertain and numerous in temporal networks,
it is impractical to keep track of newly emerging data
manually.
Self-supervised learning (SSL) provides a meaningful solution to address the above issues. Recently, SSL has achieved
promising improvement in learning graph representation [38],
[39], [40], [41], [42] via automatically exploring augmentations for unlabeled graph data. Therefore, combining SSL
with graph distance proximity, we propose LongTNE in this
work, a fine-tuning method that enhances temporal network
embedding by exploring the long-range dynamics of vertices.
First, LongTNE uses the graph traversal method to establish
the hierarchical context for vertices, distinguishing the longpath-dependent vertices from irrelevant vertices. Afterward,
LongTNE actively learns an adaptive weight matrix to assign
dynamic similarity weights to the long-path reachable vertices.
Next, LongTNE introduces the graph self-supervised learning
(Graph SSL) strategy to construct augmented training samples
from hierarchical topology and fine-tune the pretrained vertex
embeddings. Finally, LongTNE integrates an accumulated
forward update (AFU) module to acquire global temporal
evolution based on the assumption that a network will evolve
smoothly over time.
Collectively, several noteworthy contributions to our study
can be summarized as follows.
1) We study the effectiveness of long-range dynamics in
TNE and propose an extension algorithm LongTNE to

deduce deep and evolutionary knowledge from temporal
networks.
2) In LongTNE, we design an augmentation strategy for
unlabeled graph data, which combines Graph SSL and
deep network topology to fine-tune the pretrained vertex
embeddings.
3) We present a time-step partition schema to prepare
network snapshots for LongTNE and elucidate the
effectiveness of LongTNE with extensive empirical
experiments on large-scale temporal networks.
II. R ELATED W ORK
A. Temporal Network Embedding
Currently, the TNE methods can be categorized into
discrete-time TNE and continuous-time TNE. Dynamic network embedding (DNE) [22] is presented to encode temporal
networks under the assumption that a network will not change
much during a short time. Learning representations over
dynamic graphs (DyRep) [43] defined the temporal evolution over networks as an association and communication
process. Hawkes process based temporal network embedding (HTNE) [44] was a Hawkes process-based method that
modeled the neighborhood formation sequence in continuous
time. Dynamic co-embedding model for temporal attributed
networks (DCTANs) [29] learned temporal network representations by integrating network topology and vertex attributes.
Other methods [7], [45], [46], [47] emphasized learning the
dynamic features by combining the global or local topology
with the temporal formation of edges. In addition, with the
in-depth study of temporal evolution patterns, many neural
network models have also been applied to learn the representations of temporal networks [30], [48], [49], [50], [51].
B. High-Order Proximity Preservation
The HP of a network is usually computed by the kth
power of its normalized adjacency matrix or is approximated by a random walk [52]. To encode static networks,
learning graph representations with global structural information (GraRep) [53] modeled the high-order neighbors of
those vertices with small degrees. HP preserved embedding
(HOPE) [54] preserved the asymmetric transitivity of networks
by approximating HP. To encode temporal networks, dynamic
HP preserved embedding (DHPE) [25] adopted the generalized singular value decomposition (SVD) to preserve HP for
learning dynamic features of networks. High-order nonlinear
information preserving (HNIP) [55] proposed a deep-guided
autoencoder to capture the highly nonlinear structure while
preserving the temporal proximity of vertices. Furthermore,
methods such as hybrid-order representation learning network
(HORLN) [11] also explored the HP of spatial data in the
anomaly detection task.
C. Graph Self-Supervised Learning
Graph SSL indicates the learning paradigm where supervision signals (or pseudo-labels) are generated from graph
data. Some studies explore different graph augmentations to
learn representations of static networks [56] and be applied
to different fields [57], [58], [59]. Self-supervised graphlevel representation learning with local and global structure

(GraphLoG) [38] is a unified framework for self-supervised
whole-graph representation learning, which preserves the
local-instance and global-semantic structure of networks.
Graph adversarial self-supervised learning (GASSL) [39] automatically generated challenging negative samples by adding
perturbations to the input and were adversarially trained for
the encoder. Graph contrastive coding (GCC) [60] developed
a self-supervised GNN pretrained framework to capture the
universal network topological properties across multiple networks. Graph contrastive learning (GraphCL) [61] designed
four types of graph augmentations to incorporate various priors
and verified the robustness of the self-supervised training fashion. Cross reconstruction transformer (CRT) [42] introduced
SSL in time-series representation learning, which was tailored
to the cross-domain dropping-reconstruction task.

In this section, we give a brief preliminary knowledge of
TNE, HP, and Graph SSL.
Definition 1 (Temporal Network): A temporal network
consists of a sequence of timestamped edges, where each
edge is established at a certain time to connect two vertices.
Formally, a temporal network is defined as G T = (V, E, A, T )
that consists of vertex set V and edge set E ⊂ V × V. A
denotes the adjacency matrix and T denotes the temporal
sequence of edges. Given a G T , it can be divided into t
snapshots according to the temporal sequence T . The tth
snapshot G t = (Vt , Et , At ) contains its vertex set Vt , edge
set Et , and the adjacency matrix At .
Definition 2 (TNE): Given a set of network snapshots
{G 1 , . . . , G t }, TNE aims to learn a mapping function L : Vt →
R|Vt |×d to obtain a d-dimensional representation XtE for G t ,
where d ≪ |Vt |. A general learning framework for TNE is
written as follows [50]:

IV. M ETHODOLOGY
The detailed framework of LongTNE is illustrated in Fig. 2.
As sketched in Fig. 2(a), LongTNE tackles the problem
formulated in (4) through a two-step fine-tuning paradigm.
The embedding stream of LongTNE contains two parts:
One is the long-distance dynamic proximity preserved with
SSL (LDPP-SSL) that aims to learn the enhanced mapping
function L∗ . It contains the long-distance dynamic proximity
preservation (LDPP) module and the Graph SSL module. The
other is the AFU that is used as the global function g to capture
evolving regularities of the temporal network.
A. Long-Distance Dynamic Proximity Preservation
As described in Section I, distinguishing the topological
roles of vertices and adapting to the dynamic similarity among
vertices are two essential problems on the road to preserving
long-range dynamics. Therefore, we present LDPP as shown
in Fig. 2(b). It first constructs the hierarchical topology for
vertices and then learns an adaptive weight matrix within
long-path reachable vertices. At last, it employs a variation
of the HP function to preserve the long-range dynamics.
1) Hierarchical Topology Construction: Considering temporal networks are directed in most realistic scenarios,
we enforce direction constraints on edges to obtain the directed
network snapshot G tD = (Vt , Et7→ , A7t→ ). In G tD , the path
between two vertices is limited from the head vertex to the
tail vertex, compressing the storage memory of adjacency
matrix A7t→ by about 50%. After that, we apply the depth-first
search (DFS) on G tD to construct the hierarchical context T
(aka long-distance traversal tree) for each head vertex, which
can be denoted as follows:

inference task of exploring long-distance associated vertices j
for the given vertex r based on the network topology. Here,
we employ Mul-LP to evaluate the performance of LongTNE
in deep knowledge discovery.

given vertices based on the absolute difference in positions
between their corresponding embeddings. We sample 80%

contrastive views for two long-path reachable vertices, thereby

learned adaptive weights within positive samples to simulate

higher performance between two long-path reachable vertices.
Therefore, we can conclude that LongTNE has a solid ability
to discover multihop knowledge from temporal networks as it
explores the long-range proximity of vertices.

VI. D ISCUSSION
A. Selection of Parameter θ
LongTNE involves a hyperparameter θ = 5 in LDPP to
prepare training samples. Here, we provide the evidence for

the setting based on the experimental results in Fig. 9. We use
LongTNE(node2vec) to perform the Mul-LP and NTR tasks
under θ ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5, 7} and find that the results are improved
in all situations from θ = 3 to θ = 5 and are stable when
θ ≥ 5.
B. Selection of Dimension Size d
Based on different dimension sizes d = {32, 64, 128, 256},
we also conduct Mul-LP and NTR on RFA and FOOD,
where the parameter θ is fixed with 5. The experimental
results in Fig. 10 say that when d = 64, the performance
of LongTNE(node2vec) is the best.
C. Training Efficiency
In Algorithm 1, LongTNE is an enhancement framework
that aims to fine-tune the pretrained vertex embedding rather
than encoding networks from scratch. Therefore, the time
complexity of LongTNE is dependent on the optimization
process and can be computed as O(ndT |V||E 7→ |), where n
is the number of sampling vertices, d is the size of dimensions, and T is the number of network snapshots. |V| and
|E 7→ | denote the number of vertices and edges in each G D ,
respectively. We train LongTNE with different strategies five
times on different networks (only one time on TMALL) based
on vertex embeddings obtained by MTNE, and their average
training time is demonstrated in Table V.
VII. C ONCLUSION

SSL to explore long-range dynamics for TNE. The effectiveness and extension of LongTNE are verified through

LongTNE with tackling anomaly detection or action recognition tasks.
