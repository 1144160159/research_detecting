Abstract—Short video platforms like TikTok have dominated
global internet traffic. Despite the deployment of robust encryption protocols (e.g., TLS 1.3 and QUIC) to protect user
confidentiality, side-channel information leaking from encrypted
traffic remains a significant privacy threat. However, accurately
identifying specific video content is increasingly challenging due
to complex Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) algorithms, which homogenize traffic patterns, especially in high-motion viral content.
Existing methods relying on simple statistical features or singleview deep learning models often struggle to capture the finegrained fingerprints of specific videos under these conditions.
In this paper, we propose a novel Multi-View Fusion Network,
namely Rocket-Transformer Fusion (RTF-Net), that integrates
time-domain convolutional features with frequency-domain attention mechanisms. Our approach utilizes MiniRocket to extract
deterministic sequential patterns from traffic bursts, while simultaneously employing Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) combined
with TinyBERT to capture global periodic dependencies in the
frequency domain. We evaluate our method on a self-collected
dataset comprising 2,000 encrypted traffic traces generated from
20 most viral short dramas (each exceeding 100 million views)
on TikTok. Experimental results demonstrate that our method
achieves a state-of-the-art classification accuracy of 91.33% using
only the first 30 seconds of viewing traffic, significantly outperforming other baseline machine learning algorithms, showing that
side-channel leakage is indeed a threat to short video platforms.
To facilitate reproducibility and future research, we have opensourced our dataset and code online.
Index Terms—Encrypted Traffic Analysis, ide-Channel Attack,
Multi-View Fusion, Video Fingerprinting.

I. I NTRODUCTION
In recent years, the landscape of mobile Internet traffic has
undergone a paradigm shift, characterized by the explosive
growth of short-form video platforms. These applications,
offering bite-sized and algorithmically curated content, have
revolutionized digital media consumption and user engagement patterns globally. Among the myriad of platforms, TikTok has emerged as the undisputed market leader, fundamentally reshaping the social media ecosystem. The platform’s
influence is evidenced by its massive and rapidly expanding
user base; recent statistics indicate that TikTok has amassed
approximately 1.59 billion monthly active users worldwide [1].

This unprecedented scale not only highlights the platform’s
dominance but also implies that a vast amount of user interaction data is being transmitted across networks every second,
making the privacy and security of these encrypted traffic flows
a critical concern for billions of users.
As short-form video platforms become the primary application for billions of mobile consumer electronics users, the security of encrypted traffic flows has become a critical concern.
These devices often handle sensitive personal data, and the
side-channel leakage identified in this paper directly threatens
the digital privacy and security of the broader consumer base.
To safeguard user data during transmission, leading shortvideo platforms have universally adopted robust encryption
protocols, primarily the QUIC protocol layered over UDP and
TLS 1.3. These mechanisms ensure that the payload content
remains confidential, making direct decryption of plaintext
data computationally infeasible for third-party eavesdroppers.
However, while encryption effectively conceals the content, it
fails to mask the underlying traffic patterns. This gap exposes
a critical vulnerability known as the side-channel attack,
where adversaries can analyze unencrypted metadata—such
as packet sizes, inter-arrival times, and burst sequences—to
infer specific user activities. If an attacker can successfully
fingerprint the specific short video a user is watching, they
can reconstruct the user’s viewing history with high precision.
This leakage extends far beyond mere curiosity; it enables the
profiling of a user’s personal interests, political orientation,
and sexual identity. The consequences range from intrusive,
hyper-targeted advertising to severe physical security risks.
For instance, identifying videos related to specific landmarks,
local news, or neighborhood events could inadvertently reveal
a user’s real-world location or home address, thereby bridging
the gap between cyber surveillance and physical safety threats.
Despite the high potential payoff for attackers, executing
such fine-grained attacks within a specifically encrypted application is practically non-trivial. Existing research on encrypted
traffic analysis predominantly focuses on Website Fingerprinting (WF), Protocol Identification, Browser Fingerprinting and
so on [2]–[4]. In those scenarios, classifiers can rely on

all video contents are delivered via the same Content Delivery

packet headers and IP-based features virtually useless. Second,
and more critically, the widespread deployment of Adaptive

the traffic patterns. The ABR mechanism dynamically adjusts
video quality and chunk sizes based on real-time network
conditions, meaning that traffic traces generated by the same

Furthermore, the QUIC protocol employs padding mechanisms
to obfuscate packet sizes, and the short duration of viral
videos limits the amount of temporal information available for
analysis, leading to substantial feature overlap among different
videos.
To overcome these sophisticated obfuscation techniques, we
propose a novel Multi-View Fusion Network, namely RocketTransformer Fusion (RTF-Net), that synergizes the strengths
of discriminative feature engineering and deep representation
learning. Unlike previous works that rely solely on timedomain analysis, our framework treats encrypted traffic fingerprinting as a dual-modal problem. The first view focuses on the
Time Domain, employing MiniRocket [5], a state-of-the-art
time series transformation method. MiniRocket utilizes a vast
set of random convolutional kernels to extract deterministic,
high-resolution features from the burst sequences, effectively
capturing the localized ”shape” and ”texture” of traffic bursts
which remain consistent for specific video contents. Complementing this, the second view explores the Frequency Domain
to mitigate the stochastic noise introduced by ABR algorithms.
We apply Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to convert the raw
traffic series into its spectral representation, which is then
processed by TinyBERT [6]. The self-attention mechanism of
TinyBERT is adept at identifying global, long-range dependencies and periodic artifacts within the frequency spectrum that
are often invisible to local convolutions. By fusing these two
complementary views—local temporal precision and global
spectral robustness—our model can effectively penetrate the
noise of ABR and encryption padding, constructing a unique
and resilient fingerprint for each viral video.
To rigorously evaluate the proposed framework, we constructed a large-scale, real-world encrypted traffic dataset. We
developed an automated traffic collection system to simulate
realistic user viewing behaviors, capturing raw traffic traces
(PCAP) directly from the network interface. The dataset targets the most influential content on the platform; specifically,
we selected 20 viral short dramas from Douyin (the Chinese
version of TikTok), each of which has garnered over 100
million views. These high-profile videos represent the most
valuable targets for potential privacy attacks due to their massive audience reach. In the preprocessing phase, we filtered the
raw packets to isolate QUIC/TLS flows and aggregated them
into burst sequences, transforming unstructured binary data
into vectorized time-series representations suitable for deep
learning. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study
to investigate fine-grained specific video fingerprinting on
the TikTok platform. To foster reproducibility and encourage
further research in the community, we have made our full
dataset and source code publicly available on online.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as
follows:
• We propose the Multi-View Fusion Network, a novel
side-channel analysis framework specifically designed to
infer encrypted traffic of short videos under complex
ABR and QUIC obfuscation. By synergizing discriminative temporal features (via MiniRocket) with global
frequency-domain attention (via FFT and TinyBERT), our
method achieves a state-of-the-art accuracy of 91.33%,
demonstrating superior robustness compared to traditional
baselines.
• We constructed and released the first publicly available
encrypted traffic dataset tailored for the TikTok
platform. This dataset comprises 2,000 real-world traffic traces collected from 20 viral short dramas (each
with over 100 million views), addressing the scarcity of
benchmarks in short video fingerprinting and facilitating
future research in the community.
II. R ELATED W ORK
A. Side-Channel Attacks
Side-channel attacks (SCAs) exploit unintended information
leakage from system implementations. Recently, research has
shifted from network-level observations to microarchitectural
and AI-specific leakages. Mai et al. [7] leverages GPU-based
side-channel from the WebGL framework to successfully infer
to accurate geographical information of a victim who is browsing a Navigation App. Li et al. [8] proposes a mutual detection
and two-stage identification framework that leverages encrypted traffic analysis to autonomously detect and accurately
identify wireless IoT devices. Haghighi et al. [9] proposes a
novel covert malware attack that spreads through intelligent
transportation systems to stealthily manipulate adaptive cruise
control parameters, thereby engineering vehicle crash statistics, and introduces a non-disruptive anomaly-based intrusion
detection system that effectively detects such attacks using
real-world data. Maar et al. [12] demonstrated that kernel
defenses can be compromised via defense-amplified TLB sidechannel leaks, enabling stable kernel exploits. Similarly, Wang
et al. [14] introduced the Page Walk Side Channel (PWSC),
which leaks secrets even during invalid pointer dereferences
by exploiting speculative execution and prefetching. In the
context of AI, Yuan et al. [11] proposed CipherSteal, the first
framework to successfully recover visually identical input data
from TEE-shielded neural networks by exploiting ciphertext
side channels. These works highlight that side-channel threats
are becoming increasingly sophisticated and fine-grained.

B. Security in Collaborative and Algorithm Systems
Collaborative learning and outsourced computing have introduced new privacy dimensions. Yu et al. [13] proposed
a reinforcement-learning-based attack that deduces global
data distribution from shared model parameters in Federated
Learning (FL), proving that global models can leak collective
knowledge. To address model integrity, Alqattan et al. [15]
introduced the Model Discrepancy Score (MDS) to detect
adversarial updates in Hierarchical FL (HFL). In outsourced
computing, Luo et al. [16] developed a secure three-party
clustering scheme that effectively identifies malicious behavior
during data processing. Furthermore, Xiao et al. [17] showed
that lightweight hybrid obfuscation at the binary level can significantly reduce malware detection rates, emphasizing the ongoing arms race between attackers and defenders. Li et al. [18]
proposes TLD-SCA, a hybrid detection model that combines
Transformer-based global dependency modeling with LSTMbased local time-series dynamics against network-based sidechannel attacks. Ahmed et al. [10] proposes AutoSCA, an
automated deep learning side-channel analysis framework that
leverages Bayesian optimization to detect side-channel attacks
against 6G network. Ali et al. [19] proposes a differential
privacy method to secure Consumer Internet of Things (CIoT)
from possible leakage.
C. Summary and Discussion
In summary, while these recent studies have significantly
advanced our understanding of side-channel leakages in hardware [12], [14] , collaborative AI [13], [15] , and TEE
environments [11] , they primarily focus on static assets
or architectural flaws. Identifying specific, dynamic content
within encrypted short-video traffic—which is obfuscated by
both ABR and QUIC—remains an underexplored challenge
that requires the dual-modal temporal and spectral analysis
proposed in RTF-Net.
III. M ETHODOLOGY
A. Threat Model
In this study, we consider a passive, local adversary who
aims to infer the specific short video content being viewed by

a victim on the TikTok platform. We assume that all communication between the client and the server is protected by robust
encryption protocols (e.g., QUIC and TLS 1.3), preventing
the attacker from inspecting packet payloads. However, the
adversary can capture unencrypted side-channel information,
specifically the sequence of packet sizes, directions, and interarrival times. To demonstrate the ubiquity of this threat, we define three realistic attack scenarios: (1) Intermediate Routing
Nodes: The adversary controls network infrastructure (e.g.,
malicious Wi-Fi, ISPs) between the victim and TikTok CDNs.
(2) Local Area Network (LAN) Attacks: An attacker on the
same LAN (e.g., coffee shop) uses ARP spoofing to redirect
and capture victim traffic. (3) On-Device Parasitic Malware:
A background Android application with standard network
permissions captures packets via APIs like VpnService
without requiring root privileges.
This is particularly dangerous for the consumer electronics ecosystem, as malicious software can leverage standard
Android APIs to capture traffic patterns without requiring
elevated root privileges. This makes the attack highly feasible
in typical consumer environments, such as using a standard
smartphone in a public Wi-Fi area.
This threat model confirms that the proposed side-channel
attack does not rely on specialized hardware or restricted
network positions, making it highly feasible in diverse realworld environments.

C. Design Rationale
While fundamental components such as FFT and SelfAttention are widely used in general signal processing, their
direct application to encrypted traffic fingerprinting is nontrivial due to the complex noise introduced by Adaptive Bitrate
(ABR) algorithms and QUIC padding. The technical core of
RTF-Net lies in the strategic orchestration of these modules to
address two specific challenges:
1) Combating ABR Stochasticity: Standard time-domain
convolutions (e.g., 1D-CNN) struggle with the jittery
burst patterns of ABR. We introduce the FrequencyDomain View (via FFT and TinyBERT) specifically to
capture the global periodic rhythms of video buffering,
which remain invariant even when packet timing fluctuates.
2) Balancing Accuracy and Efficiency: To ensure deployability on consumer electronics, we avoid heavy, fully
learnable backbones. Instead, we utilize MiniRocket
(which uses fixed kernels for rapid shape extraction)
to handle high-dimensional time series, reserving the
computationally intensive Attention mechanism only for
the compact spectral features. The design of RTF-Net

algorithms. Second, the Frequency-Domain View (via FFT
and TinyBERT) acts as a noise-invariant anchor. While ABR

fine-grained identification in real-world consumer electronics
environments.
D. Data Preprocessing and Feature Engineering
Raw encrypted traffic, particularly over the QUIC protocol,
presents significant challenges for fingerprinting due to packet
padding and header encryption. Direct packet-level analysis
is often noisy and computationally expensive. To address
this, we implement a burst-level preprocessing pipeline. First,
raw PCAP traces are filtered to isolate UDP flows carrying
QUIC payloads. We then adopt a Burst Aggregation strategy,
where a burst is defined as a continuous sequence of packets
traveling in the same direction with inter-arrival times less
than a threshold τ = 0.5s. This aggregation is critical as
it reconstructs semantic application-layer units (e.g., video
slices) from fragmented transport-layer packets. By summing
packet sizes within a burst, we effectively neutralize the impact
of QUIC padding and align the traffic representation with the
logical download events driven by the Adaptive Bitrate (ABR)
algorithm, capturing the request-response behavior intrinsic to
video streaming.
For each aggregated burst bi , we extract a 4-dimensional
feature vector vi = [Si , Di , Ci , Ii ] to represent its spatiotemporal characteristics. Specifically, Signed Burst Size (Si ) captures the volume and direction of data (positive for downloads,
negative for requests), helping to identify heavy I-frames;
Burst Duration (Di ) and Packet Count (Ci ) jointly reflect

bandwidth availability and transmission congestion, providing
resilience against network jitter; and Inter-Burst Time (Ii )
acts as the ”heartbeat” of the player’s buffer management,
serving as a unique temporal signature of the video’s encoding
structure. The resulting bursts form a sequence X which is
fixed to a length of L = 1024 via truncation or zero-padding.
Finally, to handle the high dynamic range of traffic values,
we apply a RobustScaler to normalize the features, ensuring
stability during the subsequent deep learning training.

F. Adaptive Fusion and Optimization Strategy
The final stage of RTF-Net involves fusing the dual-view
representations and optimizing the network using a specialized
loss function designed for encrypted traffic analysis.

2) Hybrid Objective Function: Focal Loss with Flooding:
Training deep models on encrypted traffic datasets faces
two specific challenges: (1) Sample Hardness Imbalance:
Some video pairs exhibit distinct bitrates and are easy to
classify, while others (e.g., videos of the same genre) generate
highly similar burst patterns, acting as ”hard samples.” (2)
Overfitting to Noise: With a limited dataset size (e.g., 2,000
traces), models tend to memorize specific network jitter or
TCP retransmission noise, driving the training loss to zero
but hurting generalization. To address these, we propose a
hybrid objective function combining Focal Loss [20] and
Flooding [21].
1. Hard Mining via Focal Loss: Standard Cross-Entropy
(CE) loss treats all samples equally. However, easily classified
samples often dominate the gradient, preventing the model
from learning subtle differences in hard samples. We adopt
Focal Loss (FL) to dynamically down-weight easy examples.
Let pt be the model’s estimated probability for the groundtruth class. The Focal Loss is defined as:

• When a sample is misclassified (pt → 0), the modulating
factor (1 − pt )γ → 1, and the loss remains unaffected.
• When a sample is correctly classified with high confidence (pt → 1), the factor (1−pt )γ → 0, down-weighting
the loss.
In our experiments, we set γ = 2.0. This forces the RTF-Net
to focus its learning capacity on the ”hard” video traces that
are easily confused due to ABR similarities.
2. Generalization via Flooding: To prevent the model from
overfitting to trivial network noise (which would result in a
training loss of zero), we implement the Flooding strategy.
We define a flood level b (a small constant, set to 0.40). The
final objective function Ltotal is:
Ltotal = |LF L − b| + b

(11)

The mechanism works as follows:
• When LF L > b, the gradient flows normally, minimizing
the loss.
• When LF L < b (i.e., the model becomes too confident),
the term |LF L − b| causes the gradient direction to flip
(gradient ascent), pushing the loss back up towards b.

E. Sensitivity Analysis
To assess the robustness of RTF-Net, we conducted ablation
studies on key hyperparameters and design choices. The results
are summarized in Table V.
1. Burst Threshold (τ ) and Sequence Length (L). The
burst aggregation threshold τ is critical for semantic traffic
representation. We found that τ = 0.5s yields the best
performance (91.33%). Lower thresholds (τ = 0.1s) tend to
fragment meaningful video chunks, while higher thresholds
(τ = 1.0s) merge distinct request cycles, both degrading
accuracy. Similarly, a sequence length of L = 1024 (approx.
30s) is optimal; reducing the input length to L = 512 or
L = 256 resulted in accuracy drops of 1.00% and 0.50%,
respectively, indicating that capturing sufficient temporal context is beneficial.
2. Spectral Transform and Input Types. We compared
FFT with Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Short-Time
Fourier Transform (STFT). FFT (Magnitude) outperformed
DCT (-1.16%) and STFT (-1.66%). This suggests that the
magnitude spectrum of FFT provides the most robust shiftinvariant features against network jitter. Regarding spectral
inputs, Magnitude proved superior to Phase and Complex
representations. Notably, even with suboptimal spectral inputs,
the model maintains high accuracy (> 90%), demonstrating
that the Time-Domain branch (MiniRocket) effectively complements the frequency view, ensuring the framework’s overall
robustness.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we identified and validated a severe privacy
vulnerability in modern short-video platforms, demonstrating
that encrypted traffic analysis can accurately infer specific
video content despite the use of advanced protocols like
QUIC and complex Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) mechanisms. To
exploit this side channel effectively, we proposed RTF-Net,
a lightweight Rocket-Transformer Fusion Network. By synergizing deterministic local shape features (via MiniRocket)
with global spectral dependencies (via FFT and TinyBERT),
RTF-Net overcomes the stochastic noise inherent in dynamic
streaming.
Extensive experiments on a real-world dataset of 2,000
traces collected from viral TikTok videos demonstrate that
our approach achieves a state-of-the-art identification accuracy
of 91.33%, significantly outperforming 13 baseline methods
including traditional machine learning classifiers and standard
deep learning models. To the best of our knowledge, this is
the first study to successfully perform fine-grained specific
