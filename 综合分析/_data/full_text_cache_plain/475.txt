Learning Decision Boundaries for Multidimensional
Anomaly Detection
Xinye Wang , Student Member, IEEE, Lei Duan , Lili Guan , Jiaxuan Xu , and Chengxin He
Abstract— In this article, we attempt to study a problem
of identifying outliers from multiple dimensions, terming it as
multidimensional anomaly detection. This task assumes that
each sample corresponds to heterogeneous discriminative spaces,
where each space characterizes distinct semantic information
along one dimension. Consequently, the abnormalities exhibited by a sample will vary under these semantically different
dimensions. In contrast to traditional anomaly detection, multidimensional anomaly detection offers a more holistic assessment
of a sample’s abnormalities. However, the heterogeneity of
discriminative spaces leads to incomparability of the outputs
from different dimensions which is the major difficulty in designing multidimensional anomaly detection methods. This article
introduces a novel model, maximum margin multidimensional
anomaly detection (ALOE), specifically tailored for multidimensional anomaly detection. ALOE constructs a convex optimization
problem with nonlinear constraints. The primary objective is
to simultaneously learn multiple decision boundaries, utilizing
the maximum margin principle and covariance regularization,
while distinguishing between outliers and normal samples under
multiple dimensions by capturing the correlation among multiple
dimensions. To obtain the optimal decision boundary under each
dimension, we devise an alternating optimization method for
this convex optimization problem. To validate the effectiveness
of ALOE, we conduct extensive experiments on 12 real-world
datasets, comparing its performance against 34 anomaly detection
methods. The experimental results demonstrate the superior
performance of ALOE.
Index Terms— Decision boundary, heterogeneous discriminative spaces, multidimensional anomaly detection.

The intuitive strategy for tackling the above issue involves
breaking multidimensional anomaly detection down into multiple standard anomaly detection tasks and then applying
standard anomaly detection methods to identify outliers.
However, this strategy neglects the interdependencies among
multiple dimensions, potentially undermining the generalization performance of the anomaly detection methods.
To address this issue, this article introduces a novel model,
termed maximum margin multidimensional anomaly detection
(ALOE). Specifically, by leveraging the effectiveness of the
maximum margin technique, ALOE maximizes the geometric
margin between the decision boundary and the origin under
each individual discriminative space, aiming to resolve the
incompatibility issue arising from the modeling outputs of various dimensions. Furthermore, ALOE integrates a covariance
regularization term into the maximum margin objective function, enabling it to capture the dependencies across different
dimensions. Guided by this dual optimization objective, ALOE
formulates a convex objective function subject to nonlinear
constraints, which can be solved using alternating optimization
with quadratic programming or closed-form solution in either
alternating step.
The main contributions of this article are as follows.
1) We make the first attempt to study the multidimensional
anomaly detection, and propose a novel model ALOE
based on the maximum margin technique and covariance
regularization for this task.
2) ALOE captures the relationships among multiple dimensions, which in turn improves the accuracy of identifying
outliers in different dimensions, and also enhances the
ALOE’s generalization performance.
3) Extensive experiments are conducted by comparing
ALOE with 34 anomaly detection methods on 12 realworld datasets for validating the effectiveness of ALOE.

II. R ELATED W ORK
A. One-Class-Based Anomaly Detection Methods
A comprehensive review of the rich literature on anomaly
detection is clearly beyond the capacity of this article, so we
propose one-class-based anomaly detection methods that are
most relevant to this article. The rest of the popular methods
are thoroughly described in a dedicated chapter in outlier
analysis [5] and reviews of anomaly detection [6], [7]. Oneclass-based anomaly detection methods strive to learn a
decision boundary that can enclose normal samples as much
as possible. This category of anomaly detection methods can
be further delineated into shallow and deep one-class anomaly
detection methods.

15269

1) Shallow One-Class: Schölkopf et al. [8] first proposed
one-class support vector machine (OCSVM) which is inspired
by the traditional SVM applied to detecting outliers from
a dataset. Its objective is to learn an optimal hyperplane
by maximizing its distance from the origin and minimizing
its distance from the instance. Based on OCSVM, several
studies have been proposed to improve the performance of
the anomaly detector. Specifically, Song et al. [9] proposed
one-class conditional random fields for sequential anomaly
detection. This method can learn from the one-class dataset,
and capture the temporal dependence structure. Swarnkar and
Hubballi [10] proposed one-class naive bayes classifier for
payload-based anomaly detection. Tian et al. [11] introduced
ramp loss function into OCSVM to improve the robustness of
OCSVM. Gautam et al. [12] combined multi-kernel learning
with OCSVM, and proposed localized multiple kernel learning one-class anomaly detection method for improving the
accuracy of outlier detection. Miao et al. [13] formulated a distributed online OCSVM for anomaly detection over networks.
He et al. [14] proposed an optimal OCSVM framework for
multivariate anomaly detection in telemetry.
All the above studies are carried out on OCSVM. As the
research progresses, Tax and Duin [15] transformed OCSVM
into another form named support vector data description
(SVDD), whose purpose is to learn a minimal hypersphere that encloses as many normal samples as possible.
Then, to improve the performance of SVDD, Pauwels and
Ambekar [16] reanalyzed the cost function of SVDD to get
a better insight into the characteristics of the solution, and
introduced two new definitions of slack variables to show
that one of the proposed methods behaves more robustly
with respect to outliers. Wang and Cherian [17] used subspaces to label data via optimizing for two objectives jointly.
Wang et al. [18] proposed an efficient adaptive multi-kernel
SVDD method for outlier detection, which automatically
learns the optimal weight for each training instance under
different kernels using the nonnegative function. Wu and Li
[19] presented a novel method for weakly supervised anomaly
detection, where a limited number of labeled normal samples
and a sufficient number of unlabeled samples are available for
modeling.
2) Deep One-Class: With the popularity of deep learning, researchers have begun to further expand one-class
anomaly detection into deep one-class anomaly detection.
Ruff et al. [20] converted SVDD to deep via neural networks
and proposed DeepSVDD to compensate for the dearth of
deep learning methods for outlier detection. Erfani et al. [21]
presented a hybrid model where an unsupervised deep belief
network is trained to extract generic underlying features, and
OCSVM is trained from the features learned by the deep
belief network. Shen et al. [22] proposed the temporal hierarchical one-class network, a temporal one-class classification
model for time series anomaly detection. Goyal et al. [23]
designed deep robust support vector data description that is
applicable to most standard domains without requiring any
side-information. Chen et al. [24] introduced the interpolated
Gaussian descriptor method that learns an one-class Gaussian anomaly classified trained with adversarial interpolated

training samples. Maziarka et al. [25] designed a flow-based
one-class detector that finds a minimal volume bounding
region for enclosing normal data. Massoli et al. [26] presented
a novel framework, named multilayer one-class classification,
to train and test deep learning models on the anomaly detection

The above anomaly detection methods are used to deal with
single outlying score or binary state and it is difficult to deal
with multidimensional anomaly detection, due to the fact that
correlations between different dimensions are not considered.
B. Multidimensional Classification
Multidimensional anomaly detection does not refer to multiple dimensions of a sample’s features, but rather to a set
of states for the sample obtained from multiple dimensions.
Currently, there is little research on multidimensional anomaly
detection. Therefore, we briefly introduce its similar task multidimensional classification. Multidimensional classification is
a new multioutput learning paradigm, where each sample in
this framework is annotated with labels from multiple semantic
class spaces [32].
For multidimensional classification, Read et al. [33] presented a new method based on multilabel research for
multidimensional classification. Wang et al. [34] presented
a novel neural network-based model to integrate the label
embedding and feature augmentation techniques to learn label
correlations. Jia and Zhang [35] attempted to feature manipulation in input space for multidimensional classification,
and designed a simple yet effective approach to enrich the
original feature space with augmented features. Then, they
also proposed another two methods [36], [37] for multidimensional classification. Huang et al. [38] attempted to
deal with the multidimensional classification problem under
the semi-supervised learning setting, so that they developed
a novel multidimensional classification approach to solve
the resulting semi-supervised multidimensional classification
problem. Jia and Zhang [32], [39] designed a new classification framework to investigate which models objects with
rich semantics by encompassing heterogeneous label spaces
and multilabel annotations. Liu et al. [40] simultaneously conducted label space fusion and considered the comprehensive
label correlations to fully exploit the latent discriminative
information hidden in the output space.

C. Multiclass Anomaly Detection
Multiclass anomaly detection aims to train an anomaly
detector based on multiple classes’ samples to detect outliers
in all classes.
For this task, You et al. [41] presented a unified framework
to accomplish anomaly detection for multiple classes. Based
on the probabilistic framework, serval studies have been
proposed. Specifically, Lu et al. [42] proposed a hierarchical vector quantized prototype-oriented Transformer. Wang
Tavakkoli [43] combined the reconstruction-based structure
with the latent space denoising diffusion probabilistic model.
Li et al. [44] designed a vague prototype-oriented diffusion
model which can extract fundamental information from the
condition for preventing the same shortcut from the input
layer. Based on the similar strategy, He et al. [45] developed a framework consisting of a pixel-space autoencoder,
a latent-space semantic-guided network, and a feature-space
pretrained feature extractor. Liu and Wang [46] designed
a model based on the unified mixed-attention autoencoder
to implement multiclass anomaly detection. Wu et al. [47]
represented a novel iterative memory review network that
selects prior reconstruction information and strengthening
the interactivity between network modules. Kang et al. [48]
employed an encoder–decoder architecture, and designed a
masked subspace-like transformer for multiclass anomaly
detection.
We further analyze the difference among anomaly detection,
multiclass anomaly detection, and multidimensional anomaly
detection. Specifically, anomaly detection attempts to train an
anomaly detector that can detect outliers from a dataset that
are significantly different from the vast majority of samples.
Then, multiclass anomaly detection aims to train a generic
anomaly detector that can simultaneously detect outliers from
multiple classes that differ from the vast majority of samples in the corresponding class. In contrast, multidimensional
anomaly detection tries to train an anomaly detector that
can simultaneously detect outliers that differ from the vast
majority of samples under different dimensions from the same
dataset.

The optimization objective is a convex function with the
following preferred properties. Specifically, a convex function
will not have a function value between any two points in
the domain of definition that is lower than the value on
the line connecting these two points. This property ensures
that the function has only one optimal solution globally, and
there will be no local optimal solution interfering with the
solution of the global optimal solution. This is why we use
the alternating optimization with quadratic programming or
closed-form solution in either alternating step to obtain the
global optimal solution. The quadratic programming solution
algorithm is used when we solve for W and ρ. This algorithm
has been shown to converge. When we solve C, this article also
shows that the solution has a lower bound and the convergence
of the optimization algorithm. Thus, the solution algorithm is
guaranteed to be convergent regardless of the order.

IV. E XPERIMENTS AND R ESULTS
We conduct experiments on a set of 12 real-world datasets,
aiming to demonstrate the superior performance of ALOE.
All experiments are conducted on a PC with AMD 3990X 64Core Processor 2.90 GHz CPU, and 256 GB memory, running

A. Experimental Settings
1) Datasets: We select 12 multidimensional datasets and
reconstruct them to ensure applicability to our task. Specifically, Edm [49] is a dataset for the task of predicting
control operations during electrical discharge machining process, where the two dimensions correspond to two controlling
variables gap and flow, respectively. Each control variable
has three actions including increase the control variable,
no action, and decrease the control variable. In multidimensional anomaly detection, for each control variable, we define
the increase the control variable as normal samples, and no
action and decrease the control variable are defined as outliers.
Jura [50] is a dataset for the task of predicting the concentration of several metal pollutants in a region of the Swiss Jura.
The two dimensions correspond to land and rock, respectively.
In multidimensional anomaly detection, for each dimension,
we define rocks containing the primary variable cadmium
as normal samples and rocks containing the secondary variables nickel and zinc as outliers. Enb [51] is a dataset for
predicting some building parameters of residential buildings.
The two dimensions correspond to overall height and glazing
area, respectively. For multidimensional anomaly detection,
in overall height, we define heights of 3.5 as normal samples,
and heights of 7 as outliers. In glazing area, we define those
with glazing as normal samples and those without glazing as
outliers. Flare12 is a dataset for predicting the number of times
certain types of solar flare occurred within 24 h period, where
the three dimensions correspond to common, moderate, and
severe solar flares, respectively. In multidimensional anomaly
detection, among three types of solar flares, we define no solar
flare occurrence as normal samples and the others as outliers.
WQanimals and WQplants [2] are two datasets for predicting
animal and plant species in Slovenian rivers, respectively. Each
of them has seven dimensions correspond to relative representation of different species. For multidimensional anomaly
detection, in each species, we define a sampling site in the
river is normal when the species is present in the river, and
an outlier if the species is not present in this river. Voice
[52] is a dataset for predicting some characteristics of a piece
of human voice from different dimensions containing mean
frequency range and speaker’s gender. For multidimensional
anomaly detection, in the first dimension, we define the

the training phase, resulting in a more precise optimal boundary learned for each dimension.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this article, we attempt to study the multidimensional
anomaly detection for the first time. For a given dataset,
this task aims to identify outliers that significantly deviate
from normal distribution in different dimensions with specific semantic information. However, the heterogeneity of the
dimension poses a severe challenge for this specific anomaly
detection, as it results in the incomparability of modeling
outputs across different dimensions. This article introduces
maximum margin technique and covariance regularization
for multidimensional anomaly detection and proposes ALOE.
ALOE constructs a convex optimization problem incorporating
nonlinear constraints. The objective function is to learn a decision boundary that can distinguish between normal samples
and outliers in the same dimension through the maximum

margin, and to capture the relationships among different
dimensions through covariance regularization. To obtain the
optimal decision boundary under each dimension, we design
the alternating optimization method for this convex optimization problem. In the future, we try to explore multidimensional
anomaly detection under multiple abnormal types.
