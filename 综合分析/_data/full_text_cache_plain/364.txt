ANEF: Adversarial Neural Encryption Framework
for Secured Consumer Electronics in Smart Cities
Faeiz Alserhani , Kamran Ahmad Awan , Member, IEEE, Amjad Alsirhani ,
Nabil Almashfi , and Korhan Cengiz , Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract— Secure communication in resource-constrained
Internet of Things (IoT) deployments—such as consumer
electronics and smart city infrastructures—faces persistent challenges from both limited processing capacity and increasingly
adaptive cyber threats. This work introduces the Adversarial
Neural Encryption Framework (ANEF), which models encryption as a non-invertible mapping trained through an adversarial
three-agent setup comprising an encoder (Alice), a decoder (Bob),
and an adversary (Eve). The framework integrates entropyregularized encryption, dynamic key scheduling with periodic
reseeding, and quantization-aware optimization to support 8-bit
inference on constrained hardware. Adversarial resistance is reinforced through residual masking and curvature-based penalties,
improving robustness against adaptive attacks. Training is guided
by a hybrid objective that balances reconstruction fidelity, orthogonality preservation, and adversarial minimization, applied over
sequential encryption states. Experiments using the UNSW-NB15
dataset evaluate accuracy, communication overhead, and adversary success rate across diverse packet sizes. ANEF achieves
94.7% decryption accuracy with a 10.3% transmission overhead,
while maintaining a 4.1% adversary success rate.
Index Terms— Secure transmission, Internet of Things, consumer electronics, cyber threats, trustworthy, smart cities.

A. Related Work
Studies addressing security in heterogeneous IoT environments explore classical encryption, federated learning,
and privacy-preserving computation. Attribute-based encryption with user revocation enables fine-grained access in
cloud-assisted IoT [4], while fully homomorphic encryption
supports secure federated learning [5]. Elliptic curve–based
key exchange with logistic regression improves integrity validation [6]. Network-layer perturbation obfuscates message
semantics [7], and blockchain–SDN hybrids detect volumetric
attacks using neural classifiers [8]. Graph neural networks
over blockchain further enhance authenticated transaction
control [9]. Resource coordination models emphasize scalability and adaptive security in IoT communication. Lightweight
authentication and key agreement schemes address latencysensitive industrial scenarios [10], while hierarchical access
control supports constrained home automation devices [11].
Federated learning with quantum-safe primitives enables
dynamic resource allocation [12]. Energy-aware routing with
aerial relays improves secrecy capacity [13], and data aggregation frameworks integrating dissemination control and
real-time validation enhance secure wireless sensor operations in dense networks [14]. Table I provides a comparative
overview of key capabilities addressed across existing and
proposed approaches.

IoT and consumer electronics deployments [17]. These devices

C. Overview of the ANEF Framework
The framework introduces an encryption model based
on adversarial neural optimization, comprising three jointly
trained networks under a minimax formulation. The encoder
applies a learned nonlinear transformation to sensor data,
initialized from a shared seed. A symmetric decoder reconstructs the original message, while an adversarial network
approximates unauthorized decryption attempts. Training maximizes the performance gap between legitimate and adversarial
reconstruction to preserve communication integrity. To reduce
passive inference risk, decoy packets statistically consistent
with ciphertext are inserted into transmissions. The system
supports quantized, low-latency inference for constrained IoT
and consumer electronics devices, operating without iterative
decoding or continuous key exchange to maintain confidentiality and computational efficiency in operational deployments.
The main contributions of the proposed approach are
summarized as below:
1) An adversarial encryption–decryption framework in
which the cryptographic mapping is learned from data
rather than defined by fixed rule.
2) A generative module that produces realistic IoT traffic
patterns to accompany encrypted messages.
3) A deployment-ready pipeline capable of performing
encryption and decryption in a single forward pass with
low latency, designed for low-power IoT and consumer
electronics hardware.

The approach starts with a co-trained neural encryption
network that encodes plaintext sensor input via a nonlinear
transformation guided by a shared initialization key. The
transformation is learned adversarially through competition
between the encoder and a decoding network that simulates
an unauthorized interceptor. In parallel, a decoder is trained
to reconstruct the original signal with high fidelity, enabling
reliable decryption for legitimate receivers in consumerelectronics-rich IoT environments. The adversarial constraint
keeps the encoded representation intelligible only to the authorized decoder while opaque to external inference. As shown in
Figure 1, all components are quantized post-training to ensure
inference efficiency on constrained consumer electronics.
Following encryption, the system introduces synthetic decoy
packets that statistically mimic genuine ciphertext sequences
but carry no semantic payload. These decoys, generated by a
secondary GAN unit, are randomly inserted into the outgoing
data stream to mitigate passive adversaries. The receiver

III. S IMULATION AND P ERFORMANCE E VALUATION
The simulations were executed using Python 3.10 and
PyTorch 2.1 on an Ubuntu 22.04 environment (see Table II).
For validation, we used the publicly available UNSW-NB15
dataset [19], which includes network traffic and labeled attack
vectors relevant to IoT security scenarios. The proposed
framework was benchmarked against recent secure learning
and encryption models, including ECCLR [6], iPET [7],
FHE-FL [5], UAVSC [13], SIADS [14], PABE-UR [4], DBSANN [8], GTxChain [9], EKAAP [10], SKAA [11], and
DRAQ [12].
A. Encryption Time Efficiency (ETE)
The Encryption Time Efficiency (ETE) quantifies the time
required to encrypt a single data packet within the proposed framework, emphasizing its applicability to real-time
IoT systems. ETE evaluation includes six scenarios—varying
packet sizes, network congestion levels, device counts, key
lengths, quantized deployments, and the presence or absence
of decoy generation. The outcomes for the six scenarios are
summarized in Figure 2. In Scenario 1, the ANEF approach
demonstrated superior time efficiency across both small and
large packet sizes, recording 10.5 ms for 100 bytes and 15.8 ms
for 1500 bytes. In contrast, ECCLR and iPET recorded
12.2 ms and 14.8 ms (100 bytes), and 18.4 ms and 21.7 ms
(1500 bytes), respectively. FHE-FL followed with 11.0 ms and
17.3 ms. The performance gap widened in larger payloads,
where ANEF retained a consistent edge of 2.6–5.9 ms over
DRAQ and SKAA.
Under congested conditions in Scenario 2, ANEF again
maintained the lowest encryption time at 13.2 ms. Competing
models such as SIADS and iPET showed increased latency,
rising to 19.8 ms and 19.5 ms, respectively. Similarly, under
low and high device densities (Scenario 3), ANEF recorded
10.9 ms (50 devices) and 23.6 ms (500 devices), outperforming DBS-ANN and GTxChain by margins of 7.6 ms and
8.9 ms, respectively. Scenario 4 showed performance under

authorized decoder in reconstructing the original plaintext
from ciphertext. This evaluation includes six scenarios—
packet size variation, network congestion, device scalability,
decoy presence, quantized weight deployment, and key size
differences. The decryption accuracy (DA) assessment presented in Figure 3 demonstrates consistent performance of
the proposed ANEF model across all defined scenarios. For
minimal payload sizes (100 bytes), ANEF reached 98.2%,
outperforming ECCLR (94.5%) and iPET (92.3%). Even at
maximum packet sizes (1500 bytes), ANEF retained a high
accuracy of 97.4%, where competitors like FHE-FL and
SIADS dropped to 94.3% and 89.8%, respectively.

exhibited exceptional performance scalability. At 50 devices,
its accuracy peaked at 99.0%, maintaining a lead over
DBS-ANN (94.4%) and EKAAP (94.1%). When scaled
to 500 devices, ANEF held 94.7%, significantly ahead of
DRAQ (85.6%) and SKAA (87.5%). With decoy insertion,

V. C ONCLUSION
This research presents a comprehensive framework for
secure data transmission in IoT systems and consumer electronics environments, addressing confidentiality, cyber threat
resilience, and system efficiency. The proposed Adversarial
Neural Encryption Framework (ANEF) integrates advanced
adversarial training, decoy generation, and quantization techniques. The novelty of the approach lies in its ability to
adaptively balance encryption efficiency and security, making
it highly suitable for resource-constrained IoT and consumer
electronics devices requiring secure communication. Practical
implications include reduced transmission overhead, enhanced
resistance to cyber threats, and improved decryption accuracy.
Experimental results show that ANEF outperforms state-ofthe-art models like ECCLR and iPET by reducing encryption
time by 20%, improving decryption accuracy by 5%, and
achieving a 30% lower Eve success rate. Future work will
explore the scalability of ANEF in large-scale consumer
electronics IoT networks with real-time traffic and evolving
cyber threat conditions.
