KD-BERT: A Lightweight Knowledge Distillation
Bidirectional Encoder Representations from
Transformers for IoT Network
Intrusion Detection
Zhong Cao , Xiaohua Liu, Zheng Zhou , Lei Ding, and Wenli Shang

Abstract—The Internet of Things (IoT) network intrusion
detection (NID) has emerged as a powerful technique
for effectively detecting attacks and protecting internet
safety across a wide range of applications. However,
existing NID models often struggle to achieve good detection performance when deployed in resource-restricted
environments. To achieve an optimal balance between detection performance and computational cost, we propose
a lightweight knowledge distillation bidirectional encoder
representations from Transformers (KD-BERT) framework
for efficient IoT intrusion detection. Specifically, we first
develop a header-payload pairs tokenization method to
preprocess the raw data. Then, we distill rich knowledge
from a large pre-trained BERTteacher model into a smaller
BERTstudent model with fewer training parameters. By leveraging KD-BERT, the BERTteacher can be compressed into the
BERTstudent , reducing the number of parameters from 135 to
9 M, while maintaining high detection accuracy. To effectively obtain the essential features, we design a data transformation strategy and fine-tuned the BERTstudent model on
several labeled IoT NID tasks. Comprehensive experimental
results on three public datasets (telemetry datasets, operating systems datasets and network traffic dataset of IoT
(TON-IoT), Edge-IIoTset, and IoMT2024) have demonstrated
superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods.

I. INTRODUCTION
ITH the proliferation of the Internet of Things (IoT),
its integration into daily life across industries, such as
healthcare, pharmacy, food and beverage, agriculture, electronics, transportation, and retail, has become ubiquitous, harnessing
its immense potential for practical solutions [1], [2], [3], [4],
[5]. However, this widespread adoption has also attracted the
attention of malicious actors, leading to a surge in IoT device
intrusions and attacks [2]. These attacks have manifested in
various forms, posing significant risks to users and society.
For instance, hackers can infiltrate wireless webcams to violate
privacy, compromise medical equipment endangering patients’
lives, or access sensitive data from children’s smartwatches and
vehicle control systems, manipulating their functionality with
dire consequences. A recent case underscores this threat, as
a survey revealed that smart home systems experienced over
12 000 illicit attacks within a week, highlighting the urgent
need for robust security measures to safeguard IoT devices and
their users. Therefore, it is imperative to develop a lightweight
IoT network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) to effectively
detect and mitigate the emerging cyber threats.
IoT systems present distinct challenges for intrusion detection
when compared to traditional networks, primarily due to their
inherent constraints in computational power, memory capacity,
battery life, and network bandwidth. Furthermore, the integration of a diverse range of heterogeneous protocols and technologies within the same system exacerbates these challenges. Previous intrusion detection models primarily relied on datasets with
manually selected features. However, network traffic feature sets
provided by various edge devices often exhibit inconsistency,
and there is no standardized network traffic feature set in this
field. As a result, feature engineering-based intrusion detection
approaches face significant challenges in manually extracting
features from the diverse range of protocols and technologies.
In contrast, end-to-end approaches offer a promising direction
for intrusion detection in IoT environments, as they have the

II. RELATED WORK
A. Knowledge Distillation
Knowledge distillation [9] is a highly effective technique for
compressing deep learning models through a transfer learning

process. In this approach, a larger, more complex teacher model
guides the training of a smaller, more compact student model,
enabling the student to mimic the functionality of the teacher.
This technique has garnered significant research attention due to
its remarkable ability to substantially reduce model sizes while
maintaining high levels of accuracy. Wang et al. [10] introduced
the concept of knowledge distillation into a triple convolutional neural network (TCNN) architecture, termed KD-TCNN,
with the aim of deriving optimal features while utilizing a
compressed student network. This approach effectively reduces
resource consumption and model parameters, enabling real-time
detection capabilities. Meanwhile, Wang et al. [11] developed
BERT of Theseus Transformer and PoolFormer, which utilizes
knowledge distillation in conjunction with a Siamese network
dimension reducer and a large teacher model. This method
successfully compresses an efficient detection model for IoT
applications, demonstrating the suitability of knowledge distillation for IoT NIDS. By enabling model compression, knowledge
distillation facilitates the practical deployment of NIDS in IoT
environments.
B. Transformer
The Transformer model [12] has achieved remarkable performance on a wide range of natural language processing tasks by
incorporating the attention mechanism, distinguishing it from
traditional deep learning approaches that rely on recurrent or
convolutional layers. Subsequently, BERT [13] further advanced
this field by leveraging a bidirectional Transformer architecture
that enables the sharing of attention and parameters, resulting in
outstanding multitask fine-tuning capabilities. These innovative
models have found widespread application in IoT NIDS. Ferrag
et al. [14] introduced a novel approach for cyber threat hunting
in 6G IoT NIDS, combining the power of generative adversarial
networks with a Transformer-based model. Their work focused
on threat mining using a new dataset, demonstrating the potential of this methodology. In Ferrag et al. [15] they presented
SecurityLLM, which builds upon BERT from the ground up,
integrating it with a large language model called FalconLLM for
enhanced threat analysis. Furthermore, they introduced PPFLE,
a structured and private format for representing network data,
addressing the challenges posed by nontextual data for language
models. Basset et al. [16] proposed federated deep learningbased intrusion detection system (FED-IDS), a framework that
utilizes federated deep learning for collaborative detection of
cyberattacks. Their approach incorporates an improved Transformer model to extract features for edge node detection, while
ensuring data privacy is preserved.
Furthermore, several studies have delved into the application
of end-to-end models grounded in Transformer architectures and
their derivatives, directly processing raw traffic data. He et al.
[17] introduced a payload encoding representation from Transformer, leveraging dynamic word embedding techniques to autonomously discern the salient features within traffic payloads.
Similarly, Lin et al. [18] presented encrypted traffic bidirectional
encoder representation from Transformer (ET-BERT), a groundbreaking traffic representation model that introduces a tailored
traffic representation method alongside two traffic-oriented

Given the inherent challenges of IoT traffic, which lack clear
semantic units and human-comprehensible content compared
to natural language and imagery, it significantly diverges from
these domains. Furthermore, IoT systems often integrate diverse
and heterogeneous protocols and technologies within the same
ecosystem [7]. To address these complexities and enable our
model to broadly learn the generalized representation of IoT

traffic during the pretraining phase, we devise an enhanced traffic
representation framework.
To refine our approach, we initially segment the traffic into
discrete packets, each of which is further divided into a header
and payload section. For traffic that solely comprises headers
or adheres to protocols, such as Bluetooth, we designate the
entire packet as the header and augment the payload section
with a dedicated [PAD] token to maintain a consistent structure.
In cases where the payload exceeds 80 bytes, we truncate it
at this threshold to ensure efficient processing. To mitigate
potential interference and enhance anonymity, we implement
a series of sanitization measures. Specifically, we replace the
original IP addresses with randomly generated ones, reset all
port numbers to zero, and strip the Ethernet headers from the
packets. These steps not only reduce unnecessary information
but also contribute to preserving the privacy of the data sources.
We tokenize raw IoT traffic datagrams into a sequence of units,
each comprising two adjacent hexadecimal bytes. These tokens
are then represented using byte-pair Encoding, with a range of 0
to 65535, supporting a maximum vocabulary size of 65 536. To
enhance pretraining tasks, we incorporate special tokens: [CLS]
as the initial token in each sequence, [SEP] to delineate headers
from payloads, [PAD] for padding to meet fixed minimum length
requirements, and [MASK] for context-based learning during
pretraining.
Finally, a comprehensive tag representation is constructed
utilizing three distinct word encodings: token embedding, position embedding, and segment embedding, to serve as the input
representation for pretraining, which are depicted in Fig. 1.
Following N iterations of Transformer encoding, where N varies
between the student and teacher models, the refined final token
embedding is derived.

Continue-pretraining: Previous research has demonstrated
that starting from scratch when training BERT on domainspecific corpora, such as the biomedical domain, and utilizing
a custom vocabulary tailored to that space can significantly

by system upgrades. Consequently, a single dataset may not adequately represent the full spectrum of traffic types, necessitating
a more nuanced approach to data collection and analysis. To
address these challenges, we incorporate continue-pretraining
into our methodology. We refine the teacher model, initially pretrained on a foundational dataset, with a specialized new dataset.
This approach facilitates adaptability to new environments and
evolving systems, ensuring representation quality is preserved
even with limited data, offering an advantage over training from
scratch with small datasets.

D. Fine-tuning using Data Transformation
During pretraining, the model is solely trained on unlabeled
byte-level data-gram traffic, fostering its ability to acquire generalized representations of network traffic. The pretrained model
can be efficiently leveraged for various downstream tasks. During fine-tuning, a minimal set of labeled traffic is utilized to
adapt the pretrained model for specific downstream tasks. Prior
to fine-tuning, traffic data must be converted into datagram
byte tokens. The [CLS] from the pretrained model’s output
encapsulates the entire input traffic’s representation.
Given the shared architecture between pretraining and finetuning, we refine all parameters by fine-tuning the pretrained
model with known traffic data representations. For intrusion detection in IoT networks, where resources are scarce and real-time
processing is crucial, a multiclass classifier leverages the [CLS]
representation from the output layer to classify potential threats.
To tackle these constraints, we propose an innovative fine-tuning
approach that utilizes a sliding window as the input mechanism,
enabling efficient and timely analysis of data streams. The
sliding window approach of fine-tuning, depicted in Fig. 1,
efficiently processes traffic data by maintaining a contextual
window. This approach requires only a minimal number of packets (K = 5) to form a window sequence, significantly reducing
computational load and ensuring real-time performance. We
utilize a continuous stream of K packets as input and extract
window sequences with a stride of S = 1. Given that packets
within a window sequence originate from the same known traffic
dataset, each sequence is assigned a corresponding label for
accurate analysis.
