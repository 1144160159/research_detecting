a b s t r a c t
The convergence of Information Technology (IT) and Operational Technology (OT) in Industry
4.0 produces diverse data streams, such as system logs, sensor readings, and network traﬃc,
which are vital for industrial security. However, existing security analytics are siloed by modality and rely on centralized processing, raising concerns regarding privacy, latency, and scalability. Although Federated Learning (FL) mitigates privacy risks, most frameworks remain unimodal, lack support for non-IID data distributions, and face adversarial evasion challenges. We
propose FedMamba, a novel multimodal Federated Learning (MMFL) framework that creates a
uniﬁed Mamba-based model to address these issues via (i) eﬃcient cross-modal learning, (ii) a
FedProx-based protocol for stable non-IID training that remains compatible with secure aggregation, and (iii) modality-speciﬁc adversarial training for robustness. Experiments on HDFS, SWaT,
and CICIoMT-2024 datasets show that the standard FedMamba achieved competitive macro F1scores of 0.9584, 0.9795, and 0.9665 relative to centralized baselines, but degraded on HDFS and
SWaT under PGD attack (0.3791 and 0.5147), whereas CICIoMT-2024 remained robust under the
same attack (0.9665). The adversarially trained FedMamba-AT sustains robust F1-scores (0.9480,
0.8357, 0.9645). FedMamba oﬀers a robust and scalable solution for secure IIoT monitoring.

1. Introduction
The fourth industrial revolution, or Industry 4.0, represents the convergence of cyber-physical systems and advanced analytics
in manufacturing environments, driven by the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) [1]. This paradigm empowers smart factories to
generate voluminous and heterogeneous data streams that span system logs, sensor telemetry, and network activity. These data are
vital for operational eﬃciency and real-time threat detection. However, the interconnected nature of Operational Technology and
Information Technology systems creates complex interdependencies, where anomalies in one domain may signal or cause disruptions
in another. This calls for a uniﬁed multimodal security analytics framework that is capable of cross-domain inference.
Conventional security analytics are insuﬃcient to address these challenges. Typically, independent models are deployed for each
data modality, resulting in siloed insights and a high operational overhead. Centralized analytics systems attempt to fuse data centrally
but face bandwidth bottlenecks, privacy regulations, and single points of failure. Consequently, there is a growing need for holistic
and decentralized solutions that preserve privacy while leveraging the full spectrum of heterogeneous factory data.

FL has emerged as a promising paradigm that trains a global model collaboratively without aggregating raw data [2,3]. However,
deploying FL in real-world industrial environments poses several challenges. First, clients naturally possess statistically heterogeneous
(non-IID) data, which can cause instability and failure to converge in standard FL algorithms [4]. Second, the transmission of model
updates to a central server, even without raw data, creates privacy vulnerabilities against an “honest-but-curious” server [5]. Third,
early studies commonly assumed that all clients possessed homogeneous, single-modality data, which is an unrealistic assumption in
diverse IIoT environments [6].
This limitation has motivated the development of multimodal federated learning (MMFL), in which clients can hold diﬀerent data
modalities. Surveys such as [6] and recent advances in MMFL [7–9] have explored architectural solutions for modality heterogeneity;
however, these methods are primarily evaluated on benign, non-security datasets (e.g., image-text benchmarks) and do not evaluate
the adversarial threat landscape of industrial environments.
Federated models are susceptible to inference-time adversarial attacks, which can subvert anomaly detection and classiﬁcation
[10]. Although defenses such as adversarial training have been studied in homogeneous FL scenarios [11], their extension to multimodal environments is non-trivial. Each modality requires custom perturbation strategies, and uniﬁed robustness is not achieved by
naïve adaptation.
The intersection of multimodality, decentralization, and adversarial robustness remains largely unaddressed, despite its signiﬁcance in IIoT systems. Surveys and representative MMFL methods [6–9] primarily evaluate benign, non-security benchmarks and do
not address adversarial robustness or extreme modality and feature skew typical of smart factories. In parallel, FL security works
on inference-time attacks and adversarial training [10,11] and generally assumes a single modality, leaving modality-speciﬁc perturbation models and aggregation under modality skew unexamined. Prior FL work for IoT/ICS [12–16] also omits an end-to-end
adversarially robust multimodal design.
To address this gap, we propose FedMamba, a framework that jointly addresses data heterogeneity, training stability, privacy,
and adversarial robustness in IIoT security. The framework uniﬁes system logs (HDFS), sensor time series (SWaT), and network
ﬂows (CICIoMT-2024) using a shared Mamba backbone. Its core is a robust federated protocol that leverages FedProx to promote
stable convergence on non-IID data and incorporates a secure-aggregation simulation to protect clients’ updates during aggregation.
Finally, it integrates a modality-speciﬁc adversarial training scheme to harden the ﬁnal model against inference-time attacks [6–11].
Our framework, to our knowledge, is among the ﬁrst to jointly address multimodal heterogeneity, privacy, and adversarial robustness
in IIoT.
Our key contributions are:
1) Uniﬁed architecture for modality feature skew. We design a single multimodal model with explicit parameter partitioning
𝜽 = 𝜽in ⊎ 𝜽back ⊎ 𝜽cls . Modality-speciﬁc adapters and heads isolate heterogeneity across logs, time series, and tabular ﬂows, while
a shared Mamba backbone enables cross-modal transfer with minimal interference (Fig. 2 and Section 3.3).
2) Modality-aware block-wise aggregation compatible with secure aggregation. Under one-modality-per-client, we provide a
principled weighting scheme that averages the shared backbone across all clients and averages each modality block only within
that modality. This is implemented by client-side pre-scaling and server-side partitioned averaging, which ﬁts standard secure
aggregation without protocol changes (Eqs. (15)–(17) and Sections 3.4, 3.5).
3) Federated optimization under modal and statistical heterogeneity. We justify why FedProx’s non-convex analysis extends to
our block-partitioned model because each block receives an unbiased estimate of its sub-objective under the proposed aggregation.
Empirically, this prevents the collapse observed with FedAvg under severe non-IID skew (Algorithm 1 and Section 4).
4) Selective federated adversarial training with cross-modal robustness transfer. We introduce a selective scheme that adversarially trains only vulnerable modalities, and show that the robustness learned in the shared backbone transfers across modalities.
This preserves clean accuracy on non-trained modalities and reduces client-side cost compared with training all modalities (Section 3.6 and Table 3).
FedMamba addresses heterogeneous IIoT environments with a shared Mamba backbone, modality-aware block-wise aggregation
that remains compatible with secure aggregation, FedProx-based optimization, and selective adversarial training.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 3 presents the system and threat model, the FedMamba architecture, training protocol, secure aggregation compatibility, and selective adversarial training. Section 4 covers experiments, robustness
analyses, ablations, and scalability. Section 5 concludes, and Appendices A–D provide supporting details.
2. Related work
Our research is situated at the conﬂuence of three critical domains: FL for IoT security, the emerging ﬁeld of MMFL, and adversarial
robustness in decentralized systems. We reviewed the state-of-the-art in each domain to precisely situate the contributions of our
FedMamba framework.
FL is widely used as a privacy-preserving approach for security analytics in IIoT. A signiﬁcant body of work has focused on
applying FL to network intrusion detection in unimodal settings. Foundational frameworks such as DeepFed [12] demonstrate the
eﬃcacy of using federated CNN-GRU models to achieve high detection rates on network traﬃc data while preserving privacy. This
line of research has been extended through various architectural and algorithmic innovations in recent years. For instance, some
works have explored hybrid ensembles [13], federated transfer learning [15], and optimized hybrid models like CNN-LSTM [16] to
improve generalization and accuracy.

Given the edge resource constraints, several studies have emphasized eﬃciency, including lightweight designs for edge deployment
[14]. Concurrently, a parallel stream of research has addressed the foundational challenges of the federated process. A primary issue
is statistical heterogeneity, where non-IID client data can cause model divergence ("client drift"), leading to unstable convergence.
Foundational algorithms, such as FedProx [4], were developed to counteract this by introducing a proximal regularization term.
Another critical challenge is the privacy of model updates, as a curious server can potentially infer client data. Cryptographic protocols,
such as secure aggregation, allow the server to aggregate only masked updates [5]. These solutions are typically evaluated in a
unimodal context.
Despite these signiﬁcant advancements in architecture, eﬃciency, and FL protocol security, a common thread unites this body of
work: it operates under the assumption that all clients possess homogeneous, single-modality data. These frameworks, while powerful,
are predominantly designed for and evaluated on a single data type, such as network traﬃc [12,13,15–18] or sensor data [14].
However, they do not address the intrinsic heterogeneity of smart factories, where system logs, sensor telemetry, and network traﬃc
coexist.
The challenge of learning from diverse data modalities across clients has given rise to multimodal federated learning (MMFL). A
survey by Che et al. [6] provides a foundational taxonomy of architectural challenges, including client-level modality heterogeneity.
Recent studies have proposed speciﬁc architectural solutions, such as enabling clients with partial modalities to collaborate [7], using
contrastive learning to align representations [9], or employing multimodal transformers [8]. Although architecturally innovative,
these methods are primarily validated on benign, non-industrial benchmarks (such as vision-language or medical datasets), with
little or no evaluation of IIoT security data. Furthermore, they generally do not incorporate adversarial threat models critical to
security applications, nor do they typically address the challenge of ensuring stable convergence under the dual pressures of modal
and statistical (non-IID) heterogeneity.
Federated systems are vulnerable to adversarial attacks. Research in this area has explored the factors inﬂuencing robustness [10]
and proposed methods for propagating robustness between clients [11], typically in unimodal and homogeneous scenarios. From an
industrial perspective, Kim [19] provides an insightful analysis of adversarial attacks in centralized industrial control systems (ICS)
environments, while Ahmad et al. [20] propose a StuxNet-inspired adversarial learning framework for detecting advanced threats in
critical infrastructure. However, a signiﬁcant gap remains in the development of modality-aware adversarial defense strategies within
a federated context, which is a challenge central to our work.
To unify advancements across these domains and address their limitations, we propose FedMamba, an MMFL framework explicitly designed for industrial IoT security. Unlike traditional FL for IoT, our approach is designed to handle client-level modality
heterogeneity. In contrast to prior MMFL work, we integrate adversarial robustness as a ﬁrst-class design objective and ensure the
privacy of updates via secure aggregation. Finally, our approach combines FedProx for stability under non-IID multimodal data with
modality-aware adversarial training, providing a practical end-to-end solution for heterogeneous smart factory environments.
3. Methodology
In this section, we formalize FedMamba and its training protocol. We ﬁrst establish centralized unimodal baselines to calibrate
the expected performance ceiling. We then specify the uniﬁed multimodal architecture, together with a FedProx-based objective
and a modality-aware aggregation rule that is compatible with secure aggregation. Finally, we extend the protocol with modalityspeciﬁc adversarial training to improve inference-time robustness. Fig. 1 provides an overview; Sections 3.2, 3.3, and 3.6 present the
corresponding deﬁnitions, equations, and algorithms. The complete notation reference is provided in Appendix A (Table A.1).
3.1. System and threat model
We deﬁne the system architecture and threat model to formally scope the security and privacy contributions.
3.1.1. System model
We model a distributed system, such as a smart factory, which comprises two primary entities:
3.1.1.1. Clients. A set of 𝐾 heterogeneous clients,  = {1, … , 𝐾}, where each client 𝑘 ∈  possesses a private local dataset 𝑘
corresponding to exactly one modality 𝑚𝑘 ∈ . Clients have suﬃcient computational resources for local training but are constrained
from sharing raw data because of privacy or network-related limitations.
3.1.1.2. Central server. A single server orchestrates this federated process. We assume an honest-but-curious coordinator. To protect
client updates, we simulate a secure-aggregation setting [5] to evaluate compatibility (see Section 3.5).
3.1.2. Threat model
We assess the robustness of the trained global model 𝑓 (⋅; 𝜽) to inference-time evasion attacks.

3.1.3. Training-time backdoor probe
To quantify the baseline susceptibility, we ran a short training-time backdoor poisoning probe without changing the learning rule
or secure aggregation simulation. In each round, 12% of the selected clients were malicious. A malicious client poisons a batch with
a probability of 0.25 and modiﬁes a random 30% of the examples in that batch, relabeling them to the target class. Triggers are
modality-aware: HDFS sets the last two tokens to id (|| − 1); SWaT adds +0.015 to the ﬁrst two sensors over the last eight time steps;

fraction 0.4, Dirichlet 𝛼=0.3. We report clean Accuracy and Macro F1 on unmodiﬁed test data and Attack Success Rate (ASR) over

3.3. A robust multimodal federated learning framework with simulated secure aggregation
The central challenge is learning from clients with fundamentally diﬀerent data modalities, an extreme form of non-IID data
distribution that we term modality feature skew, where the feature space and data structure diﬀer across client types. Two further
requirements for deployment are robustness to statistical heterogeneity (unbalanced labels within a modality) and data privacy
preservation. We address this threefold challenge by co-designing a uniﬁed model architecture and a robust, privacy-aware, federated
training algorithm.

Training our multimodal architecture in a non-IID and privacy-sensitive environment requires addressing two primary challenges:
(i) instability caused by statistical heterogeneity and (ii) the need for modality-aware aggregation that respects the partitioned parameter structure. We address these issues using a principled federated training protocol built on FedProx with custom aggregation
rules.

3.4.1. FedProx for non-IID stability

fails. Unconstrained local training causes client model weights to drift toward solutions that are optimal for locally biased data but
suboptimal globally, leading to unstable convergence.

3.5. Privacy-preserving secure aggregation
A core requirement of FedMamba is to protect client privacy from an honest-but-curious server. We achieve this through a design
that is fully compatible with cryptographic secure aggregation protocols, speciﬁcally the protocol of Bonawitz et al. [5]. This section
details our simulation approach, protocol compatibility, and formal integration with modality-aware aggregation.
3.5.1. Simulation rationale
In this study, we focused on the algorithmic convergence and robustness dynamics of FedMamba. To isolate these factors from
system-level complexities and cryptographic implementation overhead, we simulate the secure aggregation protocol in a numerically
equivalent manner as follows:
1) Each client pre-scales its update by local sample count 𝑛𝑘 before any masking would occur.
2) The server securely computes the sum of scaled updates, recovering a single aggregate from which individual contributions cannot
be inferred.
3) The server completes weighted averaging by partitioning this aggregate and dividing by public total counts 𝑁𝑡 and 𝑁𝑡(𝑚) .
The simulation does not provide cryptographic privacy guarantees; it is standard practice in federated learning to isolate learning dynamics for reproducibility [3,4]. Real-world deployment requires integration with production cryptographic libraries (e.g.,
TensorFlow Federated and PySyft), and the performance implications are discussed in Section 4.5.3.
3.5.2. Compatibility with standard secure aggregation protocols
Our modality-aware aggregation treats secure aggregation as a black-box primitive and does not require changes to the underlying
cryptographic protocol.

3.6. Hardening the framework with federated adversarial training
To improve resilience against inference-time evasion attacks, we integrated a federated adversarial training (FAT) scheme into
the framework. The objective of FAT extended beyond hardening individual clients; it leveraged the federated learning process to
collaboratively construct a single and globally robust model. While adversarial examples were generated on local client data, defensive
training enhanced feature representations within the shared Mamba backbone (𝜽back ). When clients transmitted their model updates,
these robustness-enhancing gradients were aggregated by the server. This process eﬀectively disseminated the learned resilience
across the entire network, allowing clients to collectively contribute to a global defense that was stronger than any client could build
independently. This complemented the FedProx regularization used to ensure stable convergence [21,26].

In this section, we present a comprehensive empirical evaluation of the FedMamba framework. Our analysis is structured to ﬁrst
validate the performance of our federated approach against centralized baselines using the clean data. We then diagnosed the inherent
vulnerabilities of the model to evasion attacks and subsequently demonstrated the eﬀectiveness of our proposed federated adversarial
training defense. Finally, an ablation study validated the choice of the FedProx algorithm.

4.1. Experimental setup
4.1.1. Datasets and preprocessing
Each dataset was split into training, validation, and test partitions with proportions 70%, 15%, and 15%. For binary tasks, we
selected a decision threshold 𝜏 on the validation set by maximizing the macro F1-score over a uniform grid on [0.05, 0.95] with a step
0.05. For the multiclass CICIoMT-2024 task, we use the arg max class prediction (no decision threshold). We report the Macro F1-score
for the held-out test sets for all modalities.
4.1.2. Federated environment simulation
We simulated statistical heterogeneity using a Dirichlet sampler with a concentration 𝛼 = 0.3 per class to distribute indices across
clients, followed by a non-empty enforcement pass. The resulting severe data skew for the CICIoMT-2024 task is shown in Fig. 3.
In the baseline conﬁguration, we use 𝐾 = 35 clients in total (distributed across modalities). In each round, we sampled a fraction
𝜌 = 0.4 of clients per modality uniformly at random to form 𝑡 . For clarity, Fig. 3 visualizes the CICIoMT-2024 non-IID split for a
representative subset of 10 clients.
4.2. Experimental baselines and evaluation metrics
To rigorously evaluate our framework, we established a clear set of baselines and metrics. We evaluated our framework against
the following benchmarks:
1) Centralized Specialists: Individual Mamba models trained centrally on each dataset, representing a non-federated performance
upper bound.
2) FedMamba (Standard, FedProx): Our primary federated model, trained using FedProx and secure aggregation on non-IID data.
3) FedMamba-AT (Robust): Our ﬁnal proposed model, hardened using the selective federated adversarial training scheme from
Section 3.6.
4) Weighted FedAvg: For the ablation study, we compare standard Federated Averaging under both IID and non-IID data partitions
to isolate the impact of FedProx.
We evaluated the Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and Macro F1-score of the proposed framework. Owing to the class imbalance, the
Macro F1-score was the primary metric. For binary tasks, we also reported the false-positive rate (FPR) and ROC-AUC. For CICIoMT2024, we report one-vs-rest macro-averaged ROC-AUC. The adversarial evaluation settings are summarized in Table B.3.
4.3. Performance and robustness analysis
We next present our empirical results to demonstrate the eﬃcacy and resilience of the proposed framework.
4.3.1. Baseline performance of the federated framework
Our ﬁrst evaluation established the performance of the standard FedMamba model (trained with FedProx) on clean, unseen test
data and compared it with that of the centralized specialist upper bound. The results in Table 2 show that our federated approach

4.3.2. Adversarial vulnerability of the standard model
We ﬁrst assessed the baseline robustness of the standard FedMamba. Table 3 reported Macro F1-score on clean data as well as
black-box Gaussian noise and transfer-based attacks. The Auto-PGD [28] white-box results are presented in Fig. 5. As shown there,
the standard model degraded sharply on the HDFS and SWaT datasets, whereas CICIoMT-2024 remained relatively stable.
The results revealed signiﬁcant vulnerabilities. Although the CICIoMT-2024 task remained largely unaﬀected, the model’s performance on HDFS and SWaT collapsed under attack. The Macro F1-scores plummeted by 60.4% for HDFS and 47.5% for SWaT. This
degradation underscored that high performance on clean data oﬀered no guarantee of security and provided a strong motivation for

4.4. Ablation study
To validate the core design choices of our framework, we conducted a series of ablation studies focusing on the aggregation
protocol, shared backbone architecture, and sequence model.
To justify our choice of FedProx over standard FedAvg in a non-IID environment, we performed an ablation. As shown in Table 4,
Weighted FedAvg performed well under ideal IID splits, but under realistic non-IID skew, it’s performance degraded on CICIoMT2024 while remaining competitive on HDFS and SWaT. In contrast, FedProx mitigated client drift and restored stable convergence
on CICIoMT-2024 and across all modalities.
This study revealed the critical impact of statistical heterogeneity and validated our choice of FedProx. While Weighted FedAvg
performed well under ideal IID conditions, its performance on the complex, multiclass CICIoMT-2024 task collapsed under the realistic non-IID distribution, with the Macro F1-score dropping from 0.9550 to 0.8550. This was a clear manifestation of client drift,
exacerbated by the extreme data skew, as shown in Fig. 3, where clients such as C3 and C4 possessed zero samples of the “Benign”
class. Unconstrained local training on highly biased data leads to diverged model updates that cannot be eﬀectively averaged, causing
performance collapse. In contrast, under the same non-IID conditions, FedProx prevented this collapse. Its proximal term mitigated
client drift, enabling stable convergence and achieving a ﬁnal F1-score of 0.9665, which even surpassed the IID baseline. This result
validates FedProx as a necessary component for stable convergence in heterogeneous multimodal federated environments.

4.4.2. Architectural choice: Mamba vs. transformer
To further validate our selection of Mamba as the backbone, we conducted an architectural ablation by comparing FedMamba
with an equivalent framework using a standard transformer backbone, FedTransformer. Both models were trained using the same
federated protocols. The results in Table 6 reveal a trade-oﬀ between the model performance and resource eﬃciency.
The FedTransformer model achieved near-perfect accuracy in the HDFS log analysis task (0.9977 F1-score), outperforming FedMamba. However, this came at a signiﬁcant cost: the transformer-based model was more than 2× larger (1.18 M vs. 0.54 M parameters)
and required more than 2× the communication bandwidth per client upload (~4.2 MB vs. ~1.8 MB).
This eﬃciency gap is a major consideration for IIoT security, where models are often deployed on edge devices with limited
memory, power, and network bandwidth. FedMamba delivered strong, reliable performance across all three modalities while being
substantially more lightweight and communication-eﬃcient. Given that its inference latency is comparably low, FedMamba is a more
practical and scalable solution for real-world deployment. Thus, this study conﬁrmed that Mamba is a compelling architectural choice
that strikes an excellent balance between high accuracy and the operational constraints of the IoT ecosystem. Table 6 quantiﬁes this
trade-oﬀ. Fig. 6 shows that the convergence trends matched the Mamba curves despite the larger model and upload.

4.5.2. Eﬃciency and cost analysis
The uniﬁed architecture of FedMamba provides signiﬁcant eﬃciency gains, as validated by our ablation study in Table 5. The
uniﬁed model contained only 0.54M parameters, a 62.1% reduction compared with the 1.41M parameters required by the no-share

Computationally, the linear complexity of the Mamba backbone, (𝐿𝑚 ), provides a distinct advantage over the quadratic complexity of transformer-based alternatives, particularly for long sequences in IIoT data. For real-time deployment, the converged model
was highly performant, demonstrating an inference latency of only 0.012-0.013ms per sample on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. This
high throughput conﬁrms the suitability of FedMamba for eﬃcient and real-time threat detection in high-velocity data environments.
4.5.3. Secure aggregation: Deployment feasibility and overhead
We simulated secure aggregation (SA) to isolate the learning dynamics. Here, we summarize the feasibility and expected overhead
when deploying a production SA protocol.
Protocol compatibility. Our modality-aware partitioned averaging remains a black-box consumer of SA in the sense of Bonawitz
et al. [5]. Client-side pre-scaling by 𝑛𝑘 occurs before masking, the server recovers only the masked sum, and the ﬁnal divisions by
𝑁𝑡 and 𝑁𝑡(𝑚) occur on the unmasked aggregate. No changes to the cryptographic primitives are required, which keeps the design
compatible with common SA implementations.
Expected overhead. Prior reports for SA rounds with dropout resilience indicate a communication expansion in the range of
1.6-1.8× for the MB-scale model updates. For our update size (0.54M parameters, 1.70-1.85 MB), this yields a masked upload of
roughly 2.9-3.3 MB per client. Table 8 instantiates a representative mid-point factor to show the end-to-end upload time impact at
two link speeds.
Client-side masking cost scales linearly with the update length and is typically on the order of seconds on edge-class hardware
for MB-scale updates, while server-side unmasking adds a small fraction of the aggregation time. Our pre-scaling and partitioned
divisions are algebraic operations on the unmasked sum and are negligible compared to the network transfer.

4.6. Comparative analysis with state-of-the-art frameworks
To contextualize the contributions of FedMamba, we conducted a comparative analysis with representative state-of-the-art intrusion detection systems designed for the IIoT and cyber-physical systems. Although direct numerical comparisons were precluded by
diﬀerences in datasets and experimental conditions, the overview in Table 9 highlights how FedMamba addresses critical limitations
in the literature.
The literature reveals a trend toward increasingly sophisticated FL-based detection frameworks. Methods such as those of Albanbay
et al. [29] and Chatterjee et al. [13] demonstrate strong performance under unimodal federated contexts. Zeng et al. [14] introduced
adversarial training (AT) within a federated framework for sensor data, and Rajesh et al. [15] utilized federated transfer learning
to enhance generalization. Concurrently, Kim [19] underscored the severity of the adversarial threat landscape in ICS within a
centralized paradigm.
This analysis revealed a persistent gap in the literature. Although prior studies have addressed these challenges in isolation, no
framework has provided a holistic solution that jointly satisﬁes privacy-preserving decentralized learning (federated), multimodal
processing, and resilience against evasion attacks. To the best of our knowledge, FedMamba is the ﬁrst framework to systematically
integrate and validate these three capabilities.
4.7. Limitations and future work
Our secure aggregation is simulated, so future work will integrate production cryptographic protocols and quantify end-to-end
overhead in real deployments. The current evaluation focuses on structured IIoT modalities and three datasets, thus extending FedMamba to unstructured streams such as audio and video and to broader benchmarks remains open. We assume one modality per client
and prioritize inference-time evasion, so relaxing this assumption and addressing stronger training-time threats such as poisoning,
backdoors, and Byzantine failures is a priority. Our selective adversarial training covers only HDFS and SWaT with ﬁxed 𝓁∞ budgets,
so adaptive, budget-agnostic defenses and certiﬁed robustness are promising directions. Finally, resource-aware scheduling, client
drift detection, and privacy auditing of update leakage can improve practicality at scale.
5. Conclusion
This study introduced FedMamba, an MMFL framework designed for heterogeneous IIoT and critical infrastructure environments.
We address three challenges: pronounced data heterogeneity across modalities, statistically non-IID client distributions, and the joint
requirements of privacy awareness and adversarial robustness. The contributions of this study are as follows: (i) a uniﬁed Mambabased architecture that processes and integrates diverse security modalities; (ii) a robust, privacy-aware federated training protocol
built on FedProx for convergence stability and a simulated secure aggregation layer compatible with practical protocols; and (iii) an
integrated federated adversarial training scheme that hardens the model against inference-time evasion.

Experiments validated the eﬀectiveness of our proposed approach. The standard FedMamba model performed well on clean data,
achieving Macro F1-scores of 0.9584, 0.9795, and 0.9665 on HDFS, SWaT, and CICIoMT-2024, respectively, which are competitive

has limitations. First, our simulated secure aggregation omits the communication and computational overhead of real-world cryptography. Second, while the framework excels in structured IIoT data, its scalability to unstructured modalities (e.g., video) remains to
be explored. Future work will focus on integrating production-ready cryptographic libraries, extending the framework to new data
types, and broadening the threat model to include training-time attacks such as data poisoning.

C. Secure aggregation microbenchmark protocol
This appendix deﬁnes a lightweight protocol to empirically validate the projections in Section 4.5.3.
Setup. Simulate 𝐾 ∈ {35, 70, 100} clients on the controlled LAN. Each client sends a ﬁxed tensor matching the update size (0.54M
parameters). Compare a plaintext weighted sum with production SA implementation under identical scheduler settings. Throttle
uplinks to 10 and 100 Mbps, respectively.
Variants. Evaluate both the no-dropout and dropout-resilient SA rounds. Record whether the stack requires integer payloads and,
if so, the packing or ﬁxed-point format applied.
Metrics. Over ﬁve runs per condition, the following were recorded: (i) per-client upload bytes, (ii) client masking time, (iii) server
unmasking time, and (iv) round duration. The median and interquartile range were reported.
Expectation. For MB-scale updates, communication expands by roughly 1.6-1.8× with a modest compute overhead at the client
and a small additional server-aggregation cost. Synchronization contributes a small ﬁxed addition per round, which is determined by
participation and timeouts.
