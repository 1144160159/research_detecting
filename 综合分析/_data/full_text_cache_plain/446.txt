Frequency-Domain Anomaly Detection for
Encrypted Traffic in Industrial Control Systems
Zhangfa Wu , Huifang Li , Yi Hua , Nguyen H. Tran , Senior Member, IEEE,
and Hongping Gan , Member, IEEE

Abstract—Industrial control systems (ICSs) are
becoming increasingly interconnected, rendering them
susceptible to cyber attacks. Timely detection of anomalies
in encrypted data flows is crucial for ensuring the reliability
and security of ICS. Although deep learning-based anomaly
detection methods have made significant strides, their
implementation in point-by-point mapping paradigms often
necessitates a tradeoff between feature representation
and computational efficiency, especially in resourceconstrained environments. In addition, these methods
face challenges with class imbalance and limited labeled
anomaly data. To address these challenges, we propose a
frequency-domain anomaly detection (FreAD) framework
specifically tailored for encrypted data flows in ICS. FreAD
utilizes a frequency-domain feature fusion encoding module to capture global temporal dependencies. An anomaly
scoring network integrates a small amount of labeled data
along with pseudolabeled data generated from the modified
Z-score algorithm, effectively addressing class imbalance.
Furthermore, the frequency-domain deviation prior module
can alleviate the contraction of interclass distances during
unsupervised training. Extensive experiments demonstrate
that FreAD significantly surpasses other state-of-the-art
anomaly detection algorithms.
Index Terms—Encrypted traffic, frequency-domain deviation, frequency-domain feature fusion, semi-supervised
anomaly detection.

I. INTRODUCTION
NDUSTRIAL control systems (ICSs) are increasingly interconnected through modern communication networks. The
increasing complexity of ICS and the proliferation of industrial
Internet of Things devices have brought new vulnerabilities to
cyber attacks, leading to increasingly serious network security
issues [1], especially in the detection of anomalies in encrypted
traffic [2]. Since traditional detection schemes cannot directly
analyze the abnormal activities contained in the encrypted payload content [3], such as data breaches, unauthorized access, or
even sabotage [4], therefore, timely and accurate detection of
anomalies in encrypted traffic is crucial to ensure the reliability
and security of ICS and mitigate the risk of catastrophic failures
or interruptions.
Many studies have explored the challenge of detecting anomalies in encrypted traffic within ICS through various methodologies, encompassing traditional statistical techniques [5], [6], [7],
[20], [26] as well as deep learning (DL)-based approaches [8],
[9], [10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16]. Traditional methods typically rely on handcrafted expert features, which often
struggle to capture intricate temporal dependencies and are
susceptible to challenges posed by high-dimensional data. In
contrast, DL-based techniques, such as multilayer perceptrons
(MLPs) [10], [11], autoencoders (AEs) [12], [13], [14], [15],
and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) [16], [17], have
demonstrated significant success in deciphering complex patterns from encrypted flows. Notably, recent advancements in
Transformer-based models [18], [19] exhibit enhanced capabilities in modeling global temporal dependencies. However, DLbased methods generally necessitate considerable computational
resources for both training and inference, presenting challenges
for resource-constrained ICS environments. Furthermore, pointby-point mapping techniques employed in certain architectures
may still encounter representation information bottlenecks when
processing high-frequency sequential data. These limitations,
coupled with inherent challenges, such as class imbalance and
the scarcity of labeled anomaly data, underscore the need for
alternative solutions that effectively balance performance with
operational feasibility.
To address these challenges, we introduce FreAD, a
frequency-domain semi-supervised anomaly detection framework for encrypted traffic in ICS. Specifically, FreAD leverages
a frequency-domain feature fusion encoding module (F3 EM)
to extract features from encrypted data flows, overcoming the

II. RELATED WORKS
The security and reliability of ICS have become critical
concerns as these systems become increasingly interconnected
through modern communication networks. This increased connectivity has introduced significant network security challenges,
particularly in detecting anomalies within encrypted network
traffic. Researchers have explored various categories of approaches to tackle this issue.
Traditional statistical methods typically rely on manually
crafted features extracted from encrypted network data flows [5],
[6], [7], [20]. These approaches encompass density estimation
techniques [8], clustering methods [20], and probability estimation [6], [26]. While these techniques are interpretable and
computationally efficient, they often struggle to capture complex

temporal dependencies in network flows and are vulnerable to
the challenges of high-dimensional data. For example, density
estimation methods may inadequately model intricate relationships between time steps, while clustering approaches frequently
rely on predefined assumptions about data distribution, which
limits their adaptability to dynamic network environments.
In contrast, DL-based anomaly detection methods have shown
promising results in uncovering complex patterns and relationships within encrypted traffic flows. These methods encompass MLP models [10], [11], AE approaches [12], [13],
[14], [15], and CNN frameworks [16], [17]. These models
harness the robust feature extraction capabilities of artificial
neural networks to analyze high-dimensional data and uncover
underlying patterns. However, they face challenges in modeling
global temporal dependencies and may encounter representation
bottlenecks in point-by-point mappings. Recent transformerbased models [18], [19] are particularly effective at capturing
these dependencies, yet they often involve tradeoffs regarding
training time and resource consumption, which are common in
DL techniques. For instance, OmniAnomaly [18], a variational
AE-based approach, excels in modeling intricate dependencies
in multivariate time series but struggles with computational
efficiency and scalability, particularly in high-dimensional ICS
environments. Similarly, the AnomalyTransformer [19], which
utilizes self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies, necessitates prior knowledge of the dataset’s anomaly
ratio for effective training, limiting its applicability in resourceconstrained settings. These DL-based methods also share common limitations, including sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning,
high computational complexity, and difficulties in addressing
the variability inherent in ICS data.
In light of these challenges, frequency analysis methods for
anomaly detection have emerged as a complementary approach.
Specifically, the fast Fourier transform (FFT) has become a
popular alternative for analyzing time-series data, particularly
in detecting anomalies in encrypted traffic. FFT is effective at
capturing global temporal dependencies and identifying periodic patterns, making it valuable for spotting anomalies that
may be missed in the time domain [30]. However, FFT has
its limitations; a significant drawback is its inability to effectively capture transient anomalies due to the assumption
of signal stationarity. To address this issue, researchers have
suggested alternative transformations, such as the short-time
Fourier transform (STFT) [31] and wavelet transform [32].
STFT provides localized analysis through windowed FFT, but
it can suffer from time-frequency tradeoffs, which may limit
its ability to accurately capture rapid changes in the signal.
Meanwhile, wavelet transform offers multiresolution analysis to
capture both transient and periodic anomalies; however, it can
be computationally intensive and may require careful selection
of wavelet functions to achieve optimal results. Despite these
alternatives, FFT remains a favored choice for many applications
due to its computational efficiency and simplicity.
To overcome these challenges, the proposed FreAD in this
work aims to address the challenges of encrypted network traffic
anomaly detection in ICS by leveraging frequency-domain analysis and prior knowledge about anomalies. Specifically, FreAD

B. Evaluation Metrics
We employed three widely recognized evaluation metrics to
assess the performance of FreAD: AUC-ROC, AUC-PR, and
F1-score. These metrics provide a comprehensive perspective
on FreAD’s anomaly detection capabilities in encrypted traffic
flows. AUC-ROC measures the model’s ability to distinguish
between normal and anomalous data points; however, it can be
misleading in situations of class imbalance, as a high score may
not accurately reflect the model’s true detection capability for

on the model’s precision and recall, making it more suitable

V. CONCLUSION
In this article, we present FreAD, a novel frequency-domain
semisupervised anomaly detection framework designed to tackle
the challenges of detecting anomalies in encrypted network traffic within ICS. By utilizing frequency-domain features and prior
anomaly knowledge, FreAD effectively alleviates the representation information bottleneck associated with the point-by-point
mapping approach used in traditional DL-based anomaly detection models. Extensive experiments show that FreAD achieves
state-of-the-art AUC-ROC, with average improvements of 4.9%
in AUC-PR and 7.2% in F1-score. FreAD offers a promising
solution to the critical issue of anomaly detection in ICS, essential for maintaining the reliability and security of these systems.
Future work will explore transfer learning and few-shot learning
to adapt FreAD to new anomaly types. We will also assess its
performance in real ICS environments, expanding beyond the
current three benchmark datasets to ensure generalization across
various encryption protocols and systems.
