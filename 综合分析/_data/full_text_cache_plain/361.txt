An Innovative Secure and Privacy-Preserving
Federated Learning-Based Hybrid Deep Learning
Model for Intrusion Detection in Internet-Enabled
Wireless Sensor Networks
Soumya Ranjan Jena, Mohammad Zia Ur Rahman , Deepak K. Sinha , P. Rajendra Kumar, Vrince Vimal ,

Abstract—Cyberspace faces numerous security challenges,
necessitating advanced research in intrusion detection systems
(IDS) to mitigate vulnerabilities. Wireless Sensor Networks
(WSNs) connected to the Internet are particularly vulnerable, requiring robust protection mechanisms. Traditional IDS
struggle with identifying unknown attacks and maintaining
data privacy, especially in WSNs. This study proposes a novel
approach integrating Stacked Convolutional Neural Networks
(SCNN), Bidirectional Long Short Term Memory (Bi-LSTM),
and the African Vulture Optimization Algorithm (AVOA) within
a framework of Federated Learning (FL). The integrated model,
SCNN-Bi-LSTM-AVOA-FL, aims to enhance intrusion detection efficacy while preserving data privacy. A tailored AVOA
optimization method fine-tunes SCNN-Bi-LSTM hyperparameters, leveraging specialized datasets (WSN-DS, CIC-IDS-2017,
and WSN-BFSF) for attack detection and categorization.
Evaluations compare variants with and without FL techniques
(proposed-1 and proposed-2) across metrics such as accuracy,
precision, recall, and F1-Score. Results demonstrate significant

C. Integrated SCNN-Bi-LSTM
Data with large scales and high fluctuations can be effectively extracted with Stacked Convolutional Neural Networks
(SCNN). A SCNN’s hierarchical representation allows it to
identify complex patterns within data. As the SCNN layers
develop, it becomes more capable of detecting intricate patterns of intrusions. In addition to the Bi-LSTM component,
the SCNN can easily extract spatial features with the help
of the Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM)
component. A comprehensive context provides bidirectional
processing with the ability to recognize every temporal pattern
within the data. For example, port scanning attacks, which
occur over time, are especially beneficial for detecting progressive intrusions. SCNN and Bi-LSTM work synergistically
to develop a powerful intrusion detection model. This model
detects temporal and spatial intrusion patterns as an Intrusion
Detection System (IDS). SCNN-Bi-LSTM’s comprehensive
feature analysis and efficiency make it a popular choice in IoT
networks with limited computational resources. The SCNNBi-LSTM architecture is shown in Figure 1.

E. Federated Learning (FL)
In Federated Learning (FL), the preservation of unbiased
data representation and privacy is ensured through advanced
ML architectures [25]. Each iteration of the model uses
local data from the client site. Data centres, smartphones,
IoT devices, wearables, and different organizations’ data
warehouses may all serve as clients. The centralized server
aggregates model updates from the local sites without utilizing
unprocessed data. Along with smart homes and smart cities,
FL is being adopted in the industry and cyber security. As
a result of aligning the parameters of the client model with
those of the global training model, an aggregated model is
developed. In contrast to the server’s shared model, clients
are trained collaboratively [26]. In comparison to classical
ML models, FL is more secure, has compressed latency, and
consumes less power [27]. ML models are also customized for
different client nodes, so users of different nodes can interact
more effectively with the system [28].

F. Integrated FL-SCNN-Bi-LSTM
Our study aims to improve network intrusion categorization
using information from numerous nodes with the help of an
FL framework. The basic structure of the proposed model can
be seen in Figure 1, where there is one server and one client
computer with limited computing capabilities. Each client
maintains its local model, while the server shares a global
neural network with all clients. During each training round,
clients receive the most recent model parameters. Using FL
techniques, the proposed model is illustrated in Figure 1. Over
multiple rounds, different subsets of clients are iterated over
the global model to achieve the ideal state.
