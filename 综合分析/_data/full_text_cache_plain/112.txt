With the increasing demand for the protection of personal network
meta-data, encrypted networks have grown in popularity, so do the
challenge of monitoring and analyzing encrypted network traffic.
Currently, some deep learning-based methods have been proposed
to leverage statistical features for encrypted traffic classification,
which are barely affected by encryption techniques. However, these
works still suffer from two main intrinsic limitations: (1) the feature extraction process lacks a mechanism to take into account
correlations between flows in the flow sequence; and (2) a large
volume of manually-labeled data is required for training an effective
deep classifier. In this paper, we propose a novel semi-supervised
framework to address these problems. To be specific, an efficient
classifier with attention mechanism is proposed to extract features
from flow sequences with low computational cost. Then, a Mean
Teacher-style semi-supervised framework is adopted to exploit the
unlabeled traffic data, where a spatiotemporal data augmentation
method is designed as the key component to explore the spatial and
temporal relationship within the unlabeled traffic data. Experimental results on two real-world traffic datasets demonstrate that our
method outperforms state-of-the-art methods with a large margin.

INTRODUCTION

With the rapid development of the Internet, traffic classification
has become a vital task in cyberspace security and network management [2, 13, 19, 20, 24]. Many content and service providers use
traffic classification technology to improve network performance
and provide their users with high quality service and experience
[26, 28]. However, with the widespread adoption of network encryption technology, traditional rule-based solutions become more
and more ineffective due to the inaccessibility to the raw content
of encrypted data packets. Thus, it is necessary to adopt new mechanisms to effectively classify encrypted traffic.
In recent years, the identification of traffic generated by the encrypted tunnels of virtual private network (VPN) and anonymity
network has attracted much attention, and machine learning (ML)
or deep learning (DL) algorithms that deal with the robust statistical features of traffic flows have been widely acknowledged as
the mainstream method. In addition, we note that many studies
have shown that most traffic data has obvious temporal correlations
[34, 38]. For example, when a user uses an anonymity network to
browse the web, many related application requests will appear in a
short period. These application requests will generate corresponding multiple flows in the traffic. Based on the temporal correlation,
some researchers [28, 39, 42] proposed to use flow sequences for
traffic analysis. While leveraging flow sequences can help improve
classification accuracy, the feature extraction process lacks a mechanism to effectively discriminate the correlation and importance of
different flows in the flow sequence. Moreover, the real-world traffic
is often mixed with unimportant or irrelevant flows in the flow
sequence. As a result, the features extracted from the mixed flows
deteriorate classification performance. In addition, most studies are
conducted within the supervised learning framework, which means
that a large volume of manually-labeled real traffic data is required
to learn an effective classifier. Compared with labeled encrypted
traffic data, we could easily collect abundant unlabeled encrypted

To address the challenges mentioned above, we develop a semisupervised framework (MT-FlowFormer) for encrypted traffic classification. We first build a Transformer-based baseline model for
encrypted traffic classification, denoted as FlowFormer, which leverages an unique self-attention mechanism to allow interaction between related flows in the flow sequence. Since traffic classification
models are usually deployed on edge devices (e.g., router and switch)
with limited storage and computing capabilities [22, 41], our model
adopts an efficient structure for feature extraction. Concretely, to
reduce the model complexity, FlowFormer gradually reduces the
flow sequence length of the hidden representation during the feature extraction process. Besides, we apply a hierarchical structure
where the key idea is to capture both shallow-layer features and
deep-layer features. By aggregating the information from different
layers, our classifier can combine both local and global attention.
To incorporate unlabeled traffic data into training, we adopt a
Mean Teacher-style semi-supervised learning framework to train
the model. Mean Teacher has been successfully adapted to various semi-supervised tasks, such as image segmentation [7], object
detection [5], and behavior analysis [35]. However, the success of
Mean Teacher on various image-related tasks largely resorts to the
combination of a diversity of image augmentation methods and consistency regularization exerted on the augmented unlabeled images.
To adapt this mature semi-supervised paradigm into traffic data
classification, we adopt two data augmentation methods specialized
for encrypted traffic data as shown in Fig. 1: (1) according to the
temporal correlation, take the flow sequence with a timespan (i.e.,
temporal distance) from the original flow sequence, and (2) according to the spatial correlation, use the fusion algorithm to change
the data content. The prediction of the augmented flow sequence
from the student network is encouraged to be consistent with the
prediction of the raw flow sequence from the teacher network.

DL-Based Methods. The identification of encrypted traffic is a typical traffic classification task. Network traffic classification methods
can be divided into three types (i.e., rule-based, ML-based and DLbased) according to their implementation. The rule-based analysis
method can only analyze the uncomplicated network traffic, and
the port number identification has been proved to be invalid [21]
in the complex network environment.
To solve the issues with rule-based analysis methods, researchers
applied to use payload data and statistical features, both of which
are more suitable for identifying specific application scenarios in
complex traffic. However, affected by the traffic encryption technology, only the first few packets that contain handshake information
are typically unencrypted, resulting in limited information for analysis in the payload [26, 28]. In the early stage, the statistical features
of encrypted traffic were analyzed based on ML algorithms [17, 23].
Recently, benefiting from the powerful feature extraction capabilities of DL algorithms, many researchers have proposed DL-based
traffic identification methods with better performance. For instance,
Yang et al. [40] proposed a scheme to extract encrypted traffic features by convolutional neural networks (CNN), and its classification
performance is significantly improved compared to traditional machine learning algorithms. Liu et al. [19] proposed a multi-layer
encoder-decoder structure which can mine the potential sequential
characteristics of flows deeply. Zheng et al. [47] adopted a metalearning approach to learn representative features from the raw
flows and then classifies them in a unified framework.
Although these DL models have achieved great success in the
task of traffic classification, their feature extraction process ignores
the importance of different flows in the flow sequence, which deteriorate the classification performance. Transformer, as the most
effective deep learning model with attention mechanism, is very
suitable for learning and leveraging the importance of different
flows in the flow sequence for feature extraction, but the high
complexity of the traditional Transformer model hinders the deployment on edge devices. Thus, we build an efficient classifier
(FlowFormer) for encrypted traffic classification, which has excellent performance and greatly reduces the model complexity. The
specific structure of our classifier can be seen in Section 4.1.

Semi-Supervised Methods. In the task of traffic classification
through deep learning, obtaining a large amount of labeled data
is a cumbersome and time-consuming process. Therefore, many
researchers have begun to take advantage of unlabeled network
traffic data through semi-supervised learning (SSL), which is more
suitable for practical applications. Rezaei et al. [27] pre-trained
models on unlabeled dataset by fitting statistical features and then
transferred the learned weights to a new model that is re-trained
on labeled dataset. Iliyasu et al. [18] proposed a semi-supervised
learning approach using deep convolutional generative adversarial
network (DCGAN). Aouedi et al. [3] applied stacked sparse autoencoder (SSAE) to construct a semi-supervised model, and pre-trained
the network by reconstructing unlabeled data. Fahad et al. [11]
implemented semi-supervised approach through clustering and
label propagation, and experiments were carried out using sixteen
network traffic datasets. At present, SSL in traffic classification scenarios mainly rely on clustering, label propagation, and generative
models, which often have problems such as insufficient feature
extraction and unstable training.
Consistency regularization is the current research hotspot of
SSL. There are many excellent frameworks for this method, which
have been applied in a wide range of scenarios [5, 7, 14]. Mean
Teacher [11] averages model weights to form a target-generating
teacher model, which has huge advantages in large datasets and
online learning. FixMatch [29] is another advanced method for SSL,
which has achieved state-of-the-art performance across a variety of
standard SSL benchmarks by combining consistency regularization
and pseudo-labeling.
To improve the performance of SSL in traffic classification scenario, a consistency regularization scheme based on the spatialtemporal features of flow sequences is proposed in our method, which
is introduced in Section 4.2.

PRELIMINARIES

In this section, we first give the definition of the encrypted traffic
classification problem. Then, the flow generation process and the
Transformer network are introduced briefly.

3.1

Problem Definition

We consider the problem of encrypted traffic classification as semisupervised classifier learning. Our task is to leverage a few labeled
samples and many unlabeled samples for training a classifier with
excellent performance. To verify the performance and generality of
our classifier for encrypted traffic, we selected two typical encrypted
traffic networks (i.e., virtual private networks and anonymity networks) for analysis. There are three datasets in each encrypted
traffic network: a labeled training set, an unlabeled training set, and
a test set. In our experiment, the test set has fixed sample space, but
the sample space of the labeled training set and unlabeled training
set are determined according to the experimental setting.
Note that the raw traffic flow is time series data, which means
that the data has spatiotemporal relationship between flows [42].
Combining the spatiotemporal relationship of the traffic, we design our baseline model and semi-supervised framework. Specifically, our baseline model is not to analyze a flow but a flow sequence composed of multiple continuous flows. Although many
studies [28, 39, 42] have performed traffic analysis on flow sequences and proved to significantly improve classification performance, they didn’t focus on the temporal correlations between
flows. Therefore, we use the self-attention mechanism of Transformer to allow the interaction between related flows. Moreover,
we adopt a hierarchical structure to extract multiscale features.
The data augmentation strategy of our semi-supervised framework
also takes advantage of the spatiotemporal relationship, which is
applied to unlabeled data. We select temporal augmentation data

3.2

Flow Generation

In packet switching networks, traffic flow is a sequence of packets
carrying information between two hosts. The raw traffic data is
captured and stored in pcap (abbreviation of packet capture) format.
The pcap file mainly contains three parts (i.e., pcap header, packet
header, and packet data). Although the pcap file has complete information, it cannot reflect some statistical features of the flow.
Besides, encryption technology makes it difficult to directly read
the complete information of the pcap file of encrypted traffic. Thus,
it is necessary to calculate and extract the statistical features to
enrich the feature information of each flow.
For traffic mining, the preprocessing and proper mathematical transformation to find invariances are the basis for building
a classifier which performs well in practice. Our method adopts
Tranalyzer21 , a lightweight flow generator and packet analyzer, to
extract the statistical features of the raw traffic. For flows with a
long duration, we segment them according to the maximum duration setting to better meet the real-time requirements of traffic
classification. The extracted features of each flow include a total
of 84 statistical features (e.g., packet length, inter-arrival time, and
flow duration). Since the extraction of statistical features is not the
contribution of this paper, more detailed information about statistical features can be found in the instructions on the official website
of Tranalyzer2. To preserve the spatiotemporal correlations of the
raw traffic, we store the generated flow sequentially according to
the timestamp during extraction. Finally, we take continuous flows
as a flow sequence for encrypted traffic classification, where each
flow contains a total of 84 features.

Discussions

In this section, we further investigate the impact of two hyperparameters in these two data augmentation methods, that are timespan 𝜏 and beta distribution parameter 𝛼 in temporal and spatial
dimension, respectively. To better reflect the effect of different
hyperparameters for each augmentation, we only use one augmentation at a time. Besides, we discuss the impact of the weight of
unsupervised loss.
5.4.1 Parameter 𝜏 for Temporal Augmentation. Timespan 𝜏 represents the temporal distance between two flow sequences. Fig.
5 shows the results of different maximum timespans for temporal augmentation. The longer the temporal distance means the
stronger augmentation and the weaker correlation between the
flow sequences. It can be observed that increasing the maximum
timespan up to 10 or 20 is beneficial and beyond that performance
decreases. Thus it can be concluded that both too short and too long
temporal distance will weaken the effect of temporal augmentation.

CONCLUSION

In this paper, we propose a novel semi-supervised method based on
Transformer and the consistency regularization for encrypted traffic classification. Our key idea is to build an efficient Transformerbased classifier (FlowFormer) with attention mechanism to efficiently extract deep features from flow sequences. We apply Mean
Teacher-style semi-supervised framework to leverage additional
unlabeled data for further improving classification performance,
where temporal and spatial augmentations are adopted as the key
component to explore the temporal and spatial relationship within
the training traffic data. Experimental results on two real-world
traffic datasets demonstrate that the method outperforms state-ofthe-art methods with a large margin.

5.4.2 Parameter 𝛼 for Spatial Augmentation. When performing
spatial augmentation, the interpolation ratio obeys the beta distribution 𝛽 (𝛼, 𝛼). We sample interpolation ratio in it and only retain
values greater than 0.5. In this case, the larger the 𝛼, the stronger
the spatial augmentation. Fig. 6 shows the results of different values
of 𝛼. Results show that stronger spatial augmentation will benefit
classifying traffic, but if 𝛼 is too large, the accuracy will drop a bit.
This is because when 𝛼 is greater than 200, all values sampled in
beta distribution tend to be close to 0.5, which will limit the space
of synthetic samples.
