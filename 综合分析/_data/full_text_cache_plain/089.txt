Privacy-Preserving Tucker Train Decomposition
Over Blockchain-Based Encrypted
Industrial IoT Data
Jun Feng , Laurence Tianruo Yang , Fellow, IEEE, Ronghao Zhang, and Benard Safari Gavuna

Abstract—Tucker decomposition has been widely used
to extract meaningful and underlying data from heterogeneous data generated by different kinds of devices in a wide
range of industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) applications.
IIoT data uploaded to the cloud contain personal and sensitive information; thus, there is a growing concern about
data privacy. Current existing data analysis solutions, however, assume that the data are reliably and securely collected from different IIoT data providers, an assumption that
is not always true in the real world. To address the issues,
in this article we propose a privacy-preserving tucker train
decomposition based on gradient descent over blockchainbased encrypted IIoT data. Specifically, we use blockchain
techniques to enable IIoT data providers to reliably and
securely share their data by encrypting them locally before
recording them in the blockchain. We use tensor train (TT)
theory to build an efficient TT-based tucker decomposition based on gradient descent that tremendously reduces
the number of elements to be updated during the tucker
decomposition. We utilize the massive resources of fogs
and clouds to implement an efficient privacy-preserving
tucker train decomposition scheme. We use homomorphic
encryption to build our scheme that does complete tucker
train decomposition without the involvement of users. Results from a series of extensive experiments on synthetic
datasets and real-world datasets demonstrate that our proposed scheme is efficient.
Index Terms—Blockchain, cloud computing, encrypted
data, industrial IoT (IIoT), privacy protection, tensor, tensor
factorization.

I. INTRODUCTION
HE LAST decades have witnessed an exponential growth
of data as a result of the ever-increasing cutting-edge industrial IoT (IIoT) infrastructures in different application domains,
such as smart homes, smart transportation, and smart cities [1],
[2]. The enormous volume of data has ignited extensive research
both in industry and academia on how to utilize the data to
enhance industrial services provided to users for better and
enhanced living. IIoT has been adopted in many application
areas to help bring better service closer to the end-users. In
health care, for example, personal fitness information recorded
by wearable sensors can be used partly in tucker decomposition
for accurate diagnosis. IIoT systems are distributed, real-time,
adaptive, and intelligent systems involving complex heterogeneous data from hyperspaces. The main focus of IIoT is to integrate and coordinate all data collected from cyber, and physical
spaces to extract useful information used to provide high-quality
personalized services to people as well as optimizing industrial
processes [3], [4].
Smaller samples of data that lack in volume and variety are
usually distributed over different geographical locations, i.e. in
hospitals or smart cities. For efficient feature representations,
however, a large data sample is required. Collecting these smaller
datasets under the current system structure not only poses privacy concerns but also has a high communication overhead that
often prevents the data from being shared. A mechanism for
efficiently aggregating the smaller datasets from different data
providers has long been overdue in IIoT.
The data collected by IIoT devices are mostly sensitive since
they are personal and private data. This, therefore, raises a
number of data integrity, privacy, and ownership concerns as
the data are shared across many participants [5], [6]. For example, sensitive and confidential data may be leaked while being
processed, tampered with, or modified while being shared or
aggregated, and finally, IIoT data providers may no longer have
control of their data since the shared data can be accessed by
many parties, thus enabling it to be replicated by others without
their permission. As a result of the aforementioned data integrity,
privacy, and ownership concerns, many data providers have been
reluctant to share their data for processing [7].
Data privacy concerns in data analysis have witnessed extensive research in both government, industry, and academia.
Current researches have resorted to either using differential

Blockchain is basically an immutable distributed ledger. Owing to its interesting features, such as coordination, record

in society, blockchain can thus work as a public records repository for entire regions, being able to include all documents,
assets, identities, and events. The blockchain was purposely

Even though blockchain has a huge potential going forward,
there are currently challenges on how to incorporate tucker
decomposition in the blockchain. One evident challenge is how
to design an appropriate data format for highly dimensional
data that can easily be accommodated on the blockchain while
protecting data privacy for the individual data providers. Another
challenge is how to construct an accurate tucker decomposition
scheme for blockchain data that can protect the privacy of
sensitive information.
To address these issues, we propose a privacy-preserving
tucker train decomposition (PPTTD) based on gradient descent over blockchain-based encrypted IIoT data. The proposed
scheme uses homomorphic encryption to protect data privacy
in IIoT environments. The additive homomorphic cryptosystem
has a higher efficiency compared to other algorithms when
considering the efficiency in terms of encryption and decryption.
Traditional tucker decomposition optimization algorithms on
plain text needs to update many elements in every iteration; thus,
they would result in many interactions between data providers
and data analysts if they are applied directly on encrypted data.
To minimize the interactions required in processing encrypted
data, we use gradient descent and tensor train (TT) to construct
secure building blocks with homomorphic properties. By using
these building blocks to implement our proposed scheme, the
number of interactions is significantly reduced, thus a reduction
in the overall computation and communication overhead.
Specifically, the main contributions of this article are three
fold, which are as follows.
1) We use blockchain techniques to realize a reliable and
secure IIoT data-sharing platform. IIoT data providers
encrypt their corresponding IIoT data from IIoT devices
in their domain locally before they are recorded in the
blockchain using special formatted transactions.
2) We use TT theory to build an efficient TT-based tucker decomposition based on gradient descent that tremendously
reduces the number of elements that need to be updated
during the gradient-descent tucker decomposition. We
utilize the massive resources of fogs and clouds to present
a privacy-preserving computing model, based on which
we implement an efficient privacy-preserving TT-based

II. PRELIMINARIES
A. Blockchain Systems
A blockchain system is basically an immutable distributed ledger consisting of continuously growing append only
records [10], [11]. Such records include payment transactions,
contractual agreements, product sales, etc. Blockchain was essentially designed to support reliable transactions for untrusted
parties making a transaction. Owing to its disruptive effect, many
kinds of cross-industry open platforms have been proposed,
i.e., Ethereum, Ripple, and HyperLedger, and have seen their
applications in different IIoT scenarios. With regard to access
control, blockchains can be classified as either private, consortium, or public. Blockchain has interesting features, which are
fundamental in society. The following are a few of such features
that make blockchain fit for holding data.
1) Decentralization: Blockchain as a distributed ledger consists of distributed nodes set in a peer-to-peer network, with
each node (peer) maintaining a copy of the blockchain, thus,
no trusted or central administration is required. The participating peers (nodes) validate and relay all transactions in the
blockchain. The data replication in different nodes also eliminates the single point of failure, which are a major problem in
centralized systems.
2) Tamper-Proof: Blockchain utilizes consensus mechanism
to control the addition of new blocks. The consensus mechanism,
i.e., proof of work (PoW), discourages dishonest parties by
making it difficult to perform transactions as a result of, for
example, the computational power needed to accomplish the
task. The nature of PoW is, therefore, to impose computational
overhead in computing and validating transactions to inhibit
block alteration making it practically impossible to manipulate
blockchain transactions.
3) Traceability: Transaction data in a blockchain are easily
verifiable by any authorized party as all transactions are visible
to the public. It is thus possible to track a transaction down to
its source while data owners can enjoy their benefits in real time
since they can easily get paid as their data are being used by
third parties.

C. TT Decomposition
TT decomposition is a new mechanism for approximating
a tensor [13], [14]. Relative to tucker decomposition, TT decomposition can express higher order approximate tensors with
fewer parameters to avoid exponential growth of parameters with
order [15], [16]. Relative to the CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP)
decomposition, TT decomposition is a stable form of expression,
which avoids the NP-hard problem of “optimal rank solution”
in CP decomposition and the problem that CP decomposition
easily falls into local minima. In addition, the TT decomposition
is based entirely on the QR and singular value decomposition
(SVD) decompositions of a series of matrices, without any
recursion. Therefore, TT decomposition is widely used in many
fields.

III. CONSTRUCTION OF PPTTD
In this section, we present in detail our proposed privacypreserving tucker decomposition based on TT over blockchainbased encrypted IIoT data.
A. System Overview
Our framework entails building a PPTTD on top of the
blockchain. The scheme uses data stored in the blockchain,
which are formatted and encrypted by the IIoT data providers
before being saved.
An encrypted tensor is constructed from the data stored in the
blockchain. Using the building blocks, e.g., secure subtraction,

secure tensor outer product, and secure mode-n tensor product,
we construct our privacy-preserving tucker decomposition based
on TT that takes the encrypted tensor as input.
We conceptualize a data-driven IIoT platform as follows, which comprises IIoT devices, IIoT data providers,
cloud-enabled blockchain-based IIoT platform, and privacypreserving tucker decomposition module.
1) IIoT devices can sense and transmit important data from
IIoT ecosystems. The data come from a wide range of applications, e.g., smart cities and smart transport systems.
It is worth noting that IIoT devices have limited storage
and computing power, and thus cannot be used for heavy
computations.
2) IIoT data providers are responsible for collecting valuable
data from all the IIoT devices in their domain. Data from
most of the IIoT ecosystems are sensitive. Therefore, each
IIoT data provider must encrypt its data locally using
homomorphic encryption before recording them in the
blockchain.
3) Cloud-enabled blockchain-based IIoT platform is responsible for storing the encrypted IIoT data from all the IIoT
data providers. The built-in features of the blockchain
make the data stored in the chain to be tamper-proof,
immutable, and authentic, thus can be considered as a
single source of truth. The platform also makes it possible
for multiple data providers to securely share their data.
4) Tensor decomposition module built on top of blockchain
performs insightful analysis using the data stored in
the cloud-enabled blockchain. By performing the secure
tucker decomposition based on TT, underlying interesting
features of the stored IIoT data can be discovered and used
for predicting and offering better service to the IIoT users.
The aim of our framework is to protect the privacy of participating end-users and be able to perform tucker decomposition
securely in IIoT. Specifically, each IIoT data provider’s privacy
is the IIoT records in their domain. In this regard, we formulate
following two security goals.
1) Protection of the privacy of individual IIoT data providers
if confronted by honest but curious adversaries.
2) Protection of the privacy of individual IIoT data providers
if one or more of them collude with IIoT data analysts.
B. Encrypted Data Sharing via Blockchain
In this section, we discuss in detail the process of sharing data
through the blockchain. To get accurate results in our tucker
decomposition step, we assume that the data are processed and
effectively represented as a tensor. For data to be stored in the
blockchain, they have to be formatted into a transaction. Unlike
normal blockchain transactions, our transaction is divided into
two parts, labeled input and output fields. The input field records
the IIoT device type, IIoT data provider address, and encrypted
data while its corresponding output field consists of IIoT device
type, encrypted data, and address of the tucker decomposition
function. Once an IIoT data provider encrypts the IIoT data, the
provider sends the encrypted data to the blockchain for storage,
with the blockchain node receiving the transaction, broadcasting

In our scheme, the user does not participate in the computations of PPTTD after outsourcing the encrypted tensor to fogs
F1 , F2 ,...,Fn and clouds C1 and C2 . The fogs F1 , F2 ,...,Fn and
the cloud C1 only obtain encrypted values, which are gotten by
using the homomorphic cryptosystem. Therefore, the PPTTD
preserves the data owner’s privacy from the fogs F1 , F2 ,...,Fn ,
and the cloud C1 . The cloud C2 only gets blinded values or
random values. Therefore, the data owner’s privacy is also
protected from the cloud C2 in the PPTTD.
In this article, the following formal definition is employed
to describe security against semitrusted adversaries, which
has been widely accepted in the existing privacy-preserving
schemes, such as literatures [17] and [18].
Definition 1: Suppose that xk and yk denote the input and
the output of protocol F for participant Ck , respectively.
Let EImage(F, Ck ) represent the execution image of the
protocol F for participant Ck . Let ≡ represent computational indistinguishability. Then, protocol F is secure against
semitrusted adversaries if EImage(F, Ck ) ≡ SImage(F, Ck ),
where SImage(F, Ck ) is the simulated image of the protocol F for participant Ck and can be simulated from xk and
yk [17], [18].
Theorem 1: The proposed PPTTD scheme is secure in the
semitrusted model.
Proof: Suppose that EImage(PPTTD, C1 ) and SImage
(PPTTD, C1 ) are the execution image and simulated image of the cloud C1 in the PPTTD scheme, respectively.
Then, we can get EImage(PPTTD, C1 ) = {[T ]S , mk1 |1 ≤ k ≤
K}, where [T ]S = T − [T ]D , and mk1 denotes the message that the cloud C1 receives from the cloud C2 in the
PPTTD scheme. Let E(X) denote any element of tensor
X. SImage(PPTTD, C1 ) = {Rs , r1k |1 ≤ k ≤ K}, where Rs is
picked random tensor, and E(Rs ) ∈ ZN . Because [T ]D is
one random tensor and E([T ]D ) ∈ ZN , we get [T ]S ≡ Rs .
Since the basic protocols used in this article are secure,
we can call the corresponding simulator to simulate mk1
for 1 ≤ k ≤ K. We can get mk1 ≡ r1k for 1 ≤ k ≤ K. As a
result EImage(PPTTD, C1 ) ≡ SImage(PPTTD, C1 ). Suppose
that EImage(PPTTD, C2 ) and SImage(PPTTD, C2 ) are the execution image and simulated image of the cloud C2 . Similarly,
we can get EImage(PPTTD, C2 ) ≡ SImage(PPTTD, C2 ). Suppose that EImage(PPTTD, Fj ) and SImage(PPTTD, Fj ) are the

IV. PERFORMANCE EVALUATION
We evaluate, in this section, the performance of PPTTD based
on gradient descent over blockchain-based encrypted IIoT data.
We specifically evaluate our method in terms of efficiency and
accuracy using a series of extensive experiments.
Each IIoT data provider collects information from all IIoT
devices in its own domain and preprocess the data before sending them to the blockchain for storage. Preprocessing entails
aggregation, encryption, and formatting data according to the
designed transaction structure. Since IIoT data providers have
considerable computing capabilities compared to IIoT devices,
we simulate our experiments using Windows 10 PCs with Intel(R) Core(TM) (i5-7500 U) processor and 16 GB RAM for
both executing the IIoT data provider functions and running the
proposed scheme.
Based on the aforementioned experimental platform, this
article implements security operations subprotocols involved
in the fog–cloud security calculation model, such as tensor secure single-mode multiplication protocol, TT secure subtraction
protocol, and tensor secure outer product protocol. Based on
these, PPTTD is implemented. At the same time in reference
to this model, we also implement privacy-preserving tucker decomposition based on gradient descent for comparison with our
proposed method. Finally, we implement the proposed algorithm
into a distributed environment, simulate different numbers of fog
end nodes, and test the calculation cost of the “fog–cloud computing layer.” The specific experimental results are discussed in
detail.

There are quite a number of techniques that have been used
across many fields to protect the privacy of data [1]. One such
technique is data anonymization [19], a method that attempts
to protect personal and sensitive information from being easily
identified. This method, however, is prone to numerous types of
attacks especially if the attacker has background knowledge.
Another method for protecting data and user privacy is DP
[20], a technique that ensures data privacy by perturbing the
data or adding noise to the computational process before the data
are shared. The technique allows multiple parties to compute a
joint function without revealing their sensitive input information. DP-based techniques usually achieves higher accuracy in
protecting data privacy at the expense of reducing the quality
of data, thus analysis based on the perturbed data can lead to
inaccurate results.
To achieve better guarantee in data privacy protection, homomorphic cryptosystem is used to compute on encrypted data. Homomorphic encryption allows direct computation on encrypted
data while making sure that the computations are correct. Several
homomorphic encryption based algorithms have been proposed
in literatures, i.e., [5].
B. Blockchain for IoT
There has been ongoing research on how to make IoT
blockchain-enabled to improve IoT systems’ privacy [21], [22].
Many of the previous researches have focused on access control,
secure applications, cloud data storage, and cloud computations.
The introduction of Bitcoin brought about numerous possibilities on how to use blockchain technology in both financial
and nonfinancial applications. In the recent past, there has been
several attempts to use the blockchain technology in nonfinancial

VI. CONCLUSION
In this article, we presented a novel PPTTD-based on gradient
descent over blockchain-based encrypted IIoT data, which handles data privacy challenges by using blockchain techniques.
We utilized secure TT-based tucker decomposition to process
blockchain-based encrypted data collected from various IIoT
environments. In our approach, homomorphic encryption was
used to construct the secure TT-based tucker decomposition.
We finally demonstrated from experiments the security and
efficiency of our proposed approach.
Part of our future work is to develop a general framework
for implementing different types of privacy-preserving tensor
decomposition using data from multiple IIoT.
