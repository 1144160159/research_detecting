The Onion Router (TOR) network is a decentralized system of volunteer-run servers that aims to protect the
anonymity and privacy of users by routing their internet traﬃc through a series of nodes. Individuals who use
the TOR network may employ obfuscated traﬃc to conceal their internet activity from network administrators or
security systems attempting to block or monitor them. Furthermore, some may use obfuscated Tor traﬃc to hide
illegal activities, such as buying and selling illegal goods or accessing illegal services on the dark web. Despite
eﬀorts to identify and block Tor traﬃc, challenges remain, such as a limited set of features for identiﬁcation,
leading to false positives and negatives. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a novel approach
using Visual Transformation (ViT), and augmentation by Bidirectional Generative Adversarial Networks (BiGAN).
The proposed approach demonstrates superior performance on the ISCX-Tor2016 dataset, achieving 99.59%
accuracy, 99.83% recall, 99.72% precision, and 99.78% F-score, thereby outperforming current state-of-the-art
techniques.

2. Related works
There has been a growing body of research on the detection of obfuscated Tor traﬃc using deep learning techniques. One approach is the
use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction. For
example, in the work of Rust-Nguyen and Stamp (2022), the authors
proposed a CNN-based method for detecting obfuscated Tor traﬃc.
They used CNN to extract features from the network traﬃc and employed a Support Vector Machine (SVM) to classify the traﬃc as Tor
or non-Tor. The proposed method achieved high accuracy in detecting obfuscated Tor traﬃc. Another approach is the use of Recurrent
Neural Networks (RNNs) for feature extraction. Moreover, the study in
Sarkar et al. (2020) introduced a ﬂexible approach called AI-FlowDet,
a CNN-based ﬂow-splitting method that leverages the concept of scene
change detection. The method demonstrated notable improvements in
ﬂow application type classiﬁcation. Besides that, AI-FlowDet utilized
the UNB dataset for its experiments and introduced a comprehensive
set of 294 size-based and direction-based features speciﬁcally designed
for application-type characteristics (Chen and Lin, 2021).
In the work of Wang and Chen (2019), the authors proposed an
RNN-based method for detecting obfuscated Tor traﬃc. They used a
long short-term memory (LSTM) network to extract features from the
network traﬃc and then used a Decision Tree (DT) to classify the traﬃc
as Tor or non-Tor. The proposed method achieved high accuracy in
detecting obfuscated Tor traﬃc. A third approach is the use of transfer
learning to improve the performance of detection. In this study (Sarkar
et al., 2020), the authors proposed a transfer learning-based method for
detecting obfuscated Tor traﬃc. They used a pre-trained Deep Neural
Network (DNN) model as a feature extractor and then ﬁne-tuned the
model on a small dataset of Tor traﬃc. The proposed method achieved
high accuracy in detecting obfuscated Tor traﬃc.
Furthermore, Twelve features were extracted from packets using
a sliding window technique by Xu and Zou (2021). The obfuscated
Tor traﬃc was classiﬁed into three protocol obfuscation types: Meek,
Obfs4, and FTE, using seven machine learning models: Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT), Random Forest (RF), Classiﬁcation and Regression Trees (CART) decision

Okonkwo et al. (2022) proposed a CNN model with eleven layers
to classify the encrypted network traﬃc in HTTPs, VPN, and Tor. The
model transformed network packets into unique images for each trafﬁc class, based on packet size and arrival time features. The proposed
model addressed over- and under-ﬁtting issues but used a simple technique to deal with the imbalance issue. However, the model achieved
higher accuracy for VPN traﬃc than Tor traﬃc, and it was not able to
classify audio and chat traﬃc accurately. Zhao et al. (2019) presented
the MT-DNN-FL model, which stands for Multi-Task Deep Neural Network in Federated Learning (FL), to detect anomaly traﬃc in VPN and
Tor. The paper compared this model with single-task models such as
DNN, C4.5 DT, LR, KNN, and RF. The MT-DNN-FL model consisted of
three layers: (i) Input layer, (ii) Shared layers, which performed feature
extraction and multi-task settings in DNN, and (iii) Task layers, which
comprised subnetworks for each speciﬁc task. Although the study discovered that the multi-task model surpassed the single-task model in
DNN performance, the employment of an imbalanced dataset in the experiment may have inﬂuenced the model’s eﬀectiveness. Besides that,
optimizing the DNN while maintaining training performance presents
potential challenges, which could be arduous or require an extended
duration to achieve.
Furthermore, the study conducted in Abu Al-Haija et al. (2022) explored and assessed six supervised machine learning methods employed
in Darknet Traﬃc Detection Systems (DTDS) speciﬁcally designed for
IoT networks. The techniques encompass bagging decision tree ensembles (BAG-DT), AdaBoost Decision Tree ensembles (ADA-DT), RUSBoosted Decision Tree ensembles (RUS-DT), Optimizable Decision Tree
(O-DT), Optimizable K-Nearest Neighbor (O-KNN), and Optimizable
Discriminant (O-DSC). Among these methods, BAG-DT demonstrated
superior performance relative to the other techniques by eﬀectively
classifying four categories that include VPN, non-VPN, Tor, and non-Tor
traﬃc. While in Marim et al. (2023), the authors addressed the challenges of detecting and characterizing Darknet traﬃc using decision
tree-based learning models, including DT, RF, and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), which achieve high classiﬁcation accuracy. Moreover, the
paper utilized the Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE) algorithm to select the most relevant features for model prediction. Although simple
machine learning algorithms, such as decision tree-based models, can
provide competitive results for real-world problems, they may face issues related to real-time prediction.

3. Methodology
The main emphasis of this section is on elaborating on the proposed
approach for detecting obfuscated traﬃc. The approach comprises four
primary phases: (i) analyzing and comprehending the dataset, (ii) preprocessing the dataset, (iii) addressing the imbalance issue using data
augmentation via the BiGAN model, and (iv) detecting obfuscated trafﬁc using the ViT model, as illustrated in Fig. 2.
3.1. Dataset description
ISCXTor2016 dataset is a collection of network traﬃc data that was
gathered by the Information Security Center of Excellence (ISCX) at the
University of New Brunswick in Canada in 2016. The dataset contains
both normal and Tor traﬃc and consists of approximately 2.5 million
ﬂows captured over a period of 2 weeks. These ﬂows were captured
from various network interfaces and devices such as laptops, smartphones, wired and wireless interfaces, and servers (Palmieri, 2019;
Vasan and Surendiran, 2016; Jiang et al., 2022). The dataset is labeled,
it includes normal traﬃc, primarily from web browsing, ﬁle transfer,
and email activities, and Tor traﬃc which can include both legitimate
and malicious activities like ﬁle sharing, anonymous communication,
and command and control of malware. ISCXTor2016 dataset is commonly used in research on the detection of Tor traﬃc as it provides
a large and diverse set of data that can be used to train and evaluate
detection methods (Zhao et al., 2019; Lin et al., 2021). However, it’s
important to keep in mind that the dataset is not recent, and the Tor
network and its usage may have changed since 2016.
The ISCXTor2016 dataset, also known as the Tor-nonTor dataset,
is divided into two scenarios, A and B, based on time features and includes ﬁve diﬀerent ﬂow-timeout values: 10 s, 15 s, 30 s, 60 s, and
120 s. Scenario A is a binary classiﬁcation task that distinguishes traﬃc
between Tor and NonTor, as presented in Table 2. However, Scenario A
has achieved high-performance results in prior works, such as in Johnson et al. (2021); Okonkwo et al. (2022).
On the other hand, Scenario B is to ﬁnd out the type of Tor traﬃc
as one of eight categories (multi-classiﬁcation). Moreover, Scenario B
is still suﬀering from the slight performance hit of multi-classiﬁcation.
Table 3 presents eight classes for Tor traﬃc (Lashkari et al., 2017; Yin
et al., 2022).
Indeed, Tor traﬃc is related to TCP protocol where it does not
support UDP protocol, thus ISCXTor2016 dataset has TCP traﬃc with
bidirectional ﬂows: forward packet (source to destination) and backward packet (destination to source). Ultimately, there are 23 features
that calculated based on statistical time-related features have been provided in the ISCXTor2016 dataset, and the detail in Table 4 (Lashkari
et al., 2017). In particular, Active Mean, Active Std, Active Max, Active Min, Idle Mean, Idle Std, Idle Max, and Idle Min features have been
eliminated, because the most value of these features is zero which leads
to noise for Obfuscated traﬃc detection. The next section presents the
data preprocessing in detail.

To address data imbalances in training and testing, heavy data augmentation was applied to the images. Therefore, in the proposed model,
data augmentation was implemented using a BiGAN on image data generated from the previous preprocessing phase. The BiGAN architecture
comprises three primary components: (i) Encoder, which is a neural
network that maps an input data sample to a low-dimensional representation or embedding; (ii) Generator, which is a neural network that
generates synthetic data samples similar to the original data from the
latent code (Kaplan and Alptekin, 2020; Xu et al., 2022). The generator is trained to produce synthetic samples that cannot be distinguished
from real data samples by the discriminator; and (iii) Discriminator,
which is a neural network that evaluates the authenticity of the generated samples. The discriminator is trained to diﬀerentiate between real
and synthetic data samples and to reconstruct the original data sample
from its embedding. Fig. 5 illustrates the architecture of BiGAN (Yang
and Li Bigan, 2021; Ding et al., 2020).
In BiGAN, an encoder (E) component has been added to the typical
GAN structure, transforming the input data into a latent space representation. The discriminator (D) receives both the latent representation

3.4. Obfuscated traﬃc detection phase
The ViT model is a type of deep learning model that was introduced
for image classiﬁcation tasks in 2020 by Dosovitskiy et al. ViT was originally developed for Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks where the
main idea behind ViT is to convert an image into a sequence of patches
and then process them using a transformer-based architecture as shown
in Fig. 7. The transformer model is able to capture long-range dependencies between the patches and produce a ﬁxed-length feature vector that
represents the image. Moreover, the ViT model has demonstrated impressive performance on several image classiﬁcation benchmarks and
has been noted for its ability to handle large-scale image recognition
tasks while being computationally eﬃcient (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Li
et al., 2023).
The architecture of ViT is designed to process the input image
patches and learn complex relationships between them using selfattention mechanisms in the transformer layers. However, ViT does
involve a form of feature extraction in the sense that it extracts image patches from the input image and converts them into embedding
vectors, which are then processed by the transformer layers. These
embedding vectors can be considered low-level features, and the transformer layers learn higher-level representations of these features (Paul
and Chen, 2022; Chen et al., 2022).

The combination of BiGAN and ViT, two deep learning architectures,
has exhibited the potential to improve the performance of Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS). BiGAN is a generative adversarial network that
can learn both the generator and discriminator networks bidirectionally, resulting in the capture of complex and high-dimensional data
distributions. This property has made BiGAN a suitable candidate for
learning the underlying data augmentation to increase the network
traﬃc and assist other models in detecting traﬃc patterns, thereby increasing the detection rates, reducing the false positive rates of IDS, and
overcoming the overﬁtting issue. Similarly, ViT is a class of deep neural networks that can capture long-range dependencies in image data
by utilizing self-attention mechanisms. ViT has demonstrated remarkable performance in image classiﬁcation tasks and has been extended
to IDS, where ViT-based models can learn to extract features from network traﬃc and detect anomalies in traﬃc patterns. The self-attention
mechanism in ViT-based IDS models facilitates the capture of the dependencies between network packets and the identiﬁcation of patterns
associated with malicious activities. Furthermore, researchers have explored the combination of BiGAN and ViT in IDS, which has shown
promise in achieving even higher accuracy and detection rates by using BiGAN to learn the underlying data distribution and ViT to extract
relevant features from the network traﬃc. Fig. 9 depicts a visual representation of the experimental results obtained in this paper.

the detection of obfuscated Tor traﬃc using deep learning could involve
the utilization of recurrent neural networks to examine network traﬃc
