Abstract—With more and more encrypted traffic such as
HTTPS, encrypted traffic protects not only normal traffic, but
also malicious traffic. Identification of encrypted malicious traffic
without decryption has become a research hotspot. Combined
with deep learning, an important branch of machine learning,
encrypted malicious traffic detection has achieved good results.
This paper reviews the detection of encrypted malicious traffic
in recent years. Firstly, we classify encrypted malicious traffic.
Secondly, we sorts out the extraction characteristics of encrypted
malicious traffic, the key and difficult problems we are facing at
present. Then, with encrypted malicious traffic detection technology as the main line, we summarized the current detection model
from the four core aspects of data collection, data processing,
model training and evaluation improvement. Finally, we analyze
the problems and point out future research directions.
Index Terms—HTTPS; Deep Learning; Encrypted Malicious
Traffic; Feature Extraction; Detecting Techniques

B. HTTPS
HTTPS is also known as Hyper Text Transfer Protocol
over Secure Socket Layer. That is, the Secure Sockets Layer
(SSL) layer is added under Hyper Text Transfer Protocol
(HTTP), so the security base for HTTPS is Secure Sockets
Layer/Transport Layer Security (SSL/TLS). It uses port 443
as the communication port by default, aiming to complete
the communication that can only be encrypted and decrypted
by client and server by using symmetric key and asymmetric
key through Certificate Authority (CA) which is the public
trust organization. The detailed handshake process is shown

II. M ALICIOUS T RAFFIC C LASSIFICATION
A. Malicious traffic classification
Scientific classification of malicious traffic is the key to
extract malicious traffic characteristics so as to identify it,
because different types of malicious traffic have different
network behavior patterns. We need to select the corresponding feature extraction methods and identification methods
according to the types of malicious traffic. However, due to
the different research directions and different perspectives of
researchers on malicious traffic, there is no uniform standard
for the classification of malicious traffic. The JuMing classifies
malicious traffic into 11 categories[10]. Anquanniu divided
it into three main classes, among which there are several
subclasses[11]. Yongan divides malicious traffic into collision
library, cheating, malicious crawlers and other categories,
among which the most are crawlers[12]. Rutkowska only
classifies malicious traffic into four categories[13]. Malicious
traffic is divided into four categories and 54 subcategories by
Guancheng technology. Gang Lu and others classify malicious
traffic into nine categories according to the attack effect of
malicious traffic[14]. This paper classifies malicious traffic
according to its usage and divides it into two categories: malicious software and malicious attack. The specific categories
and corresponding descriptions are shown in Fig.2.

D. Current status of encrypted malicious traffic detection
At present, the detection of encrypted malicious traffic
mainly includes two ways according to the realization subject.
One is to extract its features in a manual way for detection.

for detection. This method is extremely labor-intensive, but
relatively accurate and easy to implement. The other is an
approach that has emerged in recent years, machine learning
in artificial intelligence, Moreover, this method has made
some achievements in image processing[5], speech translation[6], scene analysis[7], pattern recognition[8] and other
fields, showing its superior performance. It can reduce human
resources to some extent, and its accuracy can also be verified
on the known data set. Detection methods can be divided into
signature detection method and anomaly detection method[9].

III. M ALICIOUS T RAFFIC F EATURE
A. Classification of features
Feature extraction, which relates to the detection accuracy
and detection progress, is the most important part in the
detection of encrypted malicious traffic. The selection of good
features can help researchers analyze performance, shorten
the analysis and groping time, and help to understand the
implementation method and operation principle of encrypted
malicious traffic detection. However, there is no unified standard and regulation for the characteristics of malicious traffic
in the industry, so the classification of features is not unique.
As discussed by Ferreira et al.[17], there is no clear uniform standard for analyzing network traffic except for the
case of very specific traffic. Some people divide malicious
traffic feature extraction into two categories, including purely
statistical-based and content-based features. Others have classified feature engineering into four categories: spatiotemporal
features, handshake features, background features and certificate features. Of course, the information collected, the
extracted features and the selected algorithms are different
for different research purposes. Traffic usually consists of 5
tuples[18], which are source IP, target IP, source port, target
port and protocol. Generally speaking, traffic characteristics
can be divided into three categories: content characteristics,
data traffic statistical characteristics and network connection
behavior characteristics[14]. The content characteristics of
malicious traffic, which are also the most reliable features,
mainly include the unique values of malicious traffic protocols
and some very special characters and sequences contained in
their payloads. Malicious data flow statistical characteristics
and network connection behavior characteristics are the abovementioned extraction methods based solely on statistical characteristics, which are described separately because there are
still obvious differences between the two, but in fact, they
are obtained from the statistical analysis of the collected data.
Among them, the process of extracting statistical features of
malicious data streams is generally to calculate the statistical
values of data streams first, and then to extract malicious traffic
characteristics based on these statistics, which is basically in
the network layer, transport layer and application layer. In
addition, network connection features are also generated by
some malicious software, such as random scanning of IP,
and generating a large number of failed connection worm

infections, etc. Different people have different classification
methods for flow characteristics. Patel et al.[19] collected basic
features, content features, 42 features based on time and host
traffic. Meghdouri et al.[20] used the random forest method
to analyze the performance of five feature sets, and it can
also be divided into connection features, SSL features and
certificate features. Villeneuve et al.[21] analyzed the content
characteristics of Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) traffic.
Lee et al.[22] proposed a method to automatically extract
attack features, which can reduce the false positive rate very
well.
B. TLS feature
As mentioned above, HTTPS can realize encrypted information transmission mainly because SSL/TLS is its security
basis. Then when it comes to malicious traffic characteristics,
we have to talk about the characteristics of TLS, which is
generated on the basis of the SSL security protocol, and
they are juxtaposed. Benign traffic has a unique TLS feature,
which is absolutely impossible to be the same as malicious
traffic. Therefore, given the universality and practicability
of TLS protocol, TLS-based statistical feature extraction for
encrypted malicious traffic has become a hot topic of current
scholars’ research. Anderson et al.[23] extracted the features of
malicious TLS handshake traffic. TLS features mainly include
the provided password group, the length of the client public
key, TLS expansion and the password suite provided by the
server, etc. These can be captured in requests sent to the server
by the client when collecting traffic.
Blake Anderson et al.[23] found that the difference between
normal traffic and malicious traffic on TLS is mainly in expansion and password suite. Most malicious traffic uses outdated
password suite, such as 2048-bit RSA public key, while normal
traffic uses 256-bit elliptic curve to encrypt public key. This
provides a theoretical basis for correct classification.
C. Difficult problems of feature extraction
At present, the difficult problems of feature extraction of
encrypted malicious traffic mainly focus on the following
aspects:
First, as mentioned above, because there are many and
complex variants of the malicious software family, which
will lead to continuous changes in its network behavior,
how to determine the common and accurate characteristics of
malicious traffic is one of the difficulties of current research.
Malicious encrypted traffic detection with incremental learning
is the key to solve such problems.
Second, in the context of the rapid development of information technology, the network throughput is so high that
it is difficult to record all the statistical information in the
network, that is, if the detection speed does not keep up with
the transmission speed of network data, then the data packets
in it will be missed, resulting in unreported and affecting the
system performance.
Third, the features that are being used such as TLS features,
packet features and so on are indeed effective in detecting

IV. E NCRYPTED MALICIOUS TRAFFIC DETECTION
TECHNOLOGY

The essence of encrypted malicious traffic detection technology is information processing. Because of the popularity of
encrypted traffic and the invisibility of load information, it is
no longer remote to identify malicious encrypted traffic without decryption. Overcoming a large number of malicious traffic
threats and challenges, and combining the advantages of reinforcement learning in artificial intelligence, a variety of machine learning-based detection methods have been proposed.
Shahbaz Rezaei et al.[24] proposed a general framework for
flow classification based on deep learning. They generalize the
encrypted malicious traffic detection process into seven steps.
This general model is already very representative and adapts to
many algorithm models, but it does not cover unknown traffic,
that is, classifiers fail to cover unrecognized traffic. Wang et
al.[25] introduced an end-to-end encrypted traffic classification
method model based on one-dimensional convolution neural
network, which centralizes data processing into a unified endto-end framework for cut processing, and then learns the
characteristics by itself for classification. Derek Manning et
al.[26] proposed an accelerated cryptographic traffic analysis
system that utilizes an x86 multi-core platform and software
accelerator configured at the edge of an enterprise network to
minimize training and reasoning time while maintaining accuracy. Tangda Yu et al.[27] proposed an encrypted malicious
traffic detection system based on multi-AEs (Autoencoder),
which analyzed the characteristics of cryptographic protocols
from handshake to authentication. For better classification,
they also propose that the flow characteristics should be
extracted further by extending the flow eigenvectors to higher
positions, with higher accuracy. Wajdi Bazuhair et al.[28]
uniquely proposed a new encoding method, which converts
all the numerical data models into images when extracting
features, and convolution neural networks are used as training
and testing datasets after converting the connection features to
images. It is worth mentioning that the data model carrier uses
data enhancement methods, and uses Perlin noise as a carrier
function to enhance the network traffic characteristic image to
enhance the generalization of the depth model. The method
of converting a numerical data model into an image is really
novel, and the result of detection is sufficient. Incidentally,
Danie et al.[29] concentrated on executable files, converted
binary files into gray images, and classified binary data
of malware samples using convolution neural network with
94.64% accuracy. It illustrates the role of convolution neural
networks in detecting and classifying malicious software. Zhai
Mingfang et al.[30] proposed a ”six-step” model based on

B. Data processing
Data processing includes data preprocessing and feature
extraction. Feature extraction has been introduced in detail in
the previous chapter, so the following mainly describes data
preprocessing. In order to ensure that the data set is complete
and free of impurities, it is necessary to further process the
original data set, that is, to conduct deep analysis and sorting,
on this basis, to clean, integrate, mine, label and so on, so
as to ensure the training quality of the later AI model, and
make it become the data set meeting the requirements of deep
learning test. Data discretization is also a very effective and
efficient way to process[38]. Chen Tieming et al.[39] proposed
a new method of sample enhancement based on key word

bank avoidance to process data for sample enhancement. Li
Jia et al.[40] proposed a data preprocessing method based
on semantic tailoring, adaptive field partition and statistical
correlation. The idea is to transform the traffic data into matrix
data, and then normalize the data into text information. Luo
Ziming et al.[41] used the relief algorithm to preprocess the
data to reduce the data dimension and improve the accuracy of
the model. Relief algorithm is a feature weighting algorithm
that gives different weights to features based on the correlation
of each feature and category, and features with weights less
than a certain threshold are removed. There are many more
ways to preprocess data, so different preprocessing methods
should be chosen according to the model.
C. Model training
The model is the main part of malicious encryption traffic
detection technology, and it is also the most important part
of traffic detection. At present, various algorithm models have
been proposed to detect encrypted traffic in different situations.
Shekhawat et al.[42] compared three machine learning algorithms, namely SVM, XGBoost and random forest, and used
these three algorithms to train and test models for feature analysis. The result is that XGBoost performs slightly better than
Random Forest, both with an accuracy of about 99%, while
SVM performs relatively poorly. Baek et al.[43] proposed a
kind of deep learning model based on which the average recall
rate is 4.6 times of the existing model. Due to the maturity of
engineering, different machine learning algorithms are used in
different scenarios and then Guancheng Science and Technology[10] compared linear regression (LinReg), Random Forest
(Random Forest), Decision Tree (DecTree), Neural Network
(MLP), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logical Regression
(LogReg), Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and finally
found that random forest had the best comprehensive effect.
Model selection is to select the appropriate model for the
experimental goal in order to achieve the best detection results
achieved by the existing technology.
If the model of neural network is used, a loss function
needs to be defined when training the network as the direction of optimization parameters. At present, the neural
network which studies the classification problem generally
uses cross-entropy as the loss function[44]. After defining the
loss function, each time the neural network is trained, the
network parameters are updated by feedback after forward
calculation. This process generally adopts back propagation
algorithm (BP algorithm)[45]. In addition, it should be pointed
out that over-fitting is a fundamental problem in deep learning,
which refers to too much pursuit of the indicators of the
training set, resulting in unsatisfactory performance in the test
set. The main methods to reduce over-fitting are increasing
training data, simplifying models, regularization, integrated
learning methods and DropOut[46]. Researchers can choose
appropriate methods to reduce over-fitting according to the
actual situation. In order to avoid the contingency of the
experiment, the N-fold cross-validation method is used in most
experiments. The common method is ten-fold cross-validation,

D. Evaluate improvements
After the model training is completed, it is usually necessary
to evaluate the model. However, this evaluation does not
put the model into a real network environment to test its
performance, because security and other costs are too high.
Usually a certain evaluation index evaluation model is specified, including accuracy and real-time. In the classification
algorithm, the following are several basic indicators[47]: true
Positive case (TP) refers to the number of normal samples
that are correctly identified, true negative case (TN) refers to
the number of malicious samples that are correctly identified,
false positive case (FP) refers to the number of normal samples
that are misidentified and false negative case (FN) refers to
the number of malicious samples that are misidentified, and
their combination can represent the accuracy of the model.

V. S UMMARY AND P ROSPECT
With the popularity of encrypted traffic, attackers also use
encrypted traffic to realize their illegal intentions accordingly,
which endangers the privacy security of network users. The
importance of detection technology is becoming increasingly
prominent. We should tell the illegal molecule in reality that
the network is not an illegal place[50]. The detection of
encrypted malicious traffic has made considerable progress,
and its framework has been summarized by many researchers,
so defending against malicious encryption traffic is no longer
a fantasy. However, there are also some problems, or this is the
main direction of the next research on malicious decryption
traffic detection:
• Data sets: At present, most datasets are non-public
datasets, which causes the experiment can’t be reproduced, the same algorithm using different datasets results
are biased, the different algorithms can’t be compared
among other issues, the real-time performance of public
datasets is weak, and so on. Therefore, how to construct
an accepted markup method to produce a relatively perfect and easy-to-implement dataset needs further study.
• Feature extraction of encrypted malicious traffic: Although the traditional statistical features of encrypted
malicious traffic have achieved relatively good results,
relying solely on these features is far from enough to
combat encrypted malicious traffic. How to mine more
effective and direct statistical features is still a difficult
point in current research, which requires a lot of manpower and resources.
• applicability of encrypted traffic detection technology:
Currently, researchers mainly focus on experimental algorithms and can’t put them into the actual network
for detection, because the real network has considerable
uncertainty. So how to turn theory into reality is also a
big problem.
• deep learning problems: As far as in-depth learning
is concerned, some achievements have been made in
the application of encrypted malicious traffic detection,
