XAI-EdgeSFL: Explainable Edge Intelligence
With Adaptive Intrusion-Resilient Split
Federated Learning for Consumer
Healthcare Ecosystems
Yue Zhao , Farhan Ullah , Khalid Mahmood , Senior Member, IEEE, Saqib Saeed , Senior Member, IEEE,
Nazeeruddin Mohammad , Senior Member, IEEE, and Umar Raza , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—The fast growth of consumer electronics, particularly Internet of Things (IoT) devices and wearables, has
significantly changed healthcare systems by enabling real-time
monitoring and customized medical treatment. Edge computing
is changing healthcare by enabling the secure and distributed
processing of data on consumer devices. However, this integration
presents substantial security issues, raising significant concerns
regarding AI-driven decision-making, model understandability,
and privacy protection. This paper introduces XAI- EdgeSFL,
a novel framework that integrates Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) with an Adaptive Intrusion-Resilient Split Federated
Learning (SFL) architecture to provide reliable and transparent
edge health monitoring. The SFL loop computes important
features of sensitive health data using a lightweight Temporal
Convolutional Network (TCN) on edge devices and a more complex classifier on a global server. This method enhances privacy
by retaining raw data on the device. In addition, we present
an adaptive aggregation approach that dynamically weights
client model updates to evaluate intrinsic resilience against
emerging cyberthreats. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP),
Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME), and
Integrated Gradient (IG) are a multi-modal XAI suite that offers
feature-level explanations for clinical trust. The proposed method
performs well across all datasets, achieving 86.15% accuracy
and 86.01% F1-score on CICIoMT2024, 93.73% accuracy and
93.65% F1-score on N-BaIoT, and 99.46% accuracy and 99.47%
F1-score on CICIDS2017. The results show that XAI-EdgeSFL
improves security, privacy, and transparency for AI-driven consumer healthcare systems.

I. I NTRODUCTION

T

HE rapid digital transformation in healthcare, driven by
IoT, has allowed for continuous patient monitoring and
highly personalized care. The development of interconnected
ecosystems that generate a continuous stream of healthcare
data has been facilitated by the extensive adoption of consumer electronics, including smartwatches, fitness monitors,
and medical IoT devices [1]. Decentralized health surveillance
has the potential to substantially improve clinical outcomes
by using real-time data analysis and proactive intervention
strategies.
The concept of edge computing has become a leading
paradigm for facilitating efficient, privacy-preserving, distributed data processing near consumer devices. It reduces
latency and saves bandwidth by allowing local analysis, which
helps to solve privacy concerns that arise when sensitive medical information is frequently transmitted to centralized cloud
systems. However, the decentralized and broad presence of
healthcare ecosystems makes them vulnerable to cyberattacks
[2]. An effective intrusion may compromise or modify important health alarms, which may directly harm patient safety.
It, therefore, becomes imperative to prioritize the security of
such systems. The centralized structure of traditional Intrusion
Detection Systems (IDS) may have difficulties adapting to this
environment. These systems face two main challenges [3].
First, they need to handle distributed data without violating
the key privacy principles of edge computing. Second, the
low explainability of modern AI-based models often leads to
closed system decision-making, which provides little understanding of the rationale behind specific activities, which are
labeled malicious.
Federated Learning (FL), which supports the joint training
of models across devices and protects raw data, is a way of
addressing data privacy issues. Nevertheless, traditional FL
methods may be computationally heavy to run on edge devices
with limited resources, which makes them susceptible to model
poisoning attacks by malicious agents [4]. SFL presents a
distinct benefit of dividing the model into client and server

have interpretable outputs. The obscurity of such AI-based
systems in the clinical environment impedes the capacity of

The proposed XAI-EdgeSFL framework enhances security, privacy, and transparency in healthcare edge systems.

server for efficiency. The design improves resistance to model
poisoning and uses XAI tools like SHAP for interpretability,
increasing clinical trust. The following are the main research
contributions.
1) We present XAI-EdgeSFL, an innovative architecture
for consumer healthcare devices with limited resources,
which integrates XAI with adaptive intrusion-resilient
SFL. The security and transparency challenges in
distributed healthcare systems are addressed by this

2) We develop a lightweight TCN for deployment on

II. R ELATED W ORK
Traditional machine learning-based methods of threat detection in healthcare devices are rapidly being substituted by

edge-based FL approaches. This progression solves major
shortcomings of centralized approaches, notably with regard
to data privacy and the computational restrictions of consumer
medical devices [2], [6], [9]. The following subsections critically evaluate several security measures, focusing on the shift
from cloud-based to distributed intelligence employing edge
computing, FL, and SFL.
A. Edge Computing
Lin et al. [10] examine the allocation of resources in
edge computing and the architecture of IDS. The proposed
system effectively optimizes the allocation and sharing of
heterogeneous resources. A single-layer dominant and maxmin fair (SDMMF) allocation technique ensures hierarchical
fairness within a broad edge-based IDS. Allocation of multiple
resources across layers is handled using a method called
Multilayer Dominant and Max-Min Fair (MDMMF). Cui et al.
[11] improve edge computing intrusion detection using gradient boosting decision trees by addressing data imbalance,
high dimensionality, and inefficient parameter tuning. The
offered approach uses Margin SMOTE to introduce balanced
data generation and a recursive feature removal hierarchy to
select features. Also, a grid search method is optimized to a
customized one to optimize the efficiency of the parameters.
Edge computing technology brings significant time improvements to a real-time environment and occupies less bandwidth.
Singh et al. [12] suggest the creation of a smart healthcare
system based on an edge computing paradigm. The solution
proposes an intermediate edge layer that minimizes the network latency, and patient data privacy is guaranteed by using
privacy-preserving searchable encryption and an access control
system to validate illegal access to data. Eskandari et al. [13]
introduce a smart edge-based intrusion detection system that
aims at protecting IoT edge devices directly attached to it.
The system applies to low-cost IoT gateways that assume edge
computing to detect vulnerabilities that are close to the data
sources. It identifies a large number of threats, such as port
scanning, HTTP and SSH brute force, and SYN flood, with
high accuracy and a low rate of false positives. Yao et al. [14]
proposed an edge-based anomaly detection system to detect
malicious attacks. A wrapper-based feature selection method
eliminates irrelevant and duplicate features, thus improving
detection capabilities, and four types of attackers are distinguished based on their capabilities. Ensemble learning is used
to increase levels of detection, and a lightweight algorithm is
also used to minimize latency by running it at the same time
on edge nodes.
B. Federated Learning
Several research studies [15], [16] used FL approaches
in the healthcare domain. Mothukuri et al. [4] implemented
an anomaly detection methodology that is based on FL and
proactively detects IoT-based intrusions by analyzing data
from each device. This system utilizes FL cycles with GRUs,
preserves local data, and exclusively transmits learned weights
to the global server. The results show that decentralized
solutions perform better in terms of accuracy and privacy

preservation. Alamleh et al. [17] standardize and benchmark
machine learning–based IDS in FL for healthcare applications
using a multicriteria decision-making framework. A decision
matrix is generated by utilizing a dataset of 125,973 records
and 41 attributes, and the fuzzy Delphi technique is employed
to standardize evaluation criteria. Borda voting is used to
assign weights and choose the best solution. Hallaji et al. [18]
investigate the various forms of dangers and adversaries that
decentralized FL encounters, as well as its security mechanisms, dependability, and authenticity. FL is recognized for
its privacy-preserving features, but sharing model updates can
compromise model performance and data privacy. Decentralized FL aims to minimize server-related risks by leveraging
blockchain technology; however, this raises additional privacy
concerns. Santos et al. [19] introduce an FL model for a reliable network-based IDS with high-confidence model updates
over time. Unsupervised classification reliability evaluation in
a federated architecture eliminates probable misclassifications
while lowering model update costs. Using the MAWIFlow
dataset, experiments reduced false-positive and false-negative
rates by up to 12% and 9.6%, respectively. They also improved
false-positive reduction and reduced update costs. Pedro et al.
[20] evaluate the efficiency of differential privacy approaches
in training an FL-based IDS for industrial IoT. The ToN IoT
dataset’s non-IID data is used to compare privacy levels and
aggregation methods, including FedAvg and Fed+. The results
show that Fed+ maintains similar precision even when noise
is introduced during federated training.
C. Split Federated Learning
Thapa et al. [21] introduce SFL, a hybrid of FL and Split
Learning (SL) that combines their advantages while mitigating their limitations. Relay-based client coordination slows
training in SL, but it provides better model privacy and suits
resource-constrained scenarios. However, SFL considerably
reduces computing time per global epoch for multiple clients
while retaining accuracy and communication efficiency. Data
privacy and model robustness are enhanced by differential
privacy and PixelDP. Experiments show that SFL scales adequately with client count and works well in privacy-preserving
scenarios [6]. Otoum et al. [22] suggest that the SL approach
is effective in achieving a higher detection rate, accuracy,
and classification performance for an IDS. The prospective
security concerns have been addressed by employing the
SL-based IDS for advanced transportation systems. The proposed model has been analyzed and compared to other models,
including FL and transfer learning solutions. Compared to
FL and transfer learning, the suggested SL achieves a 2–5%
improvement in accuracy and a 5% improvement in detection
rates, respectively. Turina et al. [23] propose SFL, a hybrid of
federated and SL that incorporates their privacy and efficiency
advantages. SFL has a superior accuracy-privacy tradeoff than
parallel SL, allows for concurrent processing, and minimizes
client processing requirements. Additionally, it maintains high
accuracy on imbalanced data. Pasquini et al. [24] examine the
security implications of SL, a collaborative machine learning
approach that aims for high performance and low resource

E. Adaptive Split Federated Learning Framework
SFL divides the neural network among devices of the
network and a central server, thus maintaining data privacy
and avoiding computational load on the local devices. The
federated learning hyperparameters are formally stated as
T= 50 communication rounds, where each round follows
E= 1 local training round, and a client participation rate
p = 1.0 to ensure full client participation. They have been
chosen strategically to provide a consistent and repeatable
basis of the evaluation of the core contributions. In addition
to the adaptive aggregation technique and split architecture
based on TCNs, the hyperparameters are based on FL concepts
designed to handle client drift. All local client updates are
performed using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of
0.001 and batch size 32. This arrangement generated consistent
and high-quality results across all three cybersecurity datasets
examined in our experiments.
