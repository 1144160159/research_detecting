NMFAD: Neighbor-Aware Mask-Filling Attributed
Network Anomaly Detection
Liang Xi , Runze Li, Menghan Li , Dehua Miao , Ruidong Wang , and Zygmunt J. Haas , Fellow, IEEE

Abstract—As a widely adopted protocol for anomaly detection
in attributed networks, reconstruction error prioritizes comprehensive feature extraction to detect anomalies over interrogating
the differential representation between normal and abnormal
nodes. Intuitively, in attributed networks, normal nodes and their
neighbors often exhibit similarities, whereas abnormal nodes
demonstrate behaviors distinct from their neighbors. Hence, normal nodes can be accurately represented through their neighbors
and effectively reconstructed. As opposed to normal nodes, abnormal nodes represented by their neighbors may be erroneously
reconstructed as normal, resulting in increased reconstruction
error. Leveraging from this observation, we propose a novel
anomaly detection protocol called Neighbor-aware Mask-Filling
Anomaly Detection (NMFAD) for attributed networks, aiming
to maximize the variability between original and reconstructed
features of abnormal nodes filled with information from their
neighbors. Specifically, we utilize random-mask on nodes and
integrate them into the backbone Graph Neural Networks
(GNNs) to map nodes into a latent space. Subsequently, we fill
the masked nodes with embeddings from their neighbors and
smooth the abnormal nodes closer to the distribution of normal
nodes. This optimization improves the likelihood of the decoder
to reconstructing abnormal nodes as normal, thereby maximizing
the reconstruction error of abnormal nodes. Experimental results
demonstrate that, compared to the existing models, NMFAD
exhibits superior performance.in attributed networks.
Index Terms—Anomaly detection, attributed network, graph
convolutional network, reconstruction, mask-filling, neighborhood association.

I. I NTRODUCTION

A

TTRIBUTED network refers to a network in which
the nodes or edges contain attribute information. It is
a typical type of graph data where nodes represent entities and edges represent relationships between entities [1],
[2], [3]. Anomaly detection in attributed networks aims to

identify abnormal nodes, which has been widely used in many
fields, such as financial fraud [4], intrusion detection, spam
detection [5], and others [6]. Unlike time series data, tabular
data, etc, anomaly detection in graph data needs to confront
more challenges due to the particularity of graph structure,
indicating the complex “many-to-many” relationship between
nodes, and usually contain more information.
The traditional attributed network anomaly detection methods [7], [8] utilize shallow mechanisms to capture the network
structural information and node attributes. However, those
methods are unable to be applied in the case of the increasing
size of the data. Recently, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)
[9], [10], [11], [12] have been proposed to handle the graph
data. There are mainly 3 categories of graph anomaly detection methods, one-class [13], [14], self-supervised [15], [16],
[17], [18], and reconstruction error [19], [20]. They all have
achieved promising results on attributed network anomaly
detection, but most of them have some limitations in the actual
attributed networks: (1) Most methods mine the attributed
network information by designing complex network structures for inter-node interaction learning [20], [21] rather than
directly designing appropriate anomaly detection protocols.
(2) A direct consequence is that, after sufficient representation learning, the anomalies might be also reconstructed
well, resulting in the failure of the anomaly score based on
reconstruction error [1].
Apparently, in attributed networks, normal nodes and their
neighbors tend to be similar, while abnormal nodes behave differently from their neighbors. That is, normal nodes and their
neighbors tend to have similar distribution states in terms of
structure and attributes. Therefore, we can assert that using the
neighbor information for filling of masked nodes could effectively make the reconstructed abnormal nodes inconsistent
with their original ones, while the reconstructed normal nodes
similar to their original ones, thus, maximizing the difference
between normal and abnormal nodes in reconstruction error,
which can be illustrated in Fig. 1. In light of this motivation,
we propose a novel Neighbor-aware Mask-Filling Attributed
Network Anomaly Detection method (NMFAD). Specifically,
we perform random mask of nodes and input them into the
encoder to extract the embeddings. Then, we fill the masked
nodes with neighbor embedding information and smooth the
distribution using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularization to make the masked abnormal nodes closer to the
distribution of normal nodes. Finally, we process the attributed
network anomaly detection tasks using reconstruction error.

The main contributions of this paper include the following:
1) We propose a novel anomaly detection protocol, by
maximizing the variability of abnormal node between its
original features and reconstructed features filled from
neighbors, due to the dissimilarity between abnormal
nodes and neighbors.
2) We design the strategy of mask-filling with distribution smoothing to draw the mask-filled abnormal nodes
closer to the distribution of normal nodes.
3) We conducted different experiments on four datasets
of two attributed network anomaly detection scenarios
to validate the advantages of NMFAD over baseline
methods.
II. R ELATED W ORK
A. Traditional Anomaly Detection
Presently, the majority of anomaly detection methods are
unsupervised and aim to identify anomalies when confronted
with a substantial amount of unlabeled data alongside limited
labeled anomaly data [22]. These methods can be categorized
into 3 groups: clustering, one-class, and reconstruction.
1) Clustering-based methods typically follow a two-step
approach [23], [24], involving dimensionality reduction
followed by anomaly detection through clustering of
normal samples.
2) In contrast, one-class-based methods [25], [26], [27],
[28] focus on learning the normal/abnormal discriminative boundary. One-Class SVM (OC-SVM) [25] seeks
to fit a hyperplane in feature space transformed nonlinearly to enclose normal data. Support Vector Data
Description (SVDD) learns a hypersphere around normal
data to enhance the robustness. Recently, deep learningbased methods that aim to capture the embeddings and
hyperspheres have been introduced. A typical approach
is Deep-SVDD [26].
3) Reconstruction-based methods operate under the
assumption that abnormal samples cannot be
effectively reconstructed. This category includes
PCA (Principal Component Analysis)-based [29] and
AE (AutoEncoder)-based methods [30] which leverage
the reconstruction error as an anomaly score.
While these methods have demonstrated success in traditional anomaly detection, they lack generalization ability for

graph data, where the topology structure among nodes plays
a vital role [13], [31].
B. Attributed Network Embeddings
Attributed networks are prevalent in the real world, and
various methods focus on learning the node features by
incorporating both topological structure and node attributes
[32] for downstream tasks such as anomaly detection. Existing
approaches mainly include 3 strategies: random walk [8],
matrix factorization, and deep learning.
1) Among the random-walk-based methods, SANE (Sparse
Attributed Network Embedding) [33] generates node
sequences through the random walk and aggregates
the information from neighbors with attention mechanism, to learn the low-dimensional features. TextAssociated DeepWalk (TADW) [34] integrates text
features to improve DeepWalk. Extending TADW,
HSCA (Homophily, Structure, and Content Augmented)
[35] introduces regularization of neighboring nodes to
capture network homogeneity.
2) Matrix-factorization-based methods, like AANE
(Accelerated Attributed Network Embedding) [36] and
BANE (Binarized Attributed Network Embedding)
[37], combine node attribute and edge information from
neighboring nodes to capture the association between
structure and attribute information.
3) Deep-learning-based methods [38], [39] aim to learn
node embeddings and show excellent performance. HNE
(Heterogeneous Network Embedding) [40] maps the
nodes and attributes into a shared latent space for graph
learning. CSAN (Co-embedding for Static Attributed
Networks) [41] adopts a joint learning framework with
Variational AutoEncoder (VAE) to extract embeddings.
While these methods have shown promising performance
in attributed network embeddings, they still focus on feature
extraction without a targeted design for graph anomaly detection tasks.
C. Anomaly Detection in Attributed Network
The advent of deep learning and its application to graph data
scenarios has revolutionized graph anomaly detection [42].
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), such as GCN, GAT (Graph
Attention Network), etc [9], [10], [11], [12], have performed
superior performance in various graph mining tasks recently.

GNNs can learn node embeddings in a graph, with both node

1) The one-class graph anomaly detection methods such
as OC-GNN [16] and Dual-SVDAE [12], combine the

a learned hypersphere.
2) The self-supervised methods such as GraphMAE [16]
and MGAE [43], utilize the prior knowledge or data
augmentation mechanisms to learn the graph information and capture the normal/abnormal difference by a
designed anomaly score.
3) Besides, the reconstruction-error-based methods such as
Dominant [19] and AnomalyDAE [20], assume that the
abnormal nodes have larger reconstruction errors compared with normal nodes after fully capturing the data
features. These methods mainly rely on the AE frameworks, which are simple and effective, but they pay more
attention to full feature extraction for anomaly detection
rather than discovering the normal/abnormal difference.
the graph data mining rather than the difference information between normal and abnormal capturing.
Therefore, we explore the normal/abnormal variability from
the perspective of neighbor perception in the attributed networks, consider the similarity of normal nodes and neighbors,
and the dissimilarity of abnormal nodes and their neighbors,
combine the advantage of reconstruction error for anomaly
detection, This is the core idea of our approach.

B. Comparative Models
We select some currently known deep learning-based methods to compare with NMFAD. Among them, OCSVM, DeepSVDD, and DeepWalk are classical one-class methods; GAE,
ARGA, Dominant, and ComGA are reconstruction-errorbased methods; OC-GNN and Dual-SVDAE are one-class
GNN methods; CCA-SSG, GraphMAE, and CoLA are selfsupervised methods.
1) OC-SVM (2001) [25]: It is a classical anomaly
detection algorithm with a hyperplane to detect the
anomalies.
2) Deep-SVDD (2018) [26]: It is a deep one-class anomaly
detection method with SVDD. It has two strategies,
Deep-SVDD (Attr) and Deep-SVDD (Stru), which
use the attribute and structure information for model
training, respectively.
3) DeepWalk (2014) [8]: It is a classical graph embedding method, using local information through truncated
random walks to extract the embeddings and employ
OC-SVM to process anomaly detection.
4) GAE (2020) [41]: It is a generative graph selfsupervised method, which could capture the high
non-linearity of topological structure and node attributes,
and perform anomaly detection with the reconstruction
error.
5) ARGA (Adversarially Regularized Graph Autoencoder,
2018) [38]: It is an adversarial graph embedding framework based on GAE to enforce the embeddings of
topological structure and node attributes to match a prior
distribution.
6) Dominant (2019) [19]: It combines GCN with deep
AutoEncoder to reconstruct the attributed network
through topological structure and node attributes, and
spot the anomalies.
7) OC-GNN (2021) [13]: It is a GNN-based one-class
graph anomaly detection framework, such as GCN
(called OC-GCN) or GraphSage (called OC-SAGE).
8) Dual-SVDAE (2022) [14]: It contains two AEs to fuse
the structure and attribute embeddings. The anomalous
nodes can be measured by the learned structure and
attribute hyperspheres, respectively.
9) CoLA (COntrastive Learning for Anomaly detection,
2022) [17]: It is a Contrastive self-supervised Learning
Anomaly detection framework that extracts the relations
between nodes and their neighboring substructures.
10) CCA-SSG (Canonical Correlation Analysis to SelfSupervised GNN, 2021) [15]: It is a self-supervised
graph embedding learning model, which uses canonical
correlation analysis to associate two view embeddings
through data augmentation. It also could discard the
augmentation-variant information and prevent degenerated solutions.
11) GraphMAE (2022) [16]: It is a GAE with a mask
strategy, focusing on feature reconstruction with SCE.

C. Model Settings and Performance Metrics
In NMFAD, the learning rate is set to 0.01, and the epoch
is set to 200. On each dataset, we set the dimension of
embeddings as 64. We use Adam Optimizer for training.
We adopt two performance metrics to evaluate the performance: AUC and AP. All the experimental results were
obtained by averaging the results with five different seeds.
D. Parameter Experiments
We first discuss the effects of different mask ratio, β, and set
the value of the scaling factor of SCE, γ, in the loss function.
The experimental results are shown in Fig. 4 and Fig. 5.
1) In Fig. 4, the experimental results gradually decrease
as β increases on each dataset, but the overall trend is
stable. This shows that even when β is set to a larger
value, we can lend the information filling of neighbors to
ensure the NMFAD’s ability to reconstruct the masked
normal samples. We set β to 0.3 on each dataset for the
subsequent experiments.
2) In Fig. 5, as γ increases, the detection performance
gradually stabilizes from a certain initial fluctuation on
Cora, Citeseer and BlogCatalog datasets. On Flicker
dataset, The detection performance smooths out rapidly
as γ increases. These results also could reflect that γ
needs to be set to a larger value to reduce the weights
of easier samples for enhance the detection performance.
Moreover, this is why we chose SCE instead of MSE
as the reconstruction error calculation. In the subsequent
experiments, referring to the results in stable phase, we

set γ to 14 on Cora, Flickr and Citeseer datasets, and
set γ to 12 on BlogCatalog dataset, respectively.

F. Ablation Experiment
We design two variants for the ablation experiment to
highlight the usefulness of NMFAD’s motivation:
1) wo-pd: It is NMFAD without mask-filling and distribution smoothing, which is the basic framework with mask
strategy.
2) wo-d: It is NMFAD without distribution smoothing to
verify the usefulness of mask-filling.
The results are shown in Table IV. We make the following
observations.
1) On each dataset, wo-pd is the weakest of the three
models, even though it uses the mask strategy to enhance
the model’s learning ability.
2) The superiority of mask-filling can be illustrated by the
fact that wo-d outperforms wo-pd on all datasets.
3) After adding the distribution smoothing to wo-d,
NMFAD performs the best results on all datasets, which
could verify that the combination of mask-filling and
distribution smoothing presents the largest improvement.
In summary, graph anomaly detection protocols by maximizing reconstruction error of anomalies through mask-adding
and smoothing the abnormal nodes closer to the distribution
of normal nodes could significantly improve the detection
performance of NMFAD in attributed networks.

I. Visualization Experiments
We conduct two visualization experiments, one is to show
the distribution of mask-filled anomalies; one is to summarize
the anomaly score distribution.

1) To verify the motivation of NMFAD that the reconstruction of the masked anomalous nodes after mask-filling
is more like normal rather than abnormal nodes, so that
the reconstruction error of the abnormal nodes is large
enough to be detected, we visualized the embeddings of
the test set for each dataset using the T-SNE tool [47].
The results are shown in Fig. 6 (The blue dots represent
normal nodes, and the orange dots represent mask-filled
abnormal nodes).
As can be seen, the mask-filled abnormal nodes belonging to the same distribution as the normal nodes in
the latent space, would be reconstructed more like the
normal nodes and calculate a larger anomaly score based
on reconstruction error. Therefore, these visualization
results could verify that the combination of mask-filling
and distribution smoothing can limit the reconstruction
of anomalies that are similar to normal, so as to be
detected.
Moreover, in Fig. 6, there are some structural anomalies,
such as outlier and cluster. These kinds of anomalous
nodes could also be smoothed to the normal distribution
after mask-filling, and hard to be reconstructed. Therefore, these visualization results also could indicate that
NMFAD can handle some structural anomaly detection
tasks.
2) Furthermore, we compare an anomaly score distribution of Dual-SVDAE, GraphMAE, ComGA, and
NMFAD, which are the methods that performed best
in the “Comparative experiment” overall. The results
are shown in Fig. 7. As can be seen, NMFAD obtains
better normal/abnormal anomaly score distribution than
the other methods on all datasets. All the results of
Fig. 6, Fig. 7, and Table II highlight that NMFAD can
effectively capture anomalies, using the reconstruction
error between their mask-filled reconstructed features
and original features based on smoothing the abnormal
nodes closer to the distribution of normal nodes.

VI. C ONCLUSION
Attributed network is a typical kind of graph data.
Most existing graph anomaly detection methods are mainly
based on feature extraction to solely enhance the models’

learning ability, without targeted design for anomaly detection
tasks, ignoring the strong/weak similarities between normal/
abnormal nodes and neighbors, making them incompetent to
capture anomalies in attributed networks. Therefore, we fully
consider the dramatically different effects of filling on normal/abnormal samples by neighbors’ information, and propose
a novel Neighbor-aware Masked Attributed Network Anomaly
Detection method (NMFAD), whose ideas of mask, filling,
and distribution smoothing could transform abnormal nodes
closer to normal ones and maximize the reconstruction error
of anomalies to capture all anomalies as much as possible.
The experimental results show that NMFAD outperforms the
baseline schemes on four datasets and highlight the effectiveness and advantages of this novel graph anomaly detection
approach.
However, we did not test NMFAD in large graph or dynamic
graph scenarios, which are the focus of future work. Moreover,
