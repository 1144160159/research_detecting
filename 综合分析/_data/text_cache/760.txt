                  operations but may also cause social and political instability and even threaten national
                  security. To address the ever-changing cyber threats, traditional defense methods, such
                  as firewalls and intrusion detection systems (IDSs), have demonstrated their limitations
                  and are unable to counter new and complex attack methods effectively.
                     As network attacks become increasingly diverse, from traditional DDoS attacks and
                  SQL injections to more covert advanced persistent threats (APTs), their attack patterns
                  and strategies are becoming more targeted, clandestine, and complex. Traditional net-
                  work defense methods primarily rely on rule-based detection and protection; however,
                  they often struggle to cope with unknown attacks and emerging attack techniques. To
                  address this problem, increasing research is exploring the use of artificial intelligence
                  (AI) technology, particularly machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), in network
                  security. These technologies can identify potential patterns of network attacks by auto-
                  mating the teaching and analysis of large datasets. They can even provide efficient, intel-
                  ligent early warning and protection against unknown attacks [1].
                     This work addresses these gaps by integrating multimodal fusion, ensemble learning,
                  and knowledge graph reasoning into a single model that simultaneously forecasts traffic
                  flows, system logs, and event alerts. The combination method addresses three persistent
                  challenges—class imbalance, semantic interpretability, and cross-dataset generalizabil-
                  ity—by integrating graph-augmented features with ensemble classifiers and imbalance-
                  aware training. Unlike current multimodal or graph-based techniques, the new approach
                  places a greater emphasis on cross-modal alignment and interpretable attack-path rea-
                  soning, thereby exhibiting higher detection rates and more explainable alarms for secu-
                  rity analysts.

                  1.1 Research gaps
                  Current network attack detection methods face several critical limitations. Traditional
                  rule-based and signature-based systems effectively detect known attacks but fail to iden-
                  tify zero-day exploits, advanced persistent threats (APTs), and rapidly evolving attack
                  variants. Machine learning and deep learning approaches improve detection of previ-
                  ously seen patterns but are often constrained by single-source data usage, high false-
                  positive rates, and limited adaptability in dynamic network environments. Moreover,
                  these AI-based models generally function as black boxes, providing little interpretability
                  for security analysts. The proposed framework overcomes these challenges by leverag-
                  ing multimodal fusion, ensemble learning, and knowledge-graph reasoning, enabling
                  precise detection of complex attacks, interpretable alerts, and seamless integration into
                  analyst workflows.

                  1.2 Novelty of the research
                  The proposed framework is progressive against the existing state-of-the-art literature
                  as it combines multimodal fusion and reasoning based on knowledge graphs. In con-
                  trast to single-modality models, our model is an effective learner of traffic, log, and event
                  data, learning to identify complementary spatial, temporal, and semantic patterns that
                  individual modalities are unable to capture. Ensemble learning boosts stability among
                  heterogeneous attributes, whereas knowledge graph embeddings implement associa-
                  tions among objects, assault routes, and occasion links, which offer explicit semantic
                  reasoning. This two-way operation makes it possible to achieve better detection rates,

                  2 Related work
                  Recent literature highlights the growing use of knowledge graphs and machine learning
                  for cybersecurity. Knowledge graph approaches model entities and their relationships
                  to capture semantic attack patterns, enabling interpretable intrusion detection. Machine
                  learning techniques, including ensemble methods and deep learning, have been applied
                  to traffic, log, and event data for automated threat detection. Several studies combine
                  these approaches to improve both detection accuracy and interpretability. These Works
                  demonstrate hybrid frameworks, while others explore semantic reasoning to detect low-
                  frequency or novel attacks. This review establishes the context for the proposed multi-
                  modal, ensemble, and knowledge graph–based framework.
                    Although traditional intrusion detection systems such as signature-based and anom-
                  aly-based systems have been effective with traditional attacks, they can easily deal with
                  low-frequency attacks or novel attacks and do not offer much interpretability. Recent
                  AI and machine learning models enhance detection accuracy but are mostly black-box
                  and, therefore, it is hard to comprehend how analysts have been influenced to raise an
                  alarm or follow an attack path. The above limitations underscore the requirement to
                  have frameworks that are robust and generalizable in detection with semantic interpret-
                  ability, which drives the fusion of multimodal fusion, ensemble learning, and knowledge
                  graph-based reasoning to the proposed system.
                    The proposed framework aligns with research in explainable AI (XAI) by provid-
                  ing interpretable attack paths through knowledge graph reasoning, enabling analysts
                  to understand and verify alerts. It also complements cyber threat intelligence (CTI)
                  approaches by linking events across logs, traffic, and known attack patterns to identify
                  emerging threats. By bridging these fields, the system not only improves detection accu-
                  racy but also enhances analyst trust and situational awareness, expanding its relevance
                  beyond traditional intrusion detection to broader cybersecurity intelligence and deci-
                  sion-support applications.
