1. Introduction

    The fourth industrial revolution, or Industry 4.0, represents the convergence of cyber-physical systems and advanced analytics
in manufacturing environments, driven by the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) [1]. This paradigm empowers smart factories to
generate voluminous and heterogeneous data streams that span system logs, sensor telemetry, and network activity. These data are
vital for operational eﬃciency and real-time threat detection. However, the interconnected nature of Operational Technology and
Information Technology systems creates complex interdependencies, where anomalies in one domain may signal or cause disruptions
in another. This calls for a uniﬁed multimodal security analytics framework that is capable of cross-domain inference.
    Conventional security analytics are insuﬃcient to address these challenges. Typically, independent models are deployed for each
data modality, resulting in siloed insights and a high operational overhead. Centralized analytics systems attempt to fuse data centrally
but face bandwidth bottlenecks, privacy regulations, and single points of failure. Consequently, there is a growing need for holistic
and decentralized solutions that preserve privacy while leveraging the full spectrum of heterogeneous factory data.

    FL has emerged as a promising paradigm that trains a global model collaboratively without aggregating raw data [2,3]. However,
deploying FL in real-world industrial environments poses several challenges. First, clients naturally possess statistically heterogeneous
(non-IID) data, which can cause instability and failure to converge in standard FL algorithms [4]. Second, the transmission of model
updates to a central server, even without raw data, creates privacy vulnerabilities against an “honest-but-curious” server [5]. Third,
early studies commonly assumed that all clients possessed homogeneous, single-modality data, which is an unrealistic assumption in
diverse IIoT environments [6].
    This limitation has motivated the development of multimodal federated learning (MMFL), in which clients can hold diﬀerent data
modalities. Surveys such as [6] and recent advances in MMFL [7–9] have explored architectural solutions for modality heterogeneity;
however, these methods are primarily evaluated on benign, non-security datasets (e.g., image-text benchmarks) and do not evaluate
the adversarial threat landscape of industrial environments.
    Federated models are susceptible to inference-time adversarial attacks, which can subvert anomaly detection and classiﬁcation
[10]. Although defenses such as adversarial training have been studied in homogeneous FL scenarios [11], their extension to multi-
modal environments is non-trivial. Each modality requires custom perturbation strategies, and uniﬁed robustness is not achieved by
naïve adaptation.
    The intersection of multimodality, decentralization, and adversarial robustness remains largely unaddressed, despite its signiﬁ-
cance in IIoT systems. Surveys and representative MMFL methods [6–9] primarily evaluate benign, non-security benchmarks and do
not address adversarial robustness or extreme modality and feature skew typical of smart factories. In parallel, FL security works
on inference-time attacks and adversarial training [10,11] and generally assumes a single modality, leaving modality-speciﬁc per-
turbation models and aggregation under modality skew unexamined. Prior FL work for IoT/ICS [12–16] also omits an end-to-end
adversarially robust multimodal design.
    To address this gap, we propose FedMamba, a framework that jointly addresses data heterogeneity, training stability, privacy,
and adversarial robustness in IIoT security. The framework uniﬁes system logs (HDFS), sensor time series (SWaT), and network
ﬂows (CICIoMT-2024) using a shared Mamba backbone. Its core is a robust federated protocol that leverages FedProx to promote
stable convergence on non-IID data and incorporates a secure-aggregation simulation to protect clients’ updates during aggregation.
Finally, it integrates a modality-speciﬁc adversarial training scheme to harden the ﬁnal model against inference-time attacks [6–11].
Our framework, to our knowledge, is among the ﬁrst to jointly address multimodal heterogeneity, privacy, and adversarial robustness
in IIoT.
    Our key contributions are:

1) Uniﬁed architecture for modality feature skew. We design a single multimodal model with explicit parameter partitioning
   𝜽 = 𝜽in ⊎ 𝜽back ⊎ 𝜽cls . Modality-speciﬁc adapters and heads isolate heterogeneity across logs, time series, and tabular ﬂows, while
   a shared Mamba backbone enables cross-modal transfer with minimal interference (Fig. 2 and Section 3.3).
2) Modality-aware block-wise aggregation compatible with secure aggregation. Under one-modality-per-client, we provide a

   This preserves clean accuracy on non-trained modalities and reduces client-side cost compared with training all modalities (Sec-

   FedMamba addresses heterogeneous IIoT environments with a shared Mamba backbone, modality-aware block-wise aggregation
that remains compatible with secure aggregation, FedProx-based optimization, and selective adversarial training.

ture, training protocol, secure aggregation compatibility, and selective adversarial training. Section 4 covers experiments, robustness
analyses, ablations, and scalability. Section 5 concludes, and Appendices A–D provide supporting details.


2. Related work

    Our research is situated at the conﬂuence of three critical domains: FL for IoT security, the emerging ﬁeld of MMFL, and adversarial
robustness in decentralized systems. We reviewed the state-of-the-art in each domain to precisely situate the contributions of our
FedMamba framework.
    FL is widely used as a privacy-preserving approach for security analytics in IIoT. A signiﬁcant body of work has focused on
applying FL to network intrusion detection in unimodal settings. Foundational frameworks such as DeepFed [12] demonstrate the
eﬃcacy of using federated CNN-GRU models to achieve high detection rates on network traﬃc data while preserving privacy. This
line of research has been extended through various architectural and algorithmic innovations in recent years. For instance, some
works have explored hybrid ensembles [13], federated transfer learning [15], and optimized hybrid models like CNN-LSTM [16] to
improve generalization and accuracy.

     Given the edge resource constraints, several studies have emphasized eﬃciency, including lightweight designs for edge deployment
[14]. Concurrently, a parallel stream of research has addressed the foundational challenges of the federated process. A primary issue
is statistical heterogeneity, where non-IID client data can cause model divergence ("client drift"), leading to unstable convergence.
Foundational algorithms, such as FedProx [4], were developed to counteract this by introducing a proximal regularization term.
Another critical challenge is the privacy of model updates, as a curious server can potentially infer client data. Cryptographic protocols,
such as secure aggregation, allow the server to aggregate only masked updates [5]. These solutions are typically evaluated in a
unimodal context.
     Despite these signiﬁcant advancements in architecture, eﬃciency, and FL protocol security, a common thread unites this body of
work: it operates under the assumption that all clients possess homogeneous, single-modality data. These frameworks, while powerful,
are predominantly designed for and evaluated on a single data type, such as network traﬃc [12,13,15–18] or sensor data [14].
However, they do not address the intrinsic heterogeneity of smart factories, where system logs, sensor telemetry, and network traﬃc
coexist.
     The challenge of learning from diverse data modalities across clients has given rise to multimodal federated learning (MMFL). A
survey by Che et al. [6] provides a foundational taxonomy of architectural challenges, including client-level modality heterogeneity.
Recent studies have proposed speciﬁc architectural solutions, such as enabling clients with partial modalities to collaborate [7], using
contrastive learning to align representations [9], or employing multimodal transformers [8]. Although architecturally innovative,
these methods are primarily validated on benign, non-industrial benchmarks (such as vision-language or medical datasets), with
little or no evaluation of IIoT security data. Furthermore, they generally do not incorporate adversarial threat models critical to
security applications, nor do they typically address the challenge of ensuring stable convergence under the dual pressures of modal
and statistical (non-IID) heterogeneity.
     Federated systems are vulnerable to adversarial attacks. Research in this area has explored the factors inﬂuencing robustness [10]
and proposed methods for propagating robustness between clients [11], typically in unimodal and homogeneous scenarios. From an
industrial perspective, Kim [19] provides an insightful analysis of adversarial attacks in centralized industrial control systems (ICS)
environments, while Ahmad et al. [20] propose a StuxNet-inspired adversarial learning framework for detecting advanced threats in
critical infrastructure. However, a signiﬁcant gap remains in the development of modality-aware adversarial defense strategies within
a federated context, which is a challenge central to our work.
     To unify advancements across these domains and address their limitations, we propose FedMamba, an MMFL framework ex-
plicitly designed for industrial IoT security. Unlike traditional FL for IoT, our approach is designed to handle client-level modality
heterogeneity. In contrast to prior MMFL work, we integrate adversarial robustness as a ﬁrst-class design objective and ensure the
privacy of updates via secure aggregation. Finally, our approach combines FedProx for stability under non-IID multimodal data with
modality-aware adversarial training, providing a practical end-to-end solution for heterogeneous smart factory environments.
