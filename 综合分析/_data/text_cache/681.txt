                OPEN              Federated learning-powered real-
                                  time behavioral intrusion detection
                                  leveraging LSTM, attention, GANs,
                                  and large language models
                                  Abdullah AlHayan & Jalal Al-Muhtadi
                                  A novel behavior-based intrusion detection framework, the Federated Intrusion Detection and
                                  Mitigation Framework (FIDMF), is proposed to address critical challenges in modern intrusion
                                  detection, including data privacy, scalability, severe class imbalance, and the critical need for semantic
                                  understanding and explainability. FIDMF integrates federated learning for privacy-preserving
                                  collaborative training with an Attention-Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) core for robust temporal
                                  pattern detection. To substantially enhance the framework’s intelligence and transparency, FIDMF
                                  innovatively pioneers a multi-faceted integration of open-source Large Language Models (LLMs)
                                  for: 1) Contextual Feature Enrichment from raw network logs; 2) Semantic Data Augmentation,
                                  guided by LLMs to inform Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for generating semantically
                                  coherent and novel attack patterns; and 3) Explainable AI (XAI) for human-readable explanations.
                                  This holistic approach, further supported by the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE)
                                  for class balancing, results in a robust, scalable, privacy-preserving, and highly explainable Intrusion
                                  Detection System (IDS). A rigorous evaluation across the NSL-KDD, CIC-IDS2017, and UNSW-NB15
                                  datasets demonstrates FIDMF’s superior performance. On NSL-KDD, the full FIDMF framework
                                  achieved an outstanding overall accuracy of 99.40% and an F1-score of 99.38%. Critically, for the
                                  challenging minority attack class, it attained an F1-score of 99.70%, significantly outperforming prior
                                  configurations without LLM guidance. Furthermore, FIDMF maintained high accuracy on CIC-IDS2017
                                  (99.65%) and UNSW-NB15 (98.05%), confirming its strong generalizability. An in-depth ablation study
                                  validates the crucial contribution of each component, particularly the semantic intelligence provided
                                  by the LLMs. FIDMF proves its potential as a superior, intelligent, and transparent solution for real-
                                  world intrusion detection scenarios.

                                  The increasing development in digital networks like IoT, MANET, and cloud networks has raised a concern for a
                                  reliable intrusion detection system to secure highly sensitive data. Conventional IDS systems are generally faced
                                  with challenges like less flexibility to adapt to emerging attack methods and centralized processing of data. One
                                  of the serious issues related to IDS technology is user privacy data. In a decentralized system, highly sensitive
                                  data should not be sent to centralized systems for processing. In conventional systems related to IDS technology,
                                  data privacy remains compromised since raw data needs to be continuously transmitted to centralized systems
                                  to access central system processing1–3.
                                      The imbalance between classes is another important challenge that hinders the performance of IDS models.
                                  In reality, attacks are rarely occurring events, thus normal network activities take up the majority of the data
                                  in such a scenario. Such a disparity can lead to a significant drop in traditional machine learning models’
                                  performance as measured by high false-negative rates, where the system fails to detect the attacks4. Although
                                  these models are highly efficient in recognizing complex patterns, there are instances wherein DL models could
                                  be vulnerable to problems related to class imbalance issues that are not effectively mitigated using techniques

                                       Moreover, the upsurge in the need for real-time detection in Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) has been
                                  caused by the necessity of quickly responding to threats that have just come up. The real-time detection is of
                                  immense importance. Even with all the progress made in deep learning, especially with LSTMs and GANs, there
                                  still lies a big gap that these models are not yet able to work with the high-level semantic context of the raw
                                  network logs (e.g., protocol, service, flag). This ’semantic gap’ limits the detection of sophisticated attack patterns
                                  and the delivery of human-comprehensible explanations. It is thus necessary to bridge this gap to get truly
                                  intelligent, adaptive, and trustworthy IDSs. Our project tackles this issue by employing the amazing language
                                  comprehension skills of Large Language Models (LLMs). The key contributions of this work are therefore
                                  summarized as follows: This study introduces a novel, end-to-end Federated Learning-powered Attention-
                                  LSTM framework (FIDMF) that ensures strict data privacy while maintaining exceptional temporal robustness,
                                  as shown in Table 1. Crucially, FIDMF pioneers a holistic and multi-faceted integration of open-source Large
                                  Language Models (LLMs) into a federated intrusion detection context, which represents a significant departure
                                  from existing approaches. Unlike prior works that might use LLMs for static log parsing or post-hoc report
                                  generation, FIDMF integrates LLMs directly into the core detection pipeline through three synergistic modules:
                                  (i) Contextual Feature Enrichment using DistilBERT to transform raw, categorical network features into rich,
                                  semantic embeddings, bridging the ’semantic gap’ that traditional encoders and even deep learning models often
                                  miss; (ii) Semantic Data Augmentation driven by GPT-2-guided Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to
                                  generate not just statistically diverse, but also semantically coherent and truly novel attack patterns, a unique
                                  capability for zero-day threat resilience; and (iii) Explainable AI (XAI) generation via GPT-25, enabling real-
                                  time, human-understandable explanations for detected anomalies. This deep and dynamic LLM integration for
                                  semantic intelligence and transparency within the detection process is a core novelty. In addition, an advanced
                                  class imbalance mitigation strategy is proposed, which combines SMOTE for interpolation with LLM-guided
                                  Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for synthetic pattern generation to substantially improving zero-day
                                  threat resilience. Finally, the proposed FIDMF framework achieves superior and generalizable performance,
                                  attaining a state-of-the-art F1-Score of 99.38% on the NSL-KDD dataset and demonstrating consistent
                                  improvements across CIC-IDS2017 and UNSW-NB15, surpassing centralized and other state-of-the-art deep
                                  learning baselines. The effectiveness of deep learning in modern applications, including specialized areas such as
                                  computer vision and robotics, further emphasizes its potential in developing highly accurate security solutions6–8.
                                       The proposed IDS model consists of state-of-the-art methods to tackle the issues of privacy, scalability,
                                  model accuracy, class imbalance, semantic understanding, and explainability. This leads to a concept known as
                                  Federated Learning. This is crucial for maintaining privacy and protecting sensitive data in intrusion detection
                                  systems. Federated Learning is a decentralized approach to machine learning that enables multiple parties to
                                  collaboratively train a model without sharing their data by keeping the data on the local devices and exchanging
                                  only model updates, which makes it suitable for applications where privacy-sensitive data should not leave the
                                  source.

                                  • Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks are a specialized version of recurrent neural networks (RNNs)
                                    used to model sequential data while emphasizing long-term dependencies. A key advantage in intrusion de-
                                    tection is that LSTMs are particularly well-suited for processing time-series data such as network traffic logs
                                    due to their ability to capture long-term dependencies9.
                                  • Attention Mechanisms help deep learning models increase their capabilities to focus on the more important
                                    parts of the input data. The use of attention mechanisms in an IDS allows the model to focus on the important
                                    features of network traffic, enhancing its interpretability and performance10.
                                  • Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) creates new examples based on the existing examples
                                    of a minority class by interpolation4. This helps the IDS to learn better decision borders and improves classi-
                                    fication performance.
                                  • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are used to counter class imbalance by learning data distributions
                                    to generate more diverse and complex attack patterns, moving beyond interpolation techniques like SMOTE.
                                  • Large Language Models (LLMs): We implement open-source LLMs within FIDMF to narrow the semantic
                                    gap and upgrade system intelligence.

                                  semantic context from network logs far beyond traditional categorical encoders, and a fine-tuned GPT-2-
