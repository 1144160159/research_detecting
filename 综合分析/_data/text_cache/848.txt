                  1 Introduction
                  Although the interests of vital communication and it is dependant services necessitated
                  an accelerated set of features for privacy and confidentiality in the fabric of modern net-
                  works, with encrypted communication protocols leaving state-of-the-art in their dust,
                  the very design parameters of the interest, the transmission of information and there-
                  fore observability information, which the dependent infrastructure on such technology
                  has had the unintended side effect of severely degrading traditional insight and anomaly
                  detection mechanisms used for security. Now that traffic is increasingly encapsulated,
                  traditional payload-inspection-based IDSs are becoming less effective, leaving many
                  blind spots in network defence. With the trend toward adopting Zero Trust Network
                  (ZTN) architectures, which do not automatically trust any user or device (inside or
                  outside the organisation) and always verify, there is increasing demand for fast anom-
                  aly-detection algorithms for encrypted traffic that can circumvent the limitations of
                  decryption overhead. While initial traffic classification and anomaly detection have uti-
                  lised conventional machine learning techniques, practical use has been constrained by
                  the inflexibility of such methods in the face of evolving threats and their opacity [1, 2].
                     To overcome these challenges, recent work has begun to explore explainable artifi-
                  cial intelligence (XAI) methods that balance detection accuracy and interpretability for
                  security analysts. Works such as Zebin et al. [12] and Singh et al. [49]. XAI methods
                  for anomaly detection based on encrypted service calls are up-and-coming [64]. Islam
                  et al. [4] focus solely on trust and provide examples of availability, integrity, and con-
                  fidentiality; their work reminds us of the broader context of trust that must be built in
                  secure systems, including interpretability. However, most methods developed to date
                  either focus on protocol, e.g., DNS-over-HTTPS, or on static ML models that do not
                  adapt over time. Furthermore, hardening against adversarial attacks or new-to-organisa-
                  tion encryption protocols — essential elements in any meaningful Zero Trust context —
                  has received scant attention. The framework is proposed to fill such a gap by providing
                  all three dimensions, i.e., high detection accuracy, interpretability, and robustness, for
                  encrypted traffic anomaly detection.
                     To mitigate this gap, we propose a completely novel explainable anomaly detec-
                  tion framework, called ZTXPlainaAI, based on Zero Trust Networks. At its heart is an
                  explainable hybrid deep learning model: Encode: EncXplainNet, that utilises a CNNs–
                  GRUs architecture for modelling local features and temporal dependencies, and an
                  attention mechanism for interpretable decision making. Finally, SHAP-based feature
                  attribution provides post hoc explanations with both intrinsic and global interpretations.
                  Together, these design choices enable the model to achieve state-of-the-art performance
                  in anomaly detection while also providing explainability that helps security analysts eas-
                  ily apply Zero Trust principles.
                     Task: To devise an explainable AI model which can classify if the traffic is encrypted
                  or not, detect anomalies in it on the encrypted traffic based on industrial-oriented, non-
                  whimsical constraints (i.e. without inspecting payload) and on real-world observations
                  that IoT-MQTT communication generates encrypted traffic. The framework targets
                  the main objectives: (i) high classification accuracy and robustness against a variety of
                  encrypted protocols, (ii) human-understandable and interpretable rationale on the pre-
                  diction of an anomaly dedicated to human analysts, and (iii) adaptability to changing
                  network conditions using reinforcement and feedback loop mechanisms.

                     There are multiple novelties of this work. The hybrid architecture, constructed by
                  incorporating CNNs, GRUs, and attention mechanisms in EncXplainNet, uniquely
                  suits encrypted traffic flows by learning to set spatial and temporal contexts. Second,
                  our explainability pipeline enables interpretability beyond feature attribution alone by
                  introducing dual-layer explainability, providing real-time actionable transparency (using
                  SHAP and attention). Note that we also highlight the importance of robustness by
                  stress-testing under noise injection, adversarial perturbations in unseen protocols, input
                  noise, and many other unseen perturbations, demonstrating significant robustness not
                  reported in previous work. Finally, the analyst-guided feedback loop allows for adaptive
                  refinement of automated detections and builds trust through human-in-the-loop analy-
                  sis in Zero Trust environments.
                     A summary of this research contribution is as follows. It first presents ZTXPlainaAI,
                  an end-to-end explainable framework for detecting anomalies in encrypted traffic. Sec-
                  ondly, it presents EncXplainNet: a high-accuracy yet explainable hybrid deep learning
                  model that integrates CNNs, GRUs, attention, and SHAP explanations within a single
                  framework, achieving high accuracy while maintaining a good level of interpretability.
                  Third, it offers a comprehensive comparison with classical machine learning and deep
                  learning baselines and demonstrates superior performance in accuracy (0.96), F1-score
                  (0.96), and AUC (0.98). Fourth, it addresses limitations such as ablation studies, robust-
                  ness testing, and comparisons with state-of-the-art methods to demonstrate reliability
                  and generalizability. Fifth, the approach is designed to be practical and focused on Zero
                  Trust, ensuring it can be used in real-world settings where such concepts are familiar.
                     The rest of this paper is organised as follows: In Sect. 2, we position our work in the
                  context of related work, reviewing previous approaches to the analysis of encrypted
                  traffic, anomaly detection, and explainable AI. Preliminaries are provided in Sect. 3,
                  including the problem formulation (Sect. 3.1), background work on deep learning for
                  encrypted traffic identification (Sect. 3.2), foundations of XAI (Sect. 3.3), Zero Trust
                  principles (Sect. 3.4), and notations (Sect. 3.5). Section 4: Proposes the methodology
                  contributing to the dataset, system overview, EncXplainNet model architecture, inte-
                  gration of explainability in attending embedding, reinforcement loop, training setup,
                  evaluation strategy and the threat model. Experimental results are presented in Sect. 5,
                  including dataset preparation, baselines, evaluation metrics, performance comparison,
                  ablation studies, explainability outputs, robustness analysis, and benchmarking against
                  state-of-the-art methods. Results are summarised in Sect. 6, which emphasises the prin-
                  cipal outcomes and trade-offs between detection power, interpretability, and execution
                  cost. Finally, Sect. 7 concludes the paper by outlining the work’s contributions and future
                  directions, including validation on a larger dataset, a usability study targeted at analysts,
                  lightweight deployment optimisation, and defence against adversarial approaches.
                     Moving beyond the state of the art, this research contributes to the performance,
                  interpretability, and robustness of encrypted traffic anomaly detection in a Zero Trust
                  paradigm in a modular fashion, opening avenues for practical, explainable cybersecurity
                  solutions.
